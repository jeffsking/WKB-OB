# SAP Grade Selection for Baby Diaper Absorbent Core

Comparative Analysis of Three Presentations — Final Recommendation, Selection Logic, and Cross-Document Synthesis

## 1. Executive Summary

Recommended final SAP architecture: a 3-grade layered structure — a separate skin-side layer of LG LK804ND plus a poly-side blend of Nuoer KC-511 (fast-swell) × Nuoer NR-950 (delayed-swell) — at a total loading of 13.5 g/diaper and a cost of US¢1.90/diaper. This is the scheme from PPT 3 (5-Zone Core SAP REL Simulation), the most complete, executable, and cost-optimized of the three, and should be advanced as the primary route into Stage 1 validation.

Bottom line: vs. a premium single-SAP baseline, this design delivers −40% SAP cost, −59% cumulative rewet, −49% late strike-through, and +29% design capacity — using 10% less SAP mass. The core mechanism is staggered swell kinetics that defeat gel blocking across repeated insults.

## 2. Final SAP Selection Recommendation

SAP2:SAP3 blend ratio = 36:64 (leaning toward the delayed reservoir to match a 4-insult curve); SAP1 sits as a distinct separate layer and is not blended.

## 2.2 Backup / dual-sourcing

SAP3 dual-source: primary NR-950; backup BASF 9635 (CRC 38, higher cost) — qualify both to de-risk Nuoer OTIF.

## SAP1 alternative: Satellite CR740 (regional cost option).

## 2.3 Key structural increment

A REL five-fiber TAB layer (24 gsm) is sandwiched between the skin SAP and the blend, providing the capillary gradient and one-way z-flow that PPT 2 achieved instead via a high-GBP grade inside the blend.

## 3. Main Selection Logic (5 threads)

## 3.1 Skin-side ≠ storage-side — divide and conquer

The skin side only needs "fast + dry + safe" (speed / rewet / skin-contact safety dominate; maximum CRC is not the goal). Capacity and cost are loaded onto the poly side. This is the shared foundation of all three decks.

## 3.2 Defeat gel blocking via staggered swell (timing)

The core logic is not spatial layering but temporal offset: SAP2 swells fast and absorbs insults 1–2, while SAP3 (51 s vortex) stays dormant until insults 3–4. The two never seal the gel bed simultaneously, so total usable capacity rises. As PPT 1 states explicitly: the fast:slow ratio — not position — is the real design lever.

## 3.3 Single free-swell numbers lie — validate on multi-insult sequences

Both strategy decks stress that two very different formulations can show identical single-dose free-swell values; only sequential 4-insult (gush) testing exposes whether the stagger actually works. This makes bench sequence testing a hard gate, not a nice-to-have.

## 3.4 Cost lever = Asia-sourced high-CRC / high-AUL grades

SAP2/SAP3 use domestic Nuoer (~$1,300–1,350/MT) to hold cost down; SAP1 uses a globally supplied LG because skin-safety limits (residual monomer, extractables) are strictest on the directly contacted layer. Price sensitivity: shifting SAP3 to Nippon Shokubai CAW adds +10% cost; shifting SAP1 to BASF adds +6%. China-sourced balanced grades for SAP2/SAP3 are the primary cost lever.

## 3.5 Zone-match add-on to the wetness pattern

Within the 5-zone layout, Z2 (target wetness) is heaviest (424 gsm) and Z4 (rear storage) next; Z1/Z5 carry only skin-side leak barriers. The boy-pattern forward-biased wet zone is optimized; a girl-pattern (center-biased) requires a Z2/Z3 SKU variant.

## 6. Consultant's Judgment & Key Risks

6.1 The two simulations give different grade answers — do not pretend they agree.

PPT 2's skin-side N511 has GBP of only 26; PPT 3's LK804ND has GBP 53. Against PPT 1's rule that the skin layer must preserve porosity at low load, LK804ND is clearly superior — PPT 3's skin-side choice is better justified.

6.2 Biggest vulnerability in PPT 3: SAP3 (NR-950) has GBP of just 8 and a 51 s vortex — it is essentially non-permeable on its own.

Its channeling function depends entirely on the REL layer being activated (TAB Zone 2 must hit 138–143 °C, force-cooled <40 °C). If the REL activation window drifts, PPT 2's approach — using Satellite 710 (GBP 75) directly inside the blend — is more robust. Recommendation: keep PPT 2's N8+710 architecture as a fallback option for cases where REL is unstable or unavailable.

6.3 "62% utilization" is a conservative margin, not a defect — but watch the narrative.

PPT 3 shows lower utilization than the single-SAP baselines (80% / 84%) yet achieves it with less SAP and +29–36% capacity — this is headroom reserved for worst-case loading. If production data shows a more concentrated wet zone, Z4's 47% utilization is low enough to trim loading and cut cost further.

6.4 Everything rests on one assumption: a real, reproducible swell-rate gap between KC-511 and NR-950.

PPT 3's risk register already flags High (particle segregation) and Med (delay mistuned to infant 30 min–3 h intervals). The Stage 1 bench kinetics screen (30 s → 3 h) is a hard gate before freezing the spec — without it, do not proceed to pilot.

## 7. Conclusion

Primary route: PPT 3's LK804ND / KC-511 / NR-950 three-grade scheme (separate skin layer + 36:64 staggered poly-side blend + REL layer) — US¢1.90/diaper, −40% vs. premium single SAP, −59–68% rewet. Proceed only after the Stage 1 kinetics bench validation passes, and retain PPT 2's N8+710 architecture as the fallback if REL cannot be reliably activated at scale.
