# Principles for Lower Rewet

Flowback

There is enough capacity to fully lock up fluid.

There is enough capacity to lock up the fluid.

Capacity is accessible to the fluid.

Superabsorbent captures and retains fluid.

Any fluid in the core that isn’t locked up is separated from the skin/blotter.

SAM locks up fluid in a timely manner.

Product design promotes flow away from the skin.

SAM holds fluid, even under pressure 
and deswelling is minimized.

Intake layers create physical separation 
between the wet core and blotter.

Desorption

Intake materials do not trap fluid but encourage drainage to the core.

Materials have large pores to reduce capillary pulling/trapping of fluid.

Material resists compression of pores to keep pores as large as possible in-use.

The number of pores is minimized to provide less places for fluid to be trapped.

Multiple components work as a system to deliver fluid to the core.

Pore networks are well connected, and 
no fluid is trapped at interfaces.

A capillary gradient draws fluid away from skin
 and towards where it can be locked up.

Material and process variability and capability are considered in design.

For each design principle, the variability and capability of the materials and process should be 
considered during development to ensure that a reliable commercial execution is delivered.
