Hello!

My name is Kerry Clemmer and I develop intake systems as part of the Absorbent Development Team in the NA R&D ICT.

With many brands and segments interested in rewet, we have developed a set of rewet design principles that I’d like to make you aware of today.

First, it’s important to understand what rewet is. Rewet is fluid that is transferred to a blotter media under pressure.

Rewet is a useful bench method to understand loosely held fluid in an absorbent system. It is not a measure of skin dryness or perceived wetness.

The rewet design principles are useful to communicate and understand rewet and its potential causes. Note that these principles deal with urine only though rewet is possible for other fluids.

The rewet principles are divided into the two general mechanisms:

Desorption comes from the intake system where small pockets of fluid are trapped and can be expressed during rewet.

Flowback comes from the core where fluid isn’t fully locked up.

Both the product and test method design can influence which regime causes rewet in a particular case.

In the case of flowback, the first four principles deal with the absorbent core design – both capacity and SAM. The rewet principles are written from the perspective of reducing rewet or that loosely held fluid, and so changes like increasing capacity or ability to capture and hold fluid can reduce rewet.

The last two principles have to do with the intake system which can serve as a buffer or separation between the core and the rewet blotter media.

In the case of desorption, the first three principles have to do with capillarity. That’s the mechanism by which those small pockets of fluid can get trapped, so reducing the capillarity of pores within the material (or the number of pores themselves) can reduce rewet.

The next two address how the multiple components in an intake system work together to hand off fluid.

And finally the last acknowledges the variability that can happen in day-to-day production, which is included in all design principles.

I know this was a brief overview, but hopefully it’s given you some exposure to what the rewet design principles are so you can start referencing them.

The first draft of the rewet principles (and all other absorbent design principles) are available on my team’s SharePoint.

A cross-functional team is working on updates through October, including test method connections.

If you have any questions about rewet, contact myself for NA products and Ingrid Hoeger for global.
