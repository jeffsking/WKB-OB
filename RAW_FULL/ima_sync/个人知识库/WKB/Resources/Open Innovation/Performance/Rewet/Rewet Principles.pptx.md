# Background

## Rewet is the mass of fluid that transfers into a blotter media from a product over a given time and under a specified applied pressure.

$\bullet$ Changes in rewet levels are currently part of the success criteria of many projects

$\bullet$ The concept of “flowback” and “desorption” to communicate rewet mechanisms has existed for many years

$\bullet$ There is an opportunity to communicate mechanisms at the next level down

$\bullet$ A set of "principles for lower rewet" will help discussions with project teams as they work to change rewet

# Flowback

$\bullet$ Easier to understand of the two mechanisms: it's simply fluid that isn't locked up, so can flow back to the blotter/skin.

* Often concentrated in the core, as that's where fluid settles (via gravity).

[图片]
$\bullet$ Two main categories of causes & solutions:

* No Available Fluid: Trap fluid in SAM that could otherwise be available to flow back out of the core to the blotter.

* Separation from Blotter: Creating a buffer between the core from the blotter can keep any unlocked fluid from flowing back out under pressure.

# Desorption

$\bullet$ Fluid that gets trapped in the intake layers (against gravity) and can be expressed back to blotter/skin under pressure.

$\bullet$ Intake materials have empty space that essentially create a series of pores

* Pores allow fluid to penetrate into the intake system, then to drain down to the core

* Spaces between materials can also have the same effect.

[图片: Fibers Pores]
# Desorption

$\bullet$ Capillary action (capillarity) is when fluid moves through pores without or even against gravity.

* The smaller a pore is, the greater this effect can be.

* This is partially driven by surface tension, which wettability treatments modify.

$\bullet$ Drawing fluid may be a desirable trait to drain fluid from upper layers into lower layers.

$\bullet$ Larger diameter surge fibers are a common low rewet solution because they target multiple causes of rewet

$\bullet$ Note: Since smaller fibers are perceived as softer, softness and rewet are often opposites
