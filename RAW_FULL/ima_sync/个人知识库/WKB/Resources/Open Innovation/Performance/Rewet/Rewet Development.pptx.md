# Definition

$\bullet$ Rewet is the measure of product surface dryness.

* This is often used to imply or even claim skin dryness to consumers, though this is not accurate (next slide).

$\bullet$ Many of the product design levers that affect rewet also affect other absorbent system characteristics, but rewet is not correlated to leakage.

* Other absorbency tests such as intake, runoff, etc. are more suited to understanding leakage.

* Similarly, absorbent tests such as intake or retention capacity do not necessarily give insight into rewet.

# Consumer Relevancy

$\bullet$ Skin dryness is a critical factor in skin health and irritation.

$\bullet$ Rewet is one of many factors that contribute to skin moisture, so has poor correlation to skin moisture.

* Inner cover breathability is the primary driver.

$\bullet$ EWL remains the recommended method to substantiate skin dryness.

# History

$\bullet$ KC historically used TEWL as the measure of product moisture.

$\bullet$ In 2014, P&G made aggressive claims around rewet, beginning the current era of using rewet for claims and consumer communication.

* KC challenged the claim and asserted TEWL was the better method, but the NAD ruled the claim around rewet could stand.

$\bullet$ In this new era, BCC and AFC developed their own test methods to measure rewet with consumer-relevant parameters.

* Depend would later also add their own method based on the Poise method, but with curvature to better reflect their product use conditions.
