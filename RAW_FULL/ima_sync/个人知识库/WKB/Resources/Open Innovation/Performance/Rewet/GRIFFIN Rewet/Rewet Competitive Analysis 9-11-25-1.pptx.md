# Summary

$\bullet$ Pore size distribution of the liners – ALWAYS DISCREET and DEPEND liners have about the same mode pore size and AD liners have a narrower distribution of pores.

$\bullet$ Pore size distribution of the surge materials – ALWAYS DISCREET surge has about the same mode pore size as DEPEND surge, but the pore size distribution is narrower.

$\bullet$ Capillary pressure of the surge materials – ALWAYS DISCREET surge has a higher capillary pressure than DEPEND surge, but it has lower desorption energy due to lower basis weight.

$\bullet$ Bond strength of the Liner/Surge laminates – ALWAYS DISCREET liner/surge laminates with both adhesive patterns show higher peak loads versus DEPEND liner/surge laminates in peel testing and smoother peeling.

# Recommendations – what we learned from the competitive analysis

$\bullet$ Reduce the wettability of the DEPEND liner. This will lower the desorption energy leading to a drier liner and less rewet.

$\bullet$ P&G nonwovens have higher monodispersity of the pore size distribution. Could these lead to better pore connectivity in the surge and less rewet?

$\bullet$ Reduce DEPEND surge basis weight while maintaining intake speeds to retain less fluid in the surge.

$\bullet$ Reduce liner/surge interfacial gap by alternate means. The current bonding approach has room for improvement to reduce rewet further. Also note that pressure bonding the liner and surge improves rewet at the cost of reduced void volume and permeability.
