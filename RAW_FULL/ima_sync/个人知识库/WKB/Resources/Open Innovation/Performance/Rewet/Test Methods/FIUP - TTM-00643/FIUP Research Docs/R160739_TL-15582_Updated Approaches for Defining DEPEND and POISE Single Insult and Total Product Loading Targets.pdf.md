ABSTRACT

As of 2014, DEPEND and POISE lacked key fundamental research to inform

absorbent design and screening methods.  Decade-old single insult data

informed void volume targets and insult sizes for screening methods.  Most

wetting pattern data were also over 10 years old or never measured. Eliminating  these fundamental research gaps can reduce the risk of ineffective absorbent

design, optimize cost, and improve the effectiveness of screening methods.

New approaches defined in 2013-2014 generate new single insult loading data  without the need for incremental use studies and discretionary spending.  These  approaches informed updates to design targets and screening methods to be

implemented in early 2015.

INTRODUCTION

In 2012, DEPEND absorbent designers discovered that key design targets  used for absorbent design and screening protocol inputs were based on

data that was almost 10 years old.  Similar gaps existed in POISE.

Historical data observed that single insult loadings change at least every 5  years, so the use of outdated data creates potential risk of underdesigning

or overdesigning absorbent designs as well as improper inputs for

screening methods.  Individuals from the AFC Materials Team, FEI team,

and DEPEND 4SPS Leakage Team (2012-2013) collaborated to resolve

this gap and other fundamental research gaps.

Two challenges delayed the generation of new single insult data in

DEPEND until 2013-2014.  Study recruiting challenges prevented the

placement of new large-scale DEPEND use studies until 2013.  Afterwards, a new single insult study was placed on DPU2 (Study 35493), but this

study and a subsequent HUT resulted in lower loadings than in prior

studies.  The low loadings raised questions on recruit demographics, which  lead to different recruiting practices in DEPEND Leakage 4SPS HUT

studies later in 2013.

Later in 2013, the DEPEND Leakage 4SPS HUTs experimented with a

new HUT question, asking subjects to report the number of insults they

experienced with each product used if detectable.  These studies

generated a high sample size database for single insult data.  This

success led to a new approach to define and maintain single insult targets  without the need for incremental study spending.

Other screening protocol inputs were reviewed across 2013-2014.

Amongst these were the total product loading assumptions and number of  insult assumptions, whose rationale were not formally documented in

recent years.

Attached are point-of-views for new approaches to define single insult  loading targets, total loading assumptions, and number of insults

assumptions for absorbent design and screening methods.

RECOMMENDATIONS

The attached point-of-views are recommended for current and future

DEPEND and POISE absorbent designs and screening methods.  Single  insult loadings, total loadings, number of insults, and wetting pattern

should be updated every 5 years to ensure relevancy of design targets and  screening methods.  Ideally, this should be reviewed following new Urine  Loss Spectrum studies.

More detailed recommendations can be found in the attached point-of-view  documents.
