## Equipment

A number of pressure pads are available in the Clinical Research Center. The pad used for these studies was a medium size "Disper Tape Mat" available from Novel Gmb. The sensors are compressible capacitors with a threshold of 0.2 N/sq. and a resolution of 0.1 N/sq. and a equation rate is greater than 1 data point per second. The medium size mat has dimensions of 22x5 cm with 4 rows of 14 sensors each 1 cm square. The sensors are enclosed in a protective shield. The wires to connect to the computer for data acquisition are on the side of the pad and there is a "blank" row in the middle of the pad for the wires. The pad dimensions for the three available sizes are shown in Figure 10.

The pressure pad is first calibrated in the range of pressures expected during the testing. This is done by placing the pad in a flat press and compressing to a known pressure on the pad.

Commercial Size 2 Pullups Training pants (TP93) were used for this study. The pressure pad was placed inside the training part with the top of the pad 1 inch from the top of the waist and going down into the croctek region. The pad was placed between the body side liner and the absorbent core.

# APPENDIX A: BRIEF SUMMARIES OF PAST PRESSURE STUDIES

Feminine Care Pad Pressure Study (TR-896, August 1990)

This feminine care study used Novelle point sensors located on the surface of the pad and along the sides of the maxi pad code. There were six subjects who wore three different codes each and moved through four different positions: standing, squatting, sitting, and sitting with crossed legs. The highest average pressure recorded was 1.96psi for the cross-legged position. Peak pressures exceeded 6 psi.

Measurement of Static Pressures in Training Pants Using a Multi-sensor Pressure Sensitive Pad (TL-1999, October 1994).

This training pants study used Novel GmBh sensors to evaluate pressure in many different positions: standing, sitting, prone, supine, and lying on side. Each of the positions besides standing was done on both a hard and a soft surface. A sensor similar to the size of the absorbent was placed inside the product. This was a small study (three subjects) and some of the readings could have come from bending of the sensor instead of pressure from the subject.

ASPEN Pulp/Synthetic Fiber Intake Evaluation CRC D88668.07 (TL-6351, December 2000)

This diaper study used a Tekscan mat under the subject and product. The focus of this study was not pressure readings – pressure was only recorded to be related back to absorbent performance. Pressure readings were taken in a seated position only. Average contact pressure was 0.8-1.3psi. Average peak pressure was 2.0-2.8psi. It was also noted that only 1.5-6 inches of the absorbent were in contact with the mat.

Pilot Utilization of the Novel Pressure System - The DEPEND Pull-On Pant Pressure Pilot Study (TL-10149, March 2004)

This adult care study used Novel pressure sensors to collect pressure readings of four subjects in a variety of stationary positions and while moving. It was determined that the Novel system performs better than the Tekscan system when the sensors are not completely flat. Much of the collected data was below the sensitivity of the sensors so there was no data reproducibility and only minimal evaluation.
