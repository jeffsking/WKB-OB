# Desorption

$\bullet$ Fluid gets caught up in intake system materials and doesn't make it down the core to get locked up but can be released under the pressure of a blot.

$\bullet$ Think of nonwovens as matrixes of fibers with pores in between.

* Pores need to interconnect, or it can create "dead ends" where fluid gets trapped.

* Pores act like capillary tubes which can draw and hold fluid (more on next slide).

[图片: Desorption Fluid trapped within the intake system Intake materials discourage pulling or trapping fluid and encourage draining. Capillarity Pore network connectivity]
# Desorption

$\bullet$ Pore size is main driver of capillary action

* Fiber size: larger fibers = larger pores

* Resilient materials: resistant to compressing pores during use/testing

* Less fibrous material:

* Lower basis weight = less pores

* Film materials = almost no pores by design

$\bullet$ Wettability is the other knob: less wettable = less able to "climb"
