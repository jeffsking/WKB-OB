# What is Adhesive?

Hot Melt Adhesive: An adhesive that is in solid state at room temperature but is applied in its liquid state.

Types of Hot Melt Adhesive:

$\bullet$ Construction: Bonds materials together at lowest possible costs. Use mostly in lamination processes.

* Ex: Lamination adhesive, surge to liner, etc

$\bullet$ Elastic: Bonds stretchy materials

* Ex: Leg elastics, waistband, etc

$\bullet$ Garment: Sticky at room temperature and is used to secure products to clothing.

* Ex: Peel strip

$\bullet$ Our adhesive comes in drums and bricks

# Adhesive Overview

## Why Do We Use a Hot-Melt Adhesive?

* Readily bonds dissimilar materials

* Accommodate sensitive substrates

* Wide range of bond capability

* Can form temporary or permanent bonds

* Low cost for the amounts used

* Hidden bonding

* Variety of methods for product and process design

* No solvents or high toxicity (as in an epoxy)

* No dry time (as with a water or solvent borne adhesive)

# Safety Risks – Temperature, Pressure, Electrical, and Vapor

## Temperature

$\bullet$ The entire adhesive system is at an elevated temperature.

$\bullet$ Most adhesive equipment is between 300 – 350 F (150 – 175 C).

* Equivalent to a frying pan on high, a heated oven, or a car exhaust pipe.

### Pressure

$\bullet$ The entire adhesive system is under pressure

* Adhesive pressure range from 50 to 800 PSI

* Pneumatic pressures range from 0.5 to 80 psi

## Electrical

$\bullet$ The entire system is powered by 240 to 480 Volts

### Vapor

$\bullet$ Heated adhesives have strong vapors.

$\bullet$ If you need to open a melter lid, turn your head away to let vapors pass before looking into tank.

# Temperature Control

$\bullet$ Flow rates and operating pressures are affected by the temperature dependent viscosities of hotmelt materials.

$\bullet$ Pumps can be damaged when the adhesive is not up to temperature.

$\bullet$ Overheating can lead to charring, which can clog the applicator.

$\bullet$ Possible fire or other damage in the case of a runaway zone.

$\bullet$ Adhesive pattern control is dependent on temperature due to viscosity.

# Slot Coat and Spray
## Advantages and Disadvantages

### Slot Coat

+ High Intermittent Speed

+ Edge Control

+ Pattern Flexibility (Comb)

+ Thin, Uniform Adhesive

+ Less Burn-through

- Set Up Consistency

- Heat of the Nozzle to Web

- Wear of the Nozzle

- Globing due to Cutoff Edge

- Globing due to Substrate

- Surface Energy

- High MOCON Levels

- Low Melt Temps. Of Ingredients

### Spray

+ Can see Spray Pattern

+ None Contact with Web

+ Can Spray on top of Components

- Slow Intermittent Speed

- Poor Edge Control

- Build Up on Roller and Machine

- Burn-through where Filaments Cross

- Drooling when Machine is Down

- Globing when Machine is Running
