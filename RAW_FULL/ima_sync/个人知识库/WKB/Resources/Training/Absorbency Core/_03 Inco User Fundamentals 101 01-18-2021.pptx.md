#   Single Insults

$\bullet$ What are single insults?

* The fluid amount when a user experiences a single gush (user-defined)

$\bullet$ What is this data used for?

1. Informs absorbent design criteria

* How much fluid for each insult does our absorbent system need to absorb?

* Example: Minimum Void Volume > Single Insult Amount

* Urine is not compressible, so the product needs to hold the entire fluid volume

2. Provides consumer relevant parameters for modeling and test methods

* Design screening

* Claims

#   Wetting Patterns

$\bullet$ What are wetting patterns?

* Location of the fluid in the product after a single insult

$\bullet$ What is the data used for?

* Defines the target zone of the product

* Most critical location for the absorbent materials (intake, core, etc.)

* Example: Intake/core must cover the wetting pattern area

$\bullet$ Informs target zone and potentially insult location for modeling and test methods

* Wrong target zone or insult location $\rightarrow$ different results

$\bullet$ Note: Wetting pattern data on fully-loaded products do not define the target zone, but could inform absorbent utilization

#   Total Product Loading Assumptions

$\bullet$ Purchase intent:

* Inco consumers purchase products to protect their worst-case scenarios

* Goal:

* Ensure products can absorb at least the 95th percentile of total product loads

$\bullet$ Method Options:

1. Urine Loss Spectrum Studies (Expensive, but real conditions)

2. Targeted consumer loading studies for new products (Example: Thin pads)

3. HUTs


* Example:

* # of insults for design screening intake tests should load at least to the 95th percentile of total product loads

* (Total load)/(Single insult) = # of loads

#   Flow Rates

$\bullet$ No information is available on actual urge and stress incontinent flow rates

$\bullet$ Flow rates in KC tests are based on ~30 year old data from flowmetry-like tests

* Incontinent adults, but continent situations

* Full/nearly full bladder

$\bullet$ Past research measured slower flow rates for incontinent adults vs. healthy adults and children

* `<10 mL/sec` was a sign of incontinence via flowmetry tests

* Peak flow rate of 8mL/sec

* Mean flow rate of 6mL/sec (+/-3.3)

* Flow rates from 6-12mL/sec

* Flow rates decreased with age and urinary dysfunction

* Healthy adults ~18mL/sec

* Variation within occurrence and between occurrences

#   Do We Really Know Our Users?

* 2013...No

* 90-100% of absorbent design criteria was based on 10+ years old data or simply assumed

$\bullet$ 2014-2018...Getting There

* Updated most loading targets, including Thin pads

* Updated most target zones, except Thin pads

* Did not investigate flow rates

$\bullet$ 2021...???

* Overdue for single insult updates

* Changes in product offerings => Who are our users?

* Biggest Discoveries

* Single insults dropped 30-55% in DPU2 and Poise Maximum

* Male test method parameters did not reflect reality

* Thinner pads ≠ lower loads
