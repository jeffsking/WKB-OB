#   Making a Superabsorbent: Step 1 – Neutralization

$\bullet$ The monomer is the core backbone chemical of the superabsorbent

$\bullet$ It is reacted with a base (NaOH) to remove an acidic hydrogen ion and replace it with a Na$^{+}$ ion

$\bullet$ The most common commercial ion is sodium but other ions such as potassium and ammonium can be used

$\bullet$ This ion exchange will increase the superabsorbent capacity

* Higher degree of neutralization = higher capacity

* Neutralization also affects the pH of the superabsorbent, important for skin contact/safety.

* K-C uses superabsorbents with ~70% degree of neutralization

#   Making a Superabsorbent: Step 2 – Polymerization

Neutralized monomers are reacted using a free radical initiator triggered by heat, light, and/or chemical catalysts to form polymer chains. These monomers have carbon-carbon double bonds C=C and ionic groups H₂C=CH, such as a carboxylic acid group.

#   Making a Superabsorbent: Step 3 – Crosslinking

Crosslinkers are reacted with the polymer to tie the polymer chains together into a non-dissolving gel. Crosslinkers can be “reactive” and connect the chains during the polymerization, or they can be “latent” and connect the chains once they have already formed.
