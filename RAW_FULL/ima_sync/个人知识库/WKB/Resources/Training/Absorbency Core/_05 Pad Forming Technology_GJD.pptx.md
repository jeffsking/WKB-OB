#   Objective of Forming Technologies

The goal of forming technologies is to achieve a uniformly mixed absorbent core of fluff and SAM which

$\bullet$ Has high pad integrity

$\bullet$ Has little SAM damage

$\bullet$ Has correct basis weight (including zoned pads)

$\bullet$ Has low weight variability (CoV)

* Is free of clumps and hard spots

#   Pulp Fiberization

Pulp fiberization can be achieved by the use of a Fiberizer or a Hammermill. Fiberizer is the recommended standard, but both can be seen throughout facilities in North America and KCl.

(1) Gram of Pulp has Approximately (4) Million Fibers

(2) Each gram of pulp at 1200ppm is 160 lbs/hour

(3) Pulp roll is 1 ton

* Pulp Fiberization Rates

* 4,000 lbs/hr PCMC and Williams Hammermills

* 3,000 lbs/hr JOA Fiberizer (Rotor Mill)

* 2,200 lbs/hr Kamas Defiberizer

* 500 lbs/hr JOA Disk Mill

#   Pulp Fiberization

What are some common issues?

$\bullet$ Over fiberization – very short fibers

- Rotor turning too fast

- Anvil gap too tight

$\bullet$ Under fiberization – knits or clumps of pulp

- Rotor turning backwards

- Rotor turning too slow

- Anvil gap too wide

- Worn hammer or rotor tips

* Pad discoloration at low feed rate (<265gpm)

#   SAM Transport

* SAM is transported from the SAM bag to the metering hoppers in dense phase by either:

- Positive air pressure (mini pusher system)

- Vacuum (recommended standard for safety as leaks go into the piping and not into the bay)

* SAM is gravimetric metered (weight in loss) into the forming chamber to mix with fluff to create the absorbent core by either:

- Pneumatic conveyance (PD blower and eductor)

- Gravity drop (fed directly into gravity SAM chute)

#   Chamber Design

$\bullet$ Chamber design is an integral piece to maximize fluff and SAM mixing and to forming the desired pad.

$\bullet$ Design considerations include air flow, air pressure, raw material weight, machine speed, plant altitude and ambient humidity.

$\bullet$ Several different chamber designs have been created and are in use throughout the world.

$\bullet$ Turbulence in the chamber promotes SAM mixing but also introduces weight variability.

#   Chamber Design

Older versions used fluff as base material in models (at the time cores had little if any SAM)

Modeling analysis:

$\bullet$ Red = High air velocity

$\bullet$ Blue = Low air velocity

$\bullet$ Changes of air direction can exist in turbulent areas which can cause material to clump instead of mix

$\bullet$ Goal: even flow throughout chamber with high enough velocity to promote and maintain mixing.
