#   Non-Newtonian vs. Newtonian Fluids

#   NON-NEWTONIAN FLUID

Viscosity changes with

* shear rate

* time

* concentration

* temperature

Complex behavior – more difficult to predict or model how the fluid will behave

BM, Menses, Paint, wet sand, polymer melts

#   NEWTONIAN FLUID

#   Viscosity

* varies with temperature, but constant at a given temperature

* independent of shear rate and time

Relatively simple behavior – easier to predict or model how the fluid will behave

Urine, water, air, syrup, honey

#   Classes of Non-Newtonian Fluids

Fluids can belong to multiple categories

#   § Classification based on Shear Rate dependence

$\bullet$ Shear Thinning (Pseudo plastic) Menses, BM, paint, shampoo, slurries...

$\bullet$ Shear Thickening (Dilatant) Wet sand, concentrated starch suspensions...

$\bullet$ Bingham Plastic Nasal fluid, hand cream, tomato paste, toothpaste, Mayonnaise...

#   $\bullet$ Classification based on Time dependence

* Thixotropic (decrease in viscosity over time) BM

$\bullet$ Rheopectic (increase in viscosity over time)

$\bullet$ Time independent properties

#   $\bullet$ Classification based on Elasticity (micro/structure)

$\bullet$ Purely Viscous Non-Newtonian Fluids

$\bullet$ Visco-elastic Non-Newtonian Fluids Menses

#   How to describe menses fluid behavior?

Three different behaviors:

Plasticity

Elasticity

Viscosity

Both of these
models
ignore the
elasticity of
the fluid

Bingham model: fluid is plastic (has a yield stress) and viscous, but the viscosity is Newtonian

Power-law model: fluid's viscosity is non-Newtonian $\rightarrow$ shear-thinning but no plastic behavior

Non-linear visco-elastic model (like Oldroyd-4): displays elastic behavior of the fluid and non-Newtonian, but doesn't show the plasticity

Most generic descriptor is to have all three: a visco-elasto-plastic fluid
