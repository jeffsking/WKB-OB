#   SAM Chemistry and Manufacturing

#   Definitions of superabsorbent material

$\bullet$ Non-technical: Material that can take in and hold an unusually large amount of liquid (usually water).

$\bullet$ Technical: Crosslinked, long-chain, hydrophilic and hydrogen bond-capable ionic polymers (that absorb and retain large quantities of water).

$\bullet$ At KC we talk about "SAM" (superabsorbent material)

* Outside of KC, it is more common to say "SAP" (superabsorbent polymer)

$\bullet$ You may hear about "SAF" (superabsorbent fiber). This is chemically similar, but in fiber form rather than particulate.

#   Superabsorbent Base Chemistry

![H₂C=CH\nO^C\nNa^+\nacrylic acid salt\n(aka acrylate)\n](1/parseimages/figure64afc34c13e44ba5ab3dac7c82ec934a_0_1.png)
Monomers have a C=C double bond and ionic group, such as COOH.

SAM is usually petroleum-based, but various bio-based alternatives are being developed.

#   Synthetic

$\circ$ Petroleum based

* Polyacrylic acid, polyacrylamide

* Maleic anhydride copolymer

* Polyvinyl sulfonic acid

* Polyvinyl phosphonic acid

#   Bio-based

$\bullet$ Polysaccharide-based

* Carboxymethylcellulose (CMC)

* Modified starch

* Alginate, chitosan

* Poly(amino acid)-based

* Polyglutamic acid

by far the most common commercial SAM chemistry

#   Critical Characteristics of SAM:

$\bullet$ Long-chain polymer network (black) with bound ions (yellow) and mobile counterions (green).

* Counterions contribute to osmotic pressure, the driving force for absorption

* Crosslinked network (red)

* A particle is a single molecule (neglecting extractives).

* Without crosslinks, the particle would simply dissolve.

* Hydrophilic and hydrogen bond-capable

* Polymer chains must be able to interact with water

#   Why does SAM swell? 1. Osmotic pressure

Osmotic pressure: Solute (largely mobile ions) concentration is greater within SAM particle than in external solution. This drives water into the SAM by osmosis.

$\bullet$ Mobile ions cannot flow out of particle, because charge balance must be maintained.

$\bullet$ As water flows into SAM, volume of external solution is decreased. $\rightarrow$ Local solute concentration and charge concentration in external solution is increased. This is the “Donnan Exclusion” effect.

$\bullet$ Osmotic pressure depends on osmolality (moles of solute per kg water), not ionic strength.

$$\Pi_{\text{tonic}}=RT\left(c_{i}-c_{i}^{*}\right)$$

$$ \Pi_{\text{tonic}} = \text{Osmotic pressure} $$

$c_i$ = Conc. of mobile ions inside SAM

$c_i^*$ = Conc. of mobile ions in external solution

$R$ = Ideal gas constant

$T$ = Absolute temperature

#   Influence of salinity in external solution

$\bullet$ Higher solute $\rightarrow$ lower osmotic pressure $\rightarrow$ lower absorbent capacity

$\bullet$ Osmolality of 0.9% saline = Osmolality of blood plasma = 287mOsm [=mmol solute] /kg

$\bullet$ Mean osmolality of urine is about the same as osmolality of blood plasma

* But urine is much more variable due to hydration and other health states

$\bullet$ We often use 0.9% saline as a urine simulant for absorbency tests.

#   Why does SAM swell? 2. Electrostatic repulsion

Once SAM particle is hydrated, negative bound ions in nearby polymer chains repel each other, contributing to the expansive force.

$\bullet$ secondary to osmotic pressure driving force

$\bullet$ Higher charge ($Ca^{2+}, Al^{3+}$) $\rightarrow$ more charge screening $\rightarrow$ lower absorbency

(Multivalent cations can also reduce absorbency by ionic crosslinking of polymer chains)

$\bullet$ Larger ions (K+) $\rightarrow$ lower charge screening (can't approach as closely) $\rightarrow$ greater absorbency

$\bullet$ Smaller ions (Li+) $\rightarrow$ higher charge screening $\rightarrow$ lower absorbency

$\bullet$ At higher saturation most of the water is in the mobile state, and hydration effects are less important for further swell.

#   What makes a good base monomer/polymer for absorption?

$\bullet$ High charge per unit mass

* PAA > polysaccharides or peptides > proteins

$\bullet$ High molecular weight

$\bullet$ Long linear chains, no branching

* Unmodified starch is poor choice due to branched amylopectin

$\bullet$ Flexible chains for network expansion

* Cellulose chains are relatively stiff

#   SAM manufacturing. Step 1: (Partial) Neutralization

$\bullet$ Monomer reacts with NaOH to remove acidic hydrogen and replace it with Na⁺ ion

Other ions such as potassium and ammonium* can be used - less common.

Higher degree of neutralization = higher capacity

$\bullet$ ~70% neutralization is typical

![H_{2}C=CH\nO C OH + NaOH → H_{2}C=CH\nO C Na+\n](1/parseimages/figure4b8436174ed342d1b43a6592c15c8884_0_5.png)
*Some processes have polymerization first, then neutralization (e.g. SDP, Nuoer, Satellite CR7 series). Said to be more expensive (batch vs continuous process), but to give lower extractables.

#   SAM manufacturing. Step 2A- Polymerization

Partially neutralized monomers are reacted using a free radical initiator triggered by heat, light, and/or chemical catalysts to form polymer chains.

#   SAM manufacturing. Step 2B – Internal Crosslinking

Crosslinkers can be “reactive” (acting during polymerization, most common) or “latent” (acting after polymerization is complete). Crosslinks can be covalent (most common), ionic, or hydrogen bonds. Crosslinking turns polyacrylate into a non-dissolvable gel.

$\bullet$ "Extractables" are non-crosslinked polymer chains in SAM. Lower is better. Some vendors (e.g. Nuoer) claim that lower SAM extractables improves skin dryness and microclimate.

#   Reaction temperature

SAM polymerization processes can also be classified by reaction temperature

High temperature
(80-100+°C)

$\bullet$ Faster reaction, higher throughput, lower cost

$\bullet$ Lower internal crosslink density, lower AUL.

$\bullet$ Typically for high capacity SAM or industrial SAM.

Low temperature
(10-60°C)

$\bullet$ Slower reaction, better control, longer dwell time, lower throughput

$\bullet$ Higher internal crosslink density, higher AUL and gel strength

$\bullet$ Good for balanced SAM for hygiene applications

#   What chemistries must or may be included

Always needed: Water (40-70% by mass), (Partially neutralized) monomer(s), initiator(s), crosslinker(s)
Not always needed:

$\bullet$ AA Stabilizer [Commonly hydroquinone monomethyl ether (MEHQ)]

* To prevent acrylic acid dimerization as it ages. May not be required if AA is produced on site and piped directly to SAP plant.

$\bullet$ Foaming agents, foam stabilizers, or defoaming agents

* Introduce (or prevent) voids and high surface area. Foaming can be desired for faster absorption.

$\bullet$ Optical brighteners

$\bullet$ Color stability additives (antioxidants and chelators)

$\bullet$ Hydrophilicity / hydrophobicity modifiers

$\bullet$ Chain transfer agents

* Control chain length. Shorter chains $\rightarrow$ better AUL, lower process viscosity. Longer chains $\rightarrow$ higher CRC

$\bullet$ Plasticizers

$\bullet$ Rheology modifiers

$\bullet$ Antimicrobial or odor control agents

## - Polymerization

The synthesis of a superabsorbent polymer proceeds via radical chain-polymerization of acrylic acid, sodium acrylate, and at least one crosslinker.

Monomer preparation:

1. Partial neutralization of acrylic acid with NaOH

2. Addition of crosslinkers

3. Removal of oxygen

4. Addition of initiators

Radical chain-polymerizations proceed in three steps:

1. Polymerization start:

Initiation

2. Chain growth:

Propagation

3. Chain termination:

Termination

#   Absorbency Under Load – STM 00220

AUL is the amount of saline, in g/g, absorbed by SAM under a constant pressure.

* 0.16g SAM in cup with mesh bottom, and a weight on top. Measure total mass before and after exposing cup bottom to saline for 60 mins.

$\bullet$ "gel AUL": vacuum applied to remove free interstitial fluid. "AUL": no vacuum.

* 0.9psi typical, but 0.01, 0.3, and 0.6 psi are also used.

#   Relevant phenomena:

* SAM swell against pressure (e.g. baby wets diaper while sitting)

* Gel strength, stability, surface characteristics

* Liquid wicking through gel bed

#   Gel Bed Permeability – STM 00229

GBP is how well a swollen gel bed allows saline to flow through it.

Free swell GBP = no load.

0.3psi GBP = 0.3psi load maintained throughout the test.

$\bullet$ Permeability (K) is calculated from flow rate (Q) through bed of swollen SAM via

$$\text{Darcy's Law:} \quad K=\frac{Q\mu t}{(\Delta P A\rho)}$$

$$[1\text{ Darcy}=0.987\text{um}^2]$$

$\bullet$ Important for high SAM% cores, multi-insult use

#   Vortex Test for Absorption Speed – STM 00428

Vortex time is the time required for 2g SAM in 50mL saline in a stirred beaker to become a gel, meaning the vortex is gone. Measured in seconds, with stopwatch.

$\bullet$ Some vendors object that the endpoint is not obviously related to consumer use, but we find vortex time useful as it is strongly correlated with 1st cradle intake time in a high SAM% core.

#   Relevant phenomena:

$\bullet$ Absorption, swell kinetics

$\bullet$ Surface area, wettability

$\bullet$ Particle-particle attraction and/or friction when wet

#   Approaches to improve vortex time

$\bullet$ Increase surface area and/or porosity of SAM particle

* Foaming in belt reactor – creates surface and internal voids

* Sodium bicarbonate to create CO₂ in acid-base reaction with acrylic acid. (Stockhausen, LG)

* Shearing wet polymer – creates surface topography, pores that open up as particle swells

* Wet grinding after polymerization – typical for post-neutralization processes (SDP, Nuoer)

* Extruder - high shear plus rapid pressure change creates surface roughness (BASF)

* Phase inversion – partial swelling of SAM particles, then replacing solvent with non-solvent liquid to create nano-pores (K-C patent, not practiced)

* Make macro-particles from agglomerated smaller particles

$\bullet$ Reduce particle size – greater surface area to volume ratio

$\bullet$ Reduce crosslink density in core -lower resistance to expansion

$\bullet$ Hydrophilic surface treatments

#   Determination of Dust Potential – STM 00471

$\bullet$ Known as the “Dustview” test

$\bullet$ 30g of SAM fall from a funnel into a chamber. Dust remaining in the air within the chamber attenuates a laser beam

* 100 = full attenuation

* Measurements are taken over 30 seconds.

$\bullet$ Dustview (unitless) = max attenuation + attenuation at 30s

#   Color

$\bullet$ White SAM is perceived as high quality.

$\bullet$ Impurities and side reactions contribute to non-white color

$\bullet$ Color is measured by a colorimeter.

We use the "L*a*b" scale

L: brightness (0 = black, 100 = white)

a: red-green (red: >0, green: <0)

b: blue-yellow (yellow: >0, blue: <0)

Virgin SAM should have L>90.

#   Odor

#   Odor Control

SAM should not have any offensive odors. Historically, there have been some SAM grades that released a chemical smell when swollen, though it is not presently an issue. Some vendors (e.g. BASF) have tight quality controls to ensure no malodor.

SAM can adsorb some odors due to high surface area, and neutralize some odors due to low pH (e.g. ammonia). Some K-C products make odor control (OC) claims based on these built-in SAM properties.

Vendors can add additional functional chemistries to the SAM particles to control other odors, such as urine odors. This will enable stronger OC claims.

#   SAM needs by core type: FemCare absorbents

SAM consumption is low compared with urine absorbent products. In a facility that makes products for both applications, it often makes sense to choose a SAM grade for the urine-absorbing products and use that grade also for menses, rather than selecting a menses-specific SAM. If there is a need to select a menses-specific SAM, look for capacity and low cost.

#   Principles for SAM selection in menses-absorbing cores

$\bullet$ High CRC in blood (TTM-00019) to achieve retention capacity at minimal cost.

* SAM CRC in blood is about 2/3 the CRC in saline due to fouling.

$\bullet$ Vortex time and GBP not important due to low SAM%.

$\bullet$ Importance of AUL is unclear. Given low SAM%, probably not important.

#   Example targets

CRC (saline) > 30g/g

CRC (blood) > 20g/g

#   SAM needs by core type: SAM/fluff, <40% SAM

As long as there is enough void volume in the fluff for the expected insult size, the fluff will do the work of quickly intaking fluid. Fluff will also provide space between SAM particles to maintain permeability. The main job of SAM is to lock up fluid after the insult to improve skin dryness and create void volume for subsequent insults. Look for capacity and low cost.

#   Principles for SAM selection in low SAM% cores

$\bullet$ Use high CRC SAM (CRC > 35g/g) and/or low-cost SAM to achieve retention capacity target at minimal cost.

$\bullet$ Vortex time, AUL, and GBP not important.

Example targets

CRC > 35g/g

#   SAM needs by core type: SAM/fluff, 40-55% SAM

Fluff will provide fast first intake, providing sufficient void volume. Fluff will provide some space between SAM particles for permeability, so long as the swollen particles are not too soft to retain some of their shape (AUL).

#   Principles for SAM selection in moderate SAM% cores

$\bullet$ Use moderate to high CRC SAM (CRC > 33g/g) for cost

$\bullet$ Moderate AUL is important

$\bullet$ Vortex time and GBP may have some impact, especially toward upper end of SAM% range

#   SAM market notes

$\bullet$ Recent market entrants like Nuoer and Satellite (both out of China) are rapidly gaining in capability and capacity.

$\bullet$ This put extreme cost pressure on other SAM vendors around the world.

* Sanyo (parent company of SDP) exited the SAM market in 2024. SDP was one of the earlier innovators in SAM, with high performance but relatively high cost in 2024. Nuoer makes SAM with a similar production technology at a much lower price, and took much of SDP's previous volume.

* Evonik superabsorber was carved out from Evonik due to low margins and was sold in 2023 to ICIG (Germany). Renamed Stockhausen (the return of an older name).

$\bullet$ The rapid capability growth of Nuoer and Satellite is largely in the mode of "fast follow." It remains to be seen whether they can become market leaders in new innovation.

$\bullet$ The ability of KCNA to take advantage of low cost imports is hampered by an unstable tariff situation. If and when tariffs are cut, KCNA would still be wise to moderate imports in order to maintain a stable domestic supply base from Stockhausen and BASF.
