# Chapter 4 — Thermally Responsive Polymers with Amphiphilic Grafts: Smart Polymers by Macromonomer Technique

Source: Laukkanen, A. & Tenhu, H. in Smart Polymers: Applications in Biotechnology and Biomedicine, 2nd Ed. (Galaev & Mattiasson, CRC Press, 2008), pp. 115–132.
Deep-read covers source lines 1–1071 (chapter body, figures captions, and reference list start).

## Module 1: Core Arguments

Thermoresponsive water-soluble polymers undergo a sharp, temperature-triggered solubility collapse near body temperature. Several nonionic polymers (PNIPAM, PVCL, PVME) show temperature-dependent solubility driven by a delicate hydrophilic/hydrophobic balance; even small temperature changes trigger chain contraction and phase separation at ≈32 °C, which is exploited to build "smart" structures such as responsive hydrogels and thermally switchable filters. (lines 34–80)

PVCL is preferred over PNIPAM for biomedical use because of lower toxicity and hydrolytic stability. Unlike thermosensitive poly(N-alkylacrylamides), PVCL does not release low-molecular-weight amines on hydrolysis, and cytotoxicity studies found it well tolerated both below and above the phase-transition temperature. (lines 58–62, 111–114)

Amphiphilic PEO–alkyl macromonomers act as reactive surfactants that covalently bind to particle surfaces, giving superior steric stabilization. Compared with conventional surfactants (held by physical forces, prone to desorption/migration under shear or freeze–thaw), covalently bound macromonomers prevent aggregation at high temperature and high ionic strength, and PEO grafts reduce protein adsorption. (lines 197–239, 639–686)

The macromonomer technique yields three distinct architectures with tunable properties. Emulsion polymerization gives monodisperse grafted PVCL microgels; microemulsion polymerization gives ultra-high-MW graft copolymers (>1 million g/mol) suited to industrial scale; solution copolymerization gives well-defined grafts (0–34 wt%) whose reactive group at the hydrophobic end favors intramolecular association. (lines 369–442, 478–523, 703–730)

Above the LCST, amphiphilically grafted PVCL forms colloidally stable mesoglobules that solubilize hydrophobic guests and can be permanently fixed by phenol cross-linking. Heating triggers aggregation into spherical nanoparticles (≈50–120 nm, ρ≈0.40 g/cm³) that remain stable for days; phenols complex PVCL via hydrogen bonds, physically cross-linking the mesoglobule so it survives cooling. (lines 846–957)

## Module 2: Key Concepts with Operational Definitions

### 1. Lower Critical Solution Temperature (LCST) / 最低临界共溶温度

Definition (EN-primary): The temperature at which a polymer's solubility in water drops sharply, producing phase separation. It is set by the hydrophilic/hydrophobic balance along the chain and can be shifted higher or lower by increasing the fraction of hydrophobic or hydrophilic groups, respectively.
Original excerpt: "The balance between the hydrophilic and hydrophobic groups along the polymer chain determines the lower critical solution temperature (LCST), and therefore it is easily shifted to higher or lower temperatures by increasing the fraction of hydrophilic or hydrophobic groups, respectively." (lines 116–119)

### 2. Amphiphilic macromonomer / 两亲性大分子单体

Definition (EN-primary): A polymerizable nonionic surfactant of molecular weight 10³–10⁴ whose hydrophilic part is poly(ethylene oxide) and whose hydrophobicity comes from an alkyl chain; it is used to replace conventional surfactants in heterogeneous polymerization and becomes covalently attached to the product.
Original excerpt: "Some nonionic reactive surfactants are also called amphiphilic macromonomers, since the molecular weights are in the range of 10³ to 10⁴. Typically, the hydrophilic part is composed of poly(ethylene oxide) and the hydrophobicity is achieved by an alkyl chain." (lines 233–236)

### 3. Reactive surfactant (surfmer) / 反应型表面活性剂

Definition (EN-primary): A surfactant molecule that also bears a polymerizable group, so that it becomes covalently bound to the latex/particle surface and serves as either an electrostatic or steric stabilizer, eliminating the adsorption/desorption equilibrium of conventional surfactants.
Original excerpt: "The stabilization mechanism is based on covalently bound surfactant molecules that serve as either electrostatic or steric stabilizers on the latex, depending on the nature of the polymerizable surfactant." (lines 200–203)

### 4. Critical Micellization Concentration (CMC) / 临界胶束浓度

Definition (EN-primary): The threshold macromonomer concentration above which self-assembled micelles appear. Here it was determined independently by light scattering, pyrene fluorescence probe, and isothermal titration calorimetry, and serves as the process window for emulsion polymerization.
Original excerpt: "the critical micellization concentration (CMC) has been determined by light scattering, by fluorescent probe experiments, and with isothermal titration calorimetry measurement." (lines 258–260)

### 5. Steric stabilization / 位阻稳定化

Definition (EN-primary): Stabilization of colloidal particles by grafting highly water-soluble chains (e.g., PEO) onto their surface, which prevents aggregation even at elevated temperature and high ionic strength and additionally reduces protein adsorption, thereby increasing biocompatibility.
Original excerpt: "The stability of aqueous polymer dispersions can be enhanced by steric stabilization. Steric stabilization can be achieved by grafting the particle surface with, e.g., PEO or with some other highly water-soluble polymer." (lines 144–146)

### 6. Mesoglobule / 介观球（中间态聚集体）

Definition (EN-primary): A colloidally stable aggregate formed by several thousand collapsed polymer chains upon heating above the LCST — distinct from a single-chain globule. It is partially hydrated, roughly spherical (Rg/Rh ≈ 0.72–0.75), and can solubilize hydrophobic guests.
Original excerpt: "These aggregates are called mesoglobules to separate the concept of collapsed aggregate particles from single-chain globules." (lines 859–860)

### 7. Cloud point / 浊点

Definition (EN-primary): The temperature at which an aqueous polymer solution becomes turbid due to aggregation/phase separation; in this study the measured cloud-point temperature was nearly identical to the onset temperature (Tonset) of the endothermic transition.
Original excerpt: "the measured cloud-point temperatures were nearly identical to the Tonset values." (lines 813–814)

### 8. Self-association: intra- vs interpolymeric / 自缔合（分子内/分子间）

Definition (EN-primary): Below the LCST, amphiphilic grafts associate in water. At high grafting density the chain adopts a stable intrapolymeric (single-chain micelle) structure; at low grafting density a bimodal population appears — single chains (Rh 5–30 nm) plus interpolymeric associates (Rh 80–150 nm) whose abundance grows with concentration.
Original excerpt: "the amount of amphiphilic grafts is high enough to force the polymer chain to adopt a stable intrapolymeric structure." (lines 751–752)

### 9. Overlap concentration (C*) / 重叠浓度

Definition (EN-primary): The polymer concentration above which individual chains begin to overlap; above C* strong interpolymeric association is expected to dominate over single-chain behavior.
Original excerpt: "with the polymer concentration higher than the overlap concentration C*." (lines 497–498)

### 10. Physical (hydrogen-bond) cross-linking by phenols / 酚类物理交联

Definition (EN-primary): Phenols complex with PVCL through hydrogen bonds between the lactam-ring carbonyl and the phenol hydroxyl, building a physical network that can permanently fix the mesoglobule structure so the particles retain integrity upon cooling.
Original excerpt: "Phenols are known to complex with PVCL via hydrogen bond formation between the carbonyl groups of the lactam ring and the hydroxyl units of the phenol." (lines 922–924)

### 11. Pyrene fluorescence probe (I1/I3 ratio) / 芘荧光探针（I1/I3 比值）

Definition (EN-primary): A hydrophobic probe whose emission fine-structure ratio I1/I3 reports the micropolarity of its microenvironment; it drops from ≈1.60 in water to ≈0.60 in a hydrocarbon medium as pyrene enters micellar cores, and is used to locate the micellization/association onset.
Original excerpt: "The ratio decreases, from a value of ≈1.60 recorded in water to a value of ≈0.60 in a hydrocarbon medium." (lines 273–275)

### 12. Grafting degree / graft content (wt%) / 接枝度（接枝含量）

Definition (EN-primary): The mass fraction of amphiphilic macromonomer incorporated into the copolymer (varied here 0–34 wt%). It strongly controls the self-association mode and mesoglobule size, but only weakly affects Tonset.
Original excerpt: "In the solution polymerizations, the macromonomer content varied from 0 to 34 wt%." (lines 706–707)
