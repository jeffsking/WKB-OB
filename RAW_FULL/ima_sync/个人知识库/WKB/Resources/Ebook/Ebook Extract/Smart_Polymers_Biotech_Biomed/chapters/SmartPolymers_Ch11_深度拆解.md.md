# Chapter 11 — Drug Delivery Using Smart Polymers: Recent Advances

(Author: Nicholas A. Peppas; source: Smart Polymers: Applications in Biotechnology and Biomedicine, 2nd Ed., CRC Press, 2008; chapter spans lines 1–1462 of ch11.txt)

## Module 1: Core Arguments

Smart/intelligent hydrogels are the simplest responsive carriers — three-dimensional, hydrophilic, cross-linked networks that imbibe large amounts of water/biological fluid and respond to the physiological environment (pH, ionic strength, temperature, electromagnetic radiation) by expanding or contracting, enabling pulsatile/responsive drug delivery. (lines 27–69)

Network architecture is quantitatively characterizable — the key structural parameters (swollen polymer volume fraction υ2,s, molecular weight between cross-links Mc, and mesh size ξ) are determined by the Flory–Rehner equilibrium-swelling theory and rubber-elasticity theory; ionic moieties add a ΔGionic contribution to the total free energy. (lines 93–272)

Drug release is governed by three mechanism classes — diffusion-controlled, chemically controlled, and swelling-controlled systems; ordinary Fickian diffusion underlies all, but the rate-limiting step differs (membrane/membrane-free transport, polymer erosion, or the glassy–rubbery moving front), and accurate models require coupling structural data to diffusion equations. (lines 769–791, 956–1147)

Dual pH/temperature-responsive copolymers enable intelligent pulsatile release — P(NIPAAm-co-MAA) hydrogels show composition-dependent sensitivity; incorporated macromolecular drugs (e.g., streptokinase) are released only when the mesh size expands past a critical value under the right pH/temperature. (lines 384–394, 1005–1013, 1127–1130)

Interpolymer (complexation) systems offer reversible, environment-tunable behavior — hydrogen-bonding, polyelectrolyte, stereocomplex and charge-transfer complexes (e.g., PMAA/PAA–PEG) undergo reversible complexation/decomplexation driven by pH, temperature, and PEG chain length, with a critical PEG molecular weight (2000 for PMAA, 6000 for PAA). (lines 1184–1290, 1303–1342)

## Module 2: Key Concepts with Operational Definitions

1. Smart / Intelligent Hydrogels (智能水凝胶)
Definition: Three-dimensional, hydrophilic polymeric networks capable of imbibing large amounts of water or biological fluids; insoluble due to chemical or physical cross-links (entanglements/crystallites); they respond to the environment by swelling/contracting.
Original excerpt: "These are three-dimensional, hydrophilic, polymeric networks capable of imbibing large amounts of water or biological fluids." (lines 34–37)

2. Polymer Volume Fraction in the Swollen State, υ2,s (溶胀态聚合物体积分数)
Definition: A structural parameter measuring the amount of biological fluid imbibed by the hydrogel; it is one of the three principal network-characterization parameters (with Mc and ξ).
Original excerpt: "The polymer volume fraction in the swollen state is a measure of the amount of biological fluid imbibed by the hydrogel." (lines 109–113)

3. Molecular Weight Between Cross-Links, Mc (交联点间分子量)
Definition: The molecular weight of the polymer chain segment between two neighboring cross-linking points (chemical or physical); a direct measure of the degree of cross-linking. Only an average value can be calculated because polymerization is random.
Original excerpt: "The molecular weight between two consecutive cross-links … is a measure of the degree of polymer cross-linking. … only average values of Mc can be calculated." (lines 110–118)

4. Mesh Size / Correlation Length, ξ (网孔尺寸/关联长度)
Definition: The linear distance between two adjacent cross-links; the physical "pore" space available between macromolecular chains for drug diffusion. Computed as ξ = υ2,s^(−2/3) · (2CnMc/Mr)^(1/2) · l.
Original excerpt: "The correlation distance between two adjacent cross-links, ξ, provides a measure of the space available between the macromolecular chains available for drug diffusion." (lines 119–121; full formula 320–375)

5. Flory–Rehner Theory (Flory–Rehner 平衡溶胀理论)
Definition: A combination of thermodynamic (mixing) and elasticity theories stating that an immersed, equilibrium cross-linked gel experiences only two opposing forces — the thermodynamic force of mixing and the polymer retractive force — which balance at equilibrium: ΔGtotal = ΔGelastic + ΔGmixing.
Original excerpt: "a cross-linked polymer gel that is immersed in a fluid … is subject to only two opposing forces: the thermodynamic force of mixing and the retractive force of the polymer chains." (lines 126–134)

6. Rubber Elasticity Theory (橡胶弹性理论)
Definition: Describes the elastic recovery of hydrogels under small deformation and is used experimentally (tensile testing) to back out Mc. For a gel prepared in solvent: τ = (ρRT/Mc)(1 − 2Mc/Mn)(α − α⁻²)(υ2,s/υ2,r)^(1/3).
Original excerpt: "Hydrogels subjected to a relatively small deformation will rapidly recover to their original dimension." (lines 276–284; equation 289–307)

7. Physiologically Responsive Hydrogels (生理响应性水凝胶)
Definition: Hydrogels in which polymer complexes can be broken or the network swollen due to a changing external environment, exhibiting drastic swelling-ratio changes as a function of pH, ionic strength, temperature, and electromagnetic radiation.
Original excerpt: "They exhibit drastic changes in their swelling ratio, especially as a function of pH, ionic strength, temperature, and electromagnetic radiation." (lines 63–69)

8. Fick's Law of Diffusion (Fick 扩散定律)
Definition: The macroscopic description of drug movement through a polymer carrier: flux ji = −Dip·dci/dx and ∂ci/∂t = Dip·∂²ci/∂x², where Dip is the intralpolymer diffusion coefficient of species i.
Original excerpt: "the diffusion of drug molecules through the polymer carrier can be described by Fick's law of diffusion." (lines 796–812; equations 11.15–11.16)

9. Controlled-Release Mechanism Classification (控释机制分类)
Definition: Controlled-release systems are categorized by the rate-limiting step into (a) diffusion-controlled, (b) chemically controlled (erodible / pendant-chain), and (c) swelling-controlled. Ordinary diffusion participates in each to some degree.
Original excerpt: "The mechanisms of drug release offer a convenient way of categorizing controlled-release systems: Diffusion controlled; Chemically controlled; Swelling controlled." (lines 769–775)

10. Reservoir vs. Matrix Diffusion Systems (储库型 vs. 基质型扩散系统)
Definition: Reservoir systems enclose a drug core behind a polymer membrane and give time-independent (zero-order) release; matrix systems disperse drug throughout the network and give fractional release proportional to t^(1/2).
Original excerpt: "Reservoir diffusion-controlled systems consist of a bioactive agent containing a core that is separated from the external environment by a polymer membrane. … the release rate is independent of time, i.e., zero-order." (lines 957–1004)

11. Interpolymer (Complexation) Polymers (聚合物间复合物)
Definition: Higher-order structures formed between macromolecules by secondary binding forces, classified by force type as polyelectrolyte complexes (Coulomb), hydrogen-bonding complexes (Lewis acid/base), stereocomplexes (van der Waals, isotactic/syndiotactic), and charge-transfer complexes (electron donor/acceptor).
Original excerpt: "Interpolymer complexes can be classified based on the nature of the secondary binding forces as: Polyelectrolyte complexes; Hydrogen-bonding complexes; Stereocomplexes; Charge-transfer complexes." (lines 1190–1208)

12. Critical PEG Molecular Weight for Complexation (复合临界 PEG 分子量)
Definition: The minimum PEG chain length (molecular weight) needed for the complexation reaction with a polyacid to occur; experimentally 2000 g/mol for PMAA and 6000 g/mol for PAA.
Original excerpt: "the PEG critical molecular weight needed to promote the complexation reaction with PMAA and PAA is 2000 and 6000, respectively." (lines 1237–1247)

$$\xi = \upsilon_{2,s}^{-2/3}\left(\frac{2C_n M_c}{Mr}\right)^{1/2} l, \quad \alpha = \upsilon{2,s}^{-1/3}, \quad M_c\ \text{from Flory–Rehner (Eq. 11.3/11.4)}$$

### 5.2 Acquisition / Distribution Layer (Nonwoven) — Effective Diffusivity

- Chapter + concept: §11.3.2 porous diffusion; Deff (lines 933–952, Table 11.1 Eq. 11.19).

where ε = porosity/void fraction, τ = tortuosity, Kp = partition, Kr = constriction factor. Faxén microporous relation (Eq. 11.20) gives D/Db → 1 as solute/pore ratio shrinks (lines 946–952).
- Action suggestion: Engineer acquisition-layer nonwoven porosity (ε) and tortuosity (τ) to maximize Deff of urine/aqueous fluid. Use the ε/τ leverage explicitly in process parameter selection for fiber denier, basis weight, and calendering.
