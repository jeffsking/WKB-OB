# Chapter 10 — Smart Boronate-Containing Copolymers and Gels at Solid–Liquid Interfaces, Cell Membranes, and Tissues

(Authors: Alexander E. Ivanov, Igor Yu. Galaev, and Bo Mattiasson; © 2008 Taylor & Francis Group, LLC. Source text lines 1–1634 covered.)

## Module 1: Core Arguments

BCCs are polyelectrolytes whose pendant phenylboronic acid (PBA) groups exist in both an uncharged trigonal form and a charged boronate anion, and their multipoint/multivalent interaction with diols gives far stronger specific binding than low-molecular-weight reagents — the structural basis for all biomedical applications described. (Lines 115–129, 150–155; definitions of multivalent effect at 122–129.)

Boronate-containing copolymer gels (BCGs) and soluble BCCs respond to small changes in their aqueous environment — saccharide/sugar concentration, temperature, and pH — making them stimulus-responsive "smart" materials for biosensors and self-regulated drug delivery. (Lines 139–145; 266–278.)

For glucose-responsive insulin delivery, the key unsolved target is a gel that releases insulin at physiological temperature, pH, and ionic strength; lower-pKa PBA monomers (e.g., DDOPBA, pKa 7.8 vs NAAPBA 8.6) raise the phase-transition temperature toward body temperature, opening this prospect. (Lines 292–305; 397–405.)

Sugar-binding affinity does not always increase with pH, and the pH optimum of complexation can lie below the PBA pKa — boronic acids, including neutral forms, can bind sugars (e.g., Neu5Ac reacts with neutral NPAPBA); steric arrangement and buffer composition also matter. (Lines 194–211.)

BCCs bind living cells, tissues, and mucosal surfaces via multivalent boronate–glycocalix interactions (acting lectinlike), enabling steric protection of RBC, reduction of cell–cell adhesion / leukocyte recruitment, and reversible mucoadhesive plugging of body channels — with little effect on cell viability. (Lines 954–975; 1147–1162; 1343–1359; 1362–1428.)

Nonenzymatic, boronate-based sugar sensors (swelling/impedance/SPR/photonic-crystal) operate at 1–100 mM sugar under neutral conditions, offering stable, inexpensive alternatives to glucose-oxidase sensors for bioprocess and cell-culture monitoring. (Lines 534–598; 944–949.)

## Module 2: Key Concepts with Operational Definitions

### 1. Boronate-Containing Copolymer (BCC)

- Definition: A water-soluble synthetic polyelectrolyte carrying multiple pendant phenylboronic acid (PBA) groups along the chain. Its defining operational feature is multivalent/multipoint binding to diols/sugars, which yields much stronger specific binding than monomeric boronic acids.
- Original excerpt: "water-soluble synthetic polymers with multiple phenylboronate functions (boronate-containing copolymers, BCCs) are of special interest" (Line 115–117); "the inherent property … is their multipoint (or multivalent) interaction with biological counterparts, which results in much stronger specific binding compared with low-molecular-weight reagents" (Lines 122–125).
- Chinese note: 含硼酸酯共聚物——带多个苯硼酸官能团的水溶性合成聚电解质，核心是多价/多点结合。

### 2. Boronate-Containing Copolymer Gel (BCG)

- Definition: A cross-linked network formed by PBA-containing polymers (cross-linked either by a bifunctional vinyl monomer or by complexation with long-chain polyols such as PVA). It swells/shrinks reversibly in response to sugar, temperature, and pH, functioning as a responsive matrix for sensing and self-regulated drug delivery.
- Original excerpt: "PBA-containing polymers were shown to form gels either due to their cross-linking with a bifunctional vinyl monomer or via formation of complexes with long-chain polyols, such as polyvinyl alcohol" (Lines 271–273); "responsive, swelling matrices for self-regulated drug-delivery" (Line 275).
- Chinese note: 含硼酸酯共聚物凝胶——可逆溶胀/收缩的刺激响应网络。

### 3. Phenylboronic Acid (PBA) and its ionization equilibrium

- Definition: A boronic acid derivative that forms reversible covalent complexes with vicinal diols, sugars, polyols, and glycoproteins. Operationally it exists in an acid–base equilibrium: an uncharged trigonal (boronic acid) form and a charged tetrahedral boronate anion. The anionic form is the generally accepted sugar-reactive species, but neutral forms can also bind.
- Original excerpt: "the pendant groups of phenylboronic acid (PBA) being present in both an uncharged trigonal form and a charged boronate form … in a wide range of pH" (Lines 151–153).
- Chinese note: 苯硼酸——与邻二醇/糖可逆络合；存在中性三角态与带电硼酸酯阴离子两种形式。

### 4. pKa and pH-dependent ionization

- Definition: The ionization constant (pKa) marks the pH at which half the PBA groups are ionized. For the monomer analog NPAPBA pKa ≈ 8.6; for the low-MW analog NPAPBA the cited pKa is 8.6 (Line 189), and NAAPBA copolymers show the trigonal↔anion equilibrium over pH 6.5–10 (Lines 153–155). Lowering pKa is a design strategy to raise sugar reactivity at physiological pH.
- Original excerpt: "Ionization constant pKa was reported to be 8.6 for the low-molecular-weight analog … NPAPBA" (Lines 188–189); "pH (from 6.5 to 10 for the copolymers of NAAPBA)" (Line 153–154).
- Chinese note: 电离常数——设计低 pKa 单体（如 DDOPBA 7.8）以提升生理 pH 下的糖响应。

### 5. Multivalent (multipoint) interaction

- Definition: Binding in which many pendant boronate groups on one polymer chain engage multiple diol sites on a target simultaneously. Operationally this produces avidity far exceeding the sum of monovalent affinities, can make binding effectively irreversible (e.g., BCC vs Sepharose), and is the mechanism Nature uses at cell surfaces.
- Original excerpt: "the BCCs can interact with polysaccharides in a multivalent way, thus enhancing the strength of biomimetic binding" (Lines 136–138); "multivalent interaction of the pendant PBA groups with the available diols resulted in irreversible binding of the BCC to the agarose gel" (Lines 1088–1090).
- Chinese note: 多价/多点相互作用——聚合物链上众多硼酸酯基团同时结合，大幅提升结合强度。

### 6. Bidentate (bis-boronate) glucose complex

- Definition: The favored high-stability binding mode in which a single sugar (notably glucose, but not D-galactose/D-mannose) chelates between two pendant boronate groups. This explains high sensitivity of soluble copolymers and glucose-specific cross-linking (shrinkage) of certain gels.
- Original excerpt: "allowing for bidentate chelation of glucose by the pendant boronate groups" (Lines 518–520); "glucose cross-linked the gel due to the sugar's tendency to form bidentate complexes with PBA" (Lines 658–659); "other hexoses, such as D-galactose or D-mannose, do not form bidentate complexes with boronates" (Lines 527–528).
- Chinese note: 双齿（双硼酸酯）葡萄糖络合物——葡萄糖以双齿方式螯合两个硼酸酯，稳定且灵敏。

### 7. Glucose-responsive gel / self-regulated insulin delivery

- Definition: A BCG containing chemically attached PBA designed to swell/release insulin when blood glucose is high. Operational target: controlled release at physiological temperature, pH, ionic strength — not yet achieved at the time of writing.
- Original excerpt: "a glucose-responsive polymer gel, containing chemically attached PBA, with the ability to release insulin at high concentrations of glucose" (Lines 297–298).
- Chinese note: 葡萄糖响应凝胶——高血糖时溶胀并释放胰岛素，目标为生理条件下自控给药。

### 8. Phase-Transition Temperature (TP) and Critical Flocculation Temperature (CFT)

- Definition: For soluble thermoresponsive BCCs, TP is the lower-critical-solution-temperature at which the copolymer precipitates on heating; CFT is the analogous temperature at which a copolymer flocculates. Both shift with glucose concentration because bound hydrophilic glucose prevents dehydration/aggregation of chains.
- Original excerpt: "the critical swelling temperature of the gel (10 mol% NAAPBA) varied in the range of ≈10°C as well as the critical flocculation temperature (CFT) of the copolymer (18 mol% NAAPBA) did" (Lines 460–462); "shift of the phase transition temperature (TP) of the NAAPBA–NIPAM copolymer increases with the increasing molar portion of boronates" (Lines 469–470).
- Chinese note: 相变温度/临界絮凝温度——随葡萄糖浓度移动，是温度-糖双响应的核心指标。

### 9. Mucoadhesive / glycocalix binding

- Definition: The ability of a polymer to adhere to moist mucosal/epithelial surfaces rich in highly hydrated glycoproteins (mucins). BCCs act as ideal mucoadhesives via boronate–sugar multivalent binding to the carbohydrate-rich glycocalix (cell-surface carbohydrate zone).
- Original excerpt: "BCCs exhibiting binding specificity to carbohydrates as ideal mucoadhesives" (Lines 1417–1418); "glycocalix … contains other glycoproteins and proteoglycans that extend into the extracellular space" (Lines 1270–1272).
- Chinese note: 黏膜黏附/糖萼结合——BCC 通过硼酸酯-糖多价结合黏附湿润黏膜。

### 10. PLL-g-(PEG;PBA)

- Definition: Polylysine grafted with polyethylene glycol (PEG) and m-aminophenylboronic acid (PBA) — a comblike BCC that adsorbs to mannan-containing surfaces and sterically protects cells. Its binding to mannan depends on the PEG/lysine ratio (steric repulsion) and PBA content.
- Original excerpt: "prepared comblike copolymers, PLL-g-(PEG;PBA), were found to react with mannan" (Lines 1025–1026); "copolymers … 1:21 and 1:9 PEG/lysine unit ratios reduced the number of adherent IC-21 cells by more than 90%" (Lines 1194–1195).
- Chinese note: PEG 与 PBA 接枝的聚赖氨酸——细胞表面包被与抗黏附试剂。

### 11. Crystalline Colloid Array (CCA) / PCCA photonic sensor

- Definition: A photonic-crystal sensing material where a crystalline colloid array embedded in a polyacrylamide hydrogel (PCCA) diffracts visible light; the diffraction wavelength reports hydrogel volume. Glucose-induced swelling red-shifts the diffraction.
- Original excerpt: "incorporated a crystalline colloid array (CCA) into a polyacrylamide hydrogel with pendant boronic acid groups (PCCA). The embedded CCA diffracted visible light and the diffraction wavelength reported on hydrogel volume" (Lines 638–641).
- Chinese note: 胶体晶体阵列（PCCA）光子传感器——衍射波长反映凝胶体积变化，实现无酶糖检测。

### 12. Sugar association constant (Kass / Log Kass)

- Definition: The equilibrium binding constant quantifying borate/boronate affinity for a given sugar/polyol. Operationally used to rank sugar selectivity and explain sensor response series (e.g., fructose ≫ glucose for phenylboronate).
- Original excerpt: "increasing borate–sugar association constants: 3, 65, 1700 M⁻¹" for α-methyl-D-glucoside, D-glucose, D-fructose (Lines 832–833); full ranked table in Table 10.1 (Lines 866–887).
- Chinese note: 糖结合常数——排序糖选择性（果糖 ≫ 葡萄糖）的核心量化参数。

### 4.1 Mechanism differences vs the preceding chapter(s)

This chapter introduces a recognition-driven, covalent (reversible diol–boronate complex) mechanism that is fundamentally different from the physicochemical bulk phase-transition mechanisms (pure temperature/pH response) typically featured in earlier smart-polymer chapters. |||
|---|---|---|

## Module 5: Business Connections (Hygiene / Sanitary Materials R&D)

The end user is an R&D expert in hygiene/sanitary materials (baby diapers, sanitary pads, adult incontinence). Boronate/smart-polymer chemistry in this chapter maps onto several commercial opportunity areas:

### 5.1 Smart wetness / glucose-sensing for diapers & incontinence products

- Chapter + concept: §10.3.3 nonenzymatic BCG sugar sensors (Lines 534–598; 944–949) + association constant Kass (Table 10.1).
- Key data: nonenzymatic glucose detection linear 1–50 mM (turbid NAAPBA–AAm gel, Lines 714–720) and 1.67–16.7 mM via BCC–PVA electrode (Lines 591–593); detection limit ≈50 µM (Lines 645–646).
- Action suggestion: Prototype a colorimetric/impedance "smart indicator" strip in the diaper core that reports urine glucose (diabetic incontinence / neonatal screening) or ionic-strength-driven wetness — replacing enzyme-based strips with a stable, cheap boronate sensor.

### 5.2 Reversible superabsorbent gel (SAP-like) via boronate cross-linking

- Chapter + concept: §10.4.3 PVA–borax / BCC–PVA gelation (Lines 1430–1443; 1473–1477) + multivalent cross-linking (Lines 136–138; 271–273).
- Key data: plug forms instantly, reduces flow 50- to 200-fold at 40 mbar (Lines 1437–1439); BCC–PVA gels have higher relaxation time / shape stability than PVA–borax (Lines 1473–1477); PVA–borate K_ass only 4 M⁻¹ (Line 1536).
- Action suggestion: Explore reversibly cross-linked SAP (boronate–diol network) for leak-barrier gels or on-demand-disposable cores; tune K_ass low (≈4 M⁻¹) so the gel stays stable in use but dissolves with a competing polyol (fructose) at end-of-life.

### 5.3 Mucoadhesive / skin-contact adhesives for pads and wound care

- Chapter + concept: §10.4.3 BCC as ideal mucoadhesive (Lines 1417–1418; 1362–1428); PLL-g-(PEG;PBA) cell-surface adhesion blocking (Lines 1182–1188).
- Key data: NAAPBA–AAm (13 mol%, 30 mg/ml) drops leukocyte adhesion to near-zero (Lines 1295–1312); higher PBA mol% → better sealing (Table 10.3, Lines 1488–1491).
- Action suggestion: Develop gentle, sugar-specific adhesive for sanitary pads / postpartum / incontinence devices that bonds to mucosal/epithelial glycocalix without cytotoxicity (compare PBA vs PBA-free PLL-g-PEG toxicity, Lines 1197–1208).

### 5.4 Ionic-strength / pH robustness for in-use environment

- Chapter + concept: §10.3.3.1 ionic-strength suppression of sensor (Lines 647–651); pH optimum below pKa (Lines 194–202).
- Key data: CCA sensor response ceases at NaCl >10 mM (Lines 647–651); 4-bromophenylboronic acid binds glucose best at pH 7.5 despite pKa 8.3 (Lines 197–198).
- Action suggestion: Diaper/urine environments are high ionic strength (~0.1–0.2 M) and variable pH — select low-pKa monomers (DDOPBA 7.8) and Na⁺-buffering gel chemistries (PEG-monomethacrylate Na⁺-binding, Lines 653–655) when designing in-core sensors.

### 5.5 Formulas / data "directly usable for process parameter selection"

- Monomer pKa selection: use DDOPBA (pKa 7.8) over NAAPBA (8.6) to shift TP toward 27°C body-proximal operation (Lines 399–403).
- Ionization curve: log(α/(1−α)) vs pH, α = degree of ionization — set processing pH to target α (Lines 236–260).
- Thermo-shift budget: glucose 0→3 g/L (16 mM) moves critical swelling/flocculation temperature by ≈10°C (Lines 459–467).
- Sensor linear range: design for 1–50 mM glucose with turbid NAAPBA–AAm gel; lowest detectable 1.67 mM (Lines 591–593, 714–720).
- Ionic-strength ceiling: keep background NaCl <10 mM for max sensor response, or add Na⁺-binding comonomer (Lines 647–651, 653–655).
- Gelation recipe (plugging): 5 w/v% PVA (Mw 125,000) + 0.1 M borax, 0.5 mL each, two-channel mix → 50–200× flow reduction at 40 mbar (Lines 1434–1439).
- On-demand dissolution: 5 w/v% fructose dissolves PVA–borax plug in 3–5 min (Lines 1478–1480; K_fructose ≫ K_PVA=4 M⁻¹, Line 1536).
- Adhesion/steric design: higher NAAPBA mol% (≥12 mol%) and lower PEG/lysine ratio improve mucosal sealing (Table 10.3, Lines 1488–1491; Table 10.2 trade-off, Lines 1052–1068).
