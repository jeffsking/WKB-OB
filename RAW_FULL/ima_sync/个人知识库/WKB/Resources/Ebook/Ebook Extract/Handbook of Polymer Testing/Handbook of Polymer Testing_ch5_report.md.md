# Handbook of Polymer Testing – Chapter 5: Hardness

Book: Handbook of Polymer Testing – Short-Term Mechanical Tests
Author: Edward N. Brown (Rapra, 2002)
Chapter: 5 – Hardness

## 1. Core Arguments（核心论点）

Hardness is not a fundamental material property.
> "The term hardness is usually taken to mean a measure of modulus derived from the resistance of the material to indentation but has also been applied to scratch resistance and resilience. The mode of deformation under an indentor is a mixture of tension, shear and compression, and hardness is by no means a fundamental property. The result depends on the indentor geometry and degree of indentation as well as the time of indentation after which the measurement is made." [Line 9-14]

Three modes of loading exist, but precision standards rely on constant-force (weights), not springs.
> "The indenting force can be applied in three ways: a) application of a constant force, the resultant indentation being measured; b) measurement of the force required to produce a constant indentation, and c) use of a spring resulting in variation of the indenting force with depth of indentation… Because springs are not considered precision measuring elements, and because the force varies with indentation, standard reference methods use weights to apply a constant force." [Line 24-35]

Metal vs. rubber vs. plastic deformation dictates when the reading is taken.
> "In metals testing, the measurement of the indentation is normally made after removal of the indenting force, whilst in rubbers it is always made with the force applied… Plastics are somewhat between the two and both approaches are used." [Line 46-50]

Different hardness scales are fundamentally not interchangeable.
> "Because of the dependencies mentioned previously, it follows that hardness values according to one method cannot generally be compared with those derived from another. However, a number of conversions have been established which, because of the arbitrary nature of the methods, are at best approximate." [Line 78-84]

Time of load application is a critical, yet inconsistently standardised, parameter.
> "An important factor is the time of application of the load before a reading is taken because the measured hardness decreases with time of application. Unfortunately, there is not universal agreement over the time to be used." [Line 149-155]

Microhardness (Vickers) is the most effective plastic test, yet no international standard exists.
> "There is little doubt that microhardness tests, particularly the Vickers method, have been successfully and usefully applied to plastics and are probably more effective that ball indentation or Rockwell. Hence, it is particularly surprising that no standard has been developed and that there has not been greater consideration of indentation being measured under load." [Line 338-342]

## 2. Key Concepts with Operationalized Definitions

### 2.1 Hardness

- Definition: "a measure of modulus derived from the resistance of the material to indentation but has also been applied to scratch resistance and resilience" [Line 9-10]
- Location: §5.1 Introduction
- Usage context: "hardness is by no means a fundamental property. The result depends on the indentor geometry and degree of indentation as well as the time of indentation" [Line 11-14]
### 2.2 Durometer

- Definition: portable hardness instruments that "always use a spring loading system" [Line 32-33]; "small, hand-held instruments with indentors of given geometry that are pressed into the surface of the material to be measured under a spring of given stiffness" [Line 106-108]
- Location: §5.1 / §5.3.1
- Usage context: "The most widely used form of durometer uses a truncated cone" [Line 40]; "Although originally designed to be hand held, they are often used on a stand which can improve reproducibility" [Line 108-109]
### 2.3 Shore A Scale

- Definition / operation: "For soft plastics the Shore A scale is used… the indentor consists of a truncated cone of included angle 35° and diameter at the flat of 0.79 mm, operating under a spring pressure given by F = 550 + 75 Ha" [Line 113-130]
- Location: §5.3.1
- Usage context: "The Shore A scale is essentially equivalent to the IRHD scale specified in ISO 48" [Line 139-140]; "test pieces must be at least 6 mm thick and measurements are made at least 12 mm from any edge" [Line 142-143]
### 2.4 Shore D Scale

- Definition / operation: "suitable for typical harder plastics materials. This has a sharper indentor of included angle 30° with only a slightly rounded (0.1 mm radius) tip and operates under a spring given by F = 445 Hd" [Line 132-136]
- Location: §5.3.1
- Usage context: "Both Shore scales are also specified for rubbers (although Shore D is not often applicable)" [Line 138]
### 2.5 Ball Indentation Hardness (ISO 2039-1)

- Definition: "hardness is related to the area of the impression and given in N mm⁻². First, a correction for deformation of the frame of the apparatus is made by a somewhat complicated procedure which incorporates empirical constants to give what are called reduced test load and reduced depth of impression" [Line 179-185]
- Location: §5.3.2
- Usage context: "5 mm diameter hardened steel ball is pressed into the test surface under a specified load. An initial load of 9.8 N is applied… The major load is then applied… such that the resulting indentation is between 0.15 and 0.35 mm. The time of application of the load is 30 seconds" [Line 168-177]
### 2.6 Rockwell Hardness (ISO 2039-2)

- Definition: "The Rockwell hardness number is given by the rather curious expression 130 – e where e is the depth of impression after removal of the major load in units of 0.002 mm" [Line 224-228]
- Location: §5.3.3
- Usage context: "there is a fundamental difference in that the indentation is measured after removal of the load, unlike the other procedures above" [Line 224-225]; "The scale should be chosen such that the hardness is between 50 and 115. Five readings are taken and averaged" [Line 228]
### 2.7 Softness (BS 2782 Method 365A)

- Definition: "Instead of converting the indentation to hardness degrees the softness number is recorded as the indentation in units of 0.01 mm" [Line 255-257]
- Location: §5.3.4
- Usage context: "The test piece is required to be between 8 and 10 mm thick… Non-standard dimensions can be used but these must be stated along with the softness number obtained" [Line 257-259]; PVC-specific: "the softness measurement must be made 7 ± 0.2 days after moulding" [Line 263]
### 2.8 Barcol Hardness

- Definition: "The indentor is a hardened steel truncated cone having an angle of 26 degrees and a flat tip of 0.137 mm. It is pressed onto the test piece under an unspecified spring pressure and the maximum indentation measured in units of 0.0076 mm" [Line 271-275]
- Location: §5.3.5
- Usage context: "specifically for glass reinforced plastics and uses the Barcol model 934-1" [Line 271-272]; ASTM D2583 "covers rigid plastics generally" [Line 277-278]
### 2.9 Vickers Hardness (HV)

- Definition: "The Vickers method uses a right diamond pyramid on a square base as the indentor and the mean diagonal of the impression is measured, the hardness relationship being given by the expression HV = 2F sin(θ/2) / d²" [Line 286-297]
- Location: §5.4
- Usage context: "the pyramid indentor usually gives a sharp impression in plastics which makes the diagonal measurement reasonably easy but the measurement is made after elastic recovery" [Line 302-303]; "In a micro Vickers test, the same indentor is used but the loads are in the range up to 200 g" [Line 299-300]
### 2.10 Apparent Hardness

- Definition: "If test pieces are too thin in relation to the indentor and load used, the base material has an effect. Different results may also be obtained on curved surfaces. Measurements made on non-standard test pieces are sometimes referred to as apparent hardness" [Line 57-59]
- Location: §5.1
- Usage context: general caution on specimen geometry
### 2.11 Knoop Micro-hardness

- Definition: "similar to the Vickers test but with the diamond indentor having rhombic cross section with diagonal lengths in the ratio 7:1. This gives rise to smaller indentations, making the shattering of brittle materials less likely, and also makes it possible for anisotropy to be detected" [Line 347-350]
- Location: §5.4
- Usage context: "standardised for metals in ISO 4545" [Line 350-351]; "Amitay-Sadovsky and Wagner evaluated the measurement of Young's modulus from Knoop microtests" [Line 69-70]

## 5. Business Connections (卫材行业应用价值)

卫材行业（卫生用品/卫材）核心材料体系：芯体 (core)、SAP（高吸水树脂，Super Absorbent Polymer）、无纺布 (nonwoven)、胶黏剂 (adhesive / hotmelt)、采购成本控制。硬度测试在这些材料上的应用价值与局限分析如下：

### 5.1 对芯体 (Core) 的价值

芯体（通常为热压/模塑成型的纤维板或塑料支架）属于硬塑料范畴。
- Shore D（[Line 132-138]）与Barcol Hardness（ASTM D2583，[Line 277-278]）是首选方法。Barcol 便携特性对生产线末端的快速 QC 特别有价值。
- 芯体厚度通常 ≥ 6 mm，满足 ISO 868 要求 [Line 142]，可直接采用 Shore D。
- 采购验收中，硬度可作为来料批次的快速一致性指标，比拉伸测试更快。

### 5.2 对 SAP（高吸水树脂）的价值

SAP 吸水后呈水凝胶态，属极软弹性体。
- Shore A（[Line 113-128]）是适用尺度；对凝胶态 SAP，甚至可能需更软的非标准方法，此时结果会被称作"apparent hardness"（[Line 57-59]）。
- 时间依赖性关键：SAP 凝胶具有显著粘弹性，读数时间（1 s vs 15 s，[Line 149-155]）将导致较大差异，需固定 SOP。
- 采购价值：用作 SAP 凝胶硬度一致性监控，防止供应商批次间交联度漂移。

### 5.3 对无纺布 (Nonwoven) 的价值

无纺布结构疏松、易变形，标准硬度测试不适用于单层无纺布。
- 引用 [Line 57-59]："If test pieces are too thin in relation to the indentor and load used, the base material has an effect" —— 无纺布无法满足 6 mm 厚度要求。
- 替代路径：对复合结构（如无纺布 + 背膜 + 芯体）可使用 Barcol 或 Shore D 进行集成硬度评价；或对熔喷/纺粘层在热压后形成的局部硬化区进行评价。

### 5.4 对胶黏剂 (Adhesive / Hotmelt) 的价值

热熔胶（如 EVA、PO、PA）固化后形成弹性体或半硬质塑料层。
- Shore A 是最常用标度，用于表征固化胶层的硬度/粘性平衡 [Line 113-138]。
- 软度标准 BS 2782 365A [Line 253-257] 对PVC 增塑胶黏剂有专门的时效规定（"must be made 7 ± 0.2 days after moulding" [Line 263]），同理胶黏剂批次固化/熟化时间必须标准化。
- 采购端：胶黏剂硬度可作为批次间流变特性一致性的低成本替代指标。

### 5.5 对采购成本控制的价值

- 测试成本极低：Shore durometer 手持便携（[Line 106]），Barcol 同样便携 [Line 271]，无需拉伸机，单次测试几秒完成，适合来料全检/抽检。
- 多标度不可直接换算的警示（[Line 78-84]）对采购至关重要：合同中必须明确指定方法（Shore A vs D vs Barcol vs Rockwell）及读数时间，否则同一材料不同供应商报出的硬度无法横向比较。
- 时间标准化（[Line 149-155]）：ISO 用 15 s、ASTM 用 1 s，卫材企业跨国采购时需统一采用一个时间 SOP，避免验收争议。
- 厚度限制（[Line 142]、[Line 257]）：薄材料（无纺布、SAP 涂层）无法满足 6 mm 要求，采购验收时应要求供应商采用"apparent hardness"并明确标注，或改用拉伸/模量指标替代。

### 5.6 应用总结矩阵

||||
|---|---|---|
|芯体 (core)|Shore D (ISO 868)、Barcol (ASTM D2583)|厚度≥6 mm，便携 QC|
|SAP 凝胶|Shore A|严格固定读数时间；结果视为 apparent hardness|
|无纺布（单层）|不适用|太薄，改用模量/克重指标|
|胶黏剂/热熔胶|Shore A；PVC 系用 BS 2782 365A|固化/熟化时间必须标准化（参考 PVC 的 7 ± 0.2 天）|
|采购合同|明确方法 + 读数时间|严禁跨标度比较（[Line 78-84]）|
