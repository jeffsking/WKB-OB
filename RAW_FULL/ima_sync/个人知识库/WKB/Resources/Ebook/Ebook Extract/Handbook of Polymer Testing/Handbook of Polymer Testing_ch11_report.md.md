# Handbook of Polymer Testing – Short-Term Mechanical Tests

## Chapter 11: Tear Properties — Deep-Read Report

Book: Handbook of Polymer Testing – Short-Term Mechanical Tests
Author: Edward N. Brown (Rapra, 2002)
Chapter: 11 — Tear Properties (Book pp. 177–184)

## 1. Core Arguments（核心论点）

CA-1 — Tear test is a "flaw-concentrated" force, not an evenly applied tensile force.
> "In a tensile test, taken to break, the force to produce failure in a nominally flawless test piece is measured. In a tear test, the force is not applied evenly but concentrated on a deliberate flaw or sharp discontinuity and the force to continuously produce new surface is measured." [Line 8-12]

CA-2 — Tear strength is geometry-dependent, not an intrinsic material property.
> "Because tear strength is very geometry dependent it is not surprising that a many different tests have been devised… Tear strength from common tests is not an intrinsic property of the material and it can be difficult to directly correlate the results of laboratory tests with service performance." [Line 15-22]

CA-3 — Tearing energy (fracture-mechanics approach) is the fundamental, geometry-independent property.
> "The tearing energy is in theory a basic material property and independent of test piece geometry so that if the tearing energy and the elastic characteristics of the material are known the force needed to tear a given geometry can be predicted." [Line 24-29]

CA-4 — Initiation force ≠ propagation force; both matter for service failure assessment.
> "A very important distinction can be made between the force to initiate a tear as opposed to that to propagate a tear… the propagation force can be very much smaller." [Line 54-59]

CA-5 — Standardisation is thin (only two internationally), and different standard bodies still diverge in geometry, speed, and reporting convention.
> "Only two methods are standardised internationally." [Line 116-117]; contrast ASTM's four methods [Line 194-210].

CA-6 — Thickness-normalised vs. absolute tear force: each convention hides a different physical reality.
> "Whilst normalising to the thickness invites people to believe that tear strength is proportional to thickness, ignoring thickness makes no compensation at all." [Line 176-180]

CA-7 — Common tests can give contradictory results; fracture-mechanics-style analysis is the emerging direction.
> "Dawson and Bowes [14] point out that falling dart impact and Elmendorf tear often give contradictory results and consider the use of single specimen J-integral analysis and crack tip opening displacement methods." [Line 233-236]

## 2. Key Concepts with Operationalized Definitions

### KC-1 — Tear test

- English definition (quotation): "In a tear test, the force is not applied evenly but concentrated on a deliberate flaw or sharp discontinuity and the force to continuously produce new surface is measured." [Line 9-11]
- Where used (original excerpt): "Tear tests are only applicable to flexible sheet and thin films." [Line 8]
- Operational note: Requires a pre-existing cut or a sharp re-entry angle; apparatus response must track rapid dynamic force changes [Line 35-37].
### KC-2 — Tear initiation vs. tear propagation

- English definition (quotation): "A very important distinction can be made between the force to initiate a tear as opposed to that to propagate a tear." [Line 54-55]
- Where used (original excerpt): "after a tear has started, perhaps because of an accidental cut, the resistance to propagation will determine whether the damage becomes catastrophic." [Line 55-57]
- Operational note: Angle tear without a cut is the only geometry measuring initiation force [Line 91-92]; all others with a pre-cut measure propagation.
### KC-3 — Tearing energy (fracture-mechanics quantity)

- English definition (quotation): "the concept of energy of tearing which is the energy required to form unit area of new surface during tearing." [Line 24-26]
- Where used (original excerpt): "the tearing energy is in theory a basic material property and independent of test piece geometry." [Line 26-28]
- Operational note: Historically obtained from the trouser test piece; now supplemented by finite element analysis and applied to plastic films [Line 29-33].
### KC-4 — Trouser tear geometry

- English definition (quotation): "A test piece 150 mm by 50 mm is cut along the centre of its long axis, from one end to half way down its length. The two 'legs' so formed are gripped in the stationary and moving jaws of a universal testing machine." [Line 117-120]
- Where used (original excerpt): "In the trouser tear the stresses must include shear forces. It allows the course of tear propagation to be followed and is a relatively easy shape to cut." [Line 94-96]
- Operational note: Standardised in ISO 6383-1 / BS 2782-360B / ASTM D1938 [Line 116-117, 185-186, 194-195].
### KC-5 — Elmendorf tear test (pendulum)

- English definition (quotation): "applies a tearing force by means of a pendulum. Energy stored in the pendulum is used to produce tearing and the amount used is indicated by the energy lost compared to the total available." [Line 39-47]
- Where used (original excerpt): "the test piece is held in the jaws of a pendulum, one jaw fixed and one attached to the pendulum arm. The pendulum is released and causes an initial cut in the test piece to propagate across it." [Line 147-149]
- Operational note: Constant-radius specimen preferred over rectangular for reproducibility; energy window 20–80 % of pendulum capacity [Line 153-163].
### KC-6 — Angle tear (initiation) test

- English definition (quotation): "The angle tear without a cut is the only geometry in general use where an initiation force is measured but it requires the angle of the cutter to be very carefully maintained to get consistent results." [Line 91-92]
- Where used (original excerpt): BS 2782 Method 360C "use the angle tear test piece without a cut… There being no cut in the test piece before testing, this method is a measure of tear initiation." [Line 188-192]; ASTM D1004 at 51 mm/min [Line 197-199].
### KC-7 — Falling dart puncture–propagation test (ASTM D2582)

- English definition (quotation): "A weighted carriage is mounted in a guide channel on a tower and released from a standard height… the probe just touches the surface of the film and thereafter penetrates and rips down the film. The length of tear so produced is taken as the measure of tear resistance." [Line 201-210]
- Where used (original excerpt): "intended to simulate snagging hazards." [Line 201]
- Operational note: Often yields results that contradict Elmendorf — see CA-7.
### KC-8 — Multi-peak trace analysis (BS ISO 6133)

- English definition (quotation): "three procedures for traces having less than 5 peaks, 5-20 peaks and more than 20 peaks… the basic question is whether the mean of the trace the peaks or the valleys are the most relevant criteria?" [Line 130-136]
- Where used (original excerpt): "an irregular wavelike trace results… the standard defines the tearing force as the mean force after ignoring the first 20 mm and last 5 mm of the tearing trace." [Line 123-125]
### KC-9 — Machine direction (MD) vs. cross direction (CD)

- English definition (quotation): "the properties of the film or sheet may vary significantly with direction. Consequently, it is common practice and often specified that tear strength is measured in both the machine or longitudinal direction and the transverse or cross direction." [Line 212-215]

## 4. Chapter Connections

Position in the book: Chapter 11 sits in the mechanical-properties cluster of Handbook of Polymer Testing – Short-Term Mechanical Tests, following chapters on tensile, flexural and impact testing and leading into adhesion/cohesion chapters. Its title page header on pp. 180/183 explicitly reads "Tear Properties," signalling a companion chapter to tensile (Ch. earlier) and impact testing.

Connection to tensile testing (previous chapters): The chapter opens by contrasting tear with tensile — "In a tensile test, taken to break, the force to produce failure in a nominally flawless test piece is measured. In a tear test, the force is not applied evenly but concentrated on a deliberate flaw…" [Line 8-12]. The same universal tensile machine is reused, but with different grips: "Most tear tests involve applying a tensile force to a test piece and a tensile test machine with suitable grips is used." [Line 35-36]. This anchors tear testing within the same apparatus family as tensile.

Connection to impact testing (falling dart): The chapter explicitly borrows the falling-dart concept from impact testing: "some people view the falling dart impact test on films as a puncture test that relates to tear resistance." [Line 224-226]; ASTM D2582 is itself a hybrid impact–tear test [Line 201-210]. Marzinsky is cited arguing Elmendorf can be instrumented like impact: "data analogous to results from an instrumented impact test can be generated." [Line 250-252].

Connection to rubber testing (historical lineage): Tear testing "many of them originating for rubbers" [Line 17-18]; the fracture-mechanics tearing-energy concept was "developed many years ago" for rubbers [Line 24]; the trouser test is "essentially the same as that used for rubbers" [Line 116-118]. Plastics adopt only a subset (ISO 6383) while rubbers retain a wider menu (crescent, Delft, etc.).

Connection to adhesion testing: BS ISO 6133 is titled "Rubber and Plastics – Analysis of Multi-Peak Traces Obtained in Determinations of Tear Strength and Adhesion Strength" [Line 126-128], and Figure 11.3 is labelled "Tear or peel adhesion trace" [Line 141], directly bridging tear and peel/adhesion — methodologically identical trace-analysis problems.

Connection to fatigue and abrasion: "Tearing can occur in almost any product made for sheet or film and is also involved in fatigue and abrasion processes in flexible materials." [Line 15-16]. Cut-growth fatigue tests "standardised for rubbers can be viewed in this way" [Line 228-230], prefiguring longer-term endurance chapters.

Connection to fracture mechanics (cross-chapter methodological theme): The chapter imports J-integral and crack-tip-opening-displacement tools from linear-elastic fracture mechanics [Line 233-236, 245-247], a methodological thread likely echoed in any dedicated fracture chapter.

## 5. Business Connections（卫材行业应用价值）

卫生材料行业（成人纸尿裤、女性卫生用品、婴儿护理品等）的核心结构是 芯体（core）— SAP（高吸水性树脂）— 无纺布（nonwoven）— 胶黏剂（adhesive）— 复合膜/薄膜（film）。本章对每一层都有直接工程价值。

5.1 薄膜/复合膜（PE 内膜、透气膜、SMS 层）— 直接受益
> "Tear tests are only applicable to flexible sheet and thin films." [Line 8]

- PE 内膜、PE/无纺布复合层、透气微孔膜都属于"flexible sheet and thin film"，Elmendorf（ISO 6383-2 / ASTM D1922）和 trouser（ISO 6383-1）是行业内通用检测手段。
- 应用：顶/底膜抗撕要求直接影响产品在包装、运输和穿着过程中的完整性；透气膜 tear resistance 是平衡透湿与防漏的关键指标。
- 商务价值：厚度归一化（"invites people to believe that tear strength is proportional to thickness" [Line 177-178]）还是绝对力值，决定供应商报价对比方式；采购谈判时明确采用哪种 convention 可避免以薄料冒充强料。
5.2 无纺布（纺粘/热粘网/水刺）— 双方向测试
> "tear strength is measured in both the machine or longitudinal direction and the transverse or cross direction." [Line 214-215]

- 无纺布存在显著的 MD/CD 各向异性（纺粘方向 vs. 垂直方向），必须双向测试；在成人失禁用品的腰部弹性区域，CD 方向 tear 常是瓶颈。
- 应用：腰围、腿围防漏弹性条的无纺布 tear 决定产品拉伸后是否易"开线"；SMS 三层布的 tear 影响芯体约束效果。
5.3 撕启动 vs. 撕传播 — 决定"漏一次还是漏全套"
> "the resistance to propagation will determine whether the damage becomes catastrophic." [Line 56-57]

- 对卫生用品，初始撕破（ initiation，如被指甲、包装边角划破）对应 ASTM D1004 / BS 360C；后续传播（如穿着后持续撕开）对应 trouser/Elmendorf。
- 商务价值：若某 SAP 芯体边缘无纺布的 propagation 强度很低（"the propagation force can be very much smaller" [Line 58-59]），即使 initiation 合格也会在使用过程中"一撕到底"导致失禁事故；QC 必须双指标管控。
5.4 胶黏剂（压敏胶条、热熔胶）
- 胶层本身不是独立 test piece，但其与无纺布/薄膜的界面 tear 直接决定了防漏胶条的性能；BS ISO 6133 的多峰迹线分析同时适用于"tear and adhesion traces" [Line 126-127]，为胶条剥离-撕破混合行为提供量化框架。

5.5 SAP 芯体封装（SAP wrap / fluff pulp wrap）
- SAP 层外面通常包裹薄纺粘布（SAP wrap），该布在吸液膨胀后需承受芯体内压产生的边缘应力；trouser 测试（"allows the course of tear propagation to be followed" [Line 94-95]）可复现芯体鼓胀时的 tear 路径。

5.6 采购成本与供应商比较
- 归一化争议：ISO 6383-1 按厚度归一（"dividing it by the film or sheet thickness" [Line 125-126]），ISO 6383-2 不归一（"tearing resistance is the tearing force and not the tearing force normalised to the test piece thickness" [Line 176-177]）。供应商报价时，同一材料可能被两种 convention 报出差异很大的"强度"数字；采购合同必须锁定 test method + reporting convention。
- 速度差异影响结果可比性：ISO trouser 200/250 mm/min、ASTM D1004 51 mm/min（"sufficiently different… to expect differences in test results" [Line 198-199]），跨供应商数据对比时必须换算或统一标准。

5.7 失效分析 — 为什么 Elmendorf 和 Falling dart 不一致
> "falling dart impact and Elmendorf tear often give contradictory results." [Line 233-234]

- 卫材产品在使用中同时经历撕（穿着、穿脱）和穿刺（硬质物体、包装货架）。仅用 Elmendorf 通过验收的产品可能在货架跌落/穿刺（D2582 场景）时失效；研发/品控建议至少两种 test 都做，或者采用 J-integral / tear analyser 这类更物理的方法 [Line 230-236, 245-247]。
5.8 成本-测试效率权衡
> "the original incentive for this approach was to have a relatively cheap and simple standalone apparatus." [Line 47-48]

- Elmendorf 摆锤设备"cheap and simple" [Line 47-48]，适合工厂来料快检；trouser + UTM 适合研发精细表征。在工厂 QC 与研发 lab 之间分工可优化测试成本。
