# Chapter 8 · Shear Properties — 深度拆书报告

书名：Handbook of Polymer Testing – Short-Term Mechanical Tests
作者：Edward N. Brown (Rapra, 2002)
章节：Chapter 8 · Shear Properties
页码范围：pp. 135–142

## 1. Core Arguments（核心论点）

Shear forces are geometrically distinct from tensile/compressive forces, yet critically under-tested.
> "Tensile and compressive forces are normal to the plane on which they act but shear forces are parallel to the plane. Simple shear can be represented by planes sliding parallel to a given plane by an amount proportional to their distance from that plane." [Line 8–10]
> "Shear stresses are involved in many applications of plastics but very little shear testing is carried out except on fibre reinforced materials." [Line 25–26]

Shear modulus and shear strength are conceptually distinct and are rarely measured in the same test.
> "Both shear modulus and the shear strength are of interest, but not necessarily measured in the same test. Shear modulus is usually measured at small strains where the stress-strain relationship is essentially linear." [Line 44–46]

No single loading geometry dominates plastic shear testing; each geometry trades off purity of shear state against practicality.
> "There are a number of loading systems which give rise to shear stresses including lap shear, punch shear, torsion and four point loading." [Line 46–47]
> The lap/sandwich geometry "has not been standardised for quasi-static tests on plastics, probably because of the difficulty of bonding test pieces" [Line 50–51]; punch shear is "relatively popular to measure shear strength of plastics" [Line 66]; torsion is "largely restricted to measuring stiffness of rubbers, flexible plastics and coated fabrics as a means of characterising low temperature performance" [Line 82–84].

Torsional (Clash & Berg) tests are the only ISO general-purpose shear tests for plastics, and their purpose is cold-temperature characterisation rather than room-temperature strength.
> "The only ISO tests for shear of plastics in general are torsional methods. ISO 458-1 … is intended for measuring stiffness in torsion at various temperatures, particularly at temperatures below 0 °C." [Line 116–118]

Standardisation in shear testing is notably loose: tolerances, deflection windows and even specimen dimensions vary between ostensibly equivalent standards.
> For ISO 458-2: "The angle of deflection is in this case limited to between 55° and 65° (so much for standardisation)" [Line 141]
> Punch shear: ASTM D732 "follows the same pattern as Method A, but uses a 50 mm diameter test piece and permits any thickness between 0.125 mm and 12.5 mm." [Line 186–187]

In composites, shear failure is deliberately engineered rather than avoided; reduced span-to-thickness ratio forces interlaminar shear failure as the failure mode.
> "The span is reduced to six times the test piece thickness to encourage shear failure." [Line 96–98]

The Iosipescu notched test is the theoretically cleanest shear configuration – the stress between the notches is pure and uniform shear, applicable beyond its original metallic/fibre-reinforced scope.
> "Perhaps surprisingly, the stress across the test piece between the notches is pure shear and is uniform. The shear stress is simply given by τ = P/A." [Line 233–238]

## 2. Key Concepts with Operationalized Definitions

### 2.1 Simple Shear

- Definition (English original): "Simple shear can be represented by planes sliding parallel to a given plane by an amount proportional to their distance from that plane." [Line 9–10]
- Formalisation: shear stress τ = F / (l × w); shear strain γ = x / h. [Line 13–19]
- Use in context: "Both shear modulus and the shear strength are of interest, but not necessarily measured in the same test." [Line 44–45] — the fundamental equations underlie every subsequent geometry.
### 2.2 Pure Shear

- Definition (English original): "Pure shear … is defined as a homogeneous strain in which one of the principal extensions is zero and the volume is unchanged. If the extension ratio l1 = α while l2 = l then l3 is 1/α." [Line 21–23]
- Use in context: Punch shear "The approximation to pure shear conditions are achieved by a punch bearing on a sheet of material supported by a die." [Line 67–69]; Iosipescu "the stress across the test piece between the notches is pure shear and is uniform." [Line 233–234]
### 2.3 Shear Modulus (G)

- Definition (English original): "Shear modulus is usually measured at small strains where the stress-strain relationship is essentially linear." [Line 45–46]
- Equation: τ/A = Gγ, i.e. F/A = Gγ. [Line 58–64]
- Use in context: "Shear modulus can be measured in torsion, although in practice it is largely restricted to measuring stiffness of rubbers, flexible plastics and coated fabrics as a means of characterising low temperature performance." [Line 82–84]
### 2.4 Punch Shear

- Definition (English original): "Punch shear geometry … The approximation to pure shear conditions are achieved by a punch bearing on a sheet of material supported by a die. The smaller the difference between the internal diameter of the die and the external diameter of the punch the nearer the approximation." [Line 66–69]
- Use in context: "The test is carried out in compression by placing the test piece in a special bolster with a close fitting punch … the load on the punch is increased so as to cause failure … within 15 to 45 seconds from first application of the load." [Line 157–160]
### 2.5 Clash and Berg Apparatus (Torsional Stiffness)

- Definition (English original): "The apparatus, commonly known as Clash and Berg apparatus, is a somewhat crude mechanical device using weights and pulleys to apply a torque to a strip test piece." [Line 121–122]
- Use in context: temperature "is controlled by immersing the test piece and the grips into a suitable liquid contained in a Dewar flask. The flask is cooled with dry ice … and then raising the temperature in steps" [Line 125–128]; used to "evaluating the effect of sub-ambient temperatures" [Line 118–119].
### 2.6 Interlaminar Shear Strength (Short-Beam Method)

- Definition (English original): "The geometry can be chosen to make shear dominant and this is the object of the so called interlaminar shear strength test for fibre reinforced plastics. The span is reduced to six times the test piece thickness to encourage shear failure." [Line 95–98]
- Use in context: ISO 14130 / BS EN ISO 14130 — "Fibre-Reinforced Plastic Composites – Determination of Apparent Interlaminar Shear Strength by Short-Beam Method, 1998." [Line 274–275]
### 2.7 Iosipescu Shear Test

- Definition (English original): "The Iosipescu test piece … Perhaps surprisingly, the stress across the test piece between the notches is pure shear and is uniform. The shear stress is simply given by τ = P/A where P is the applied force and A the cross sectional area between the notches." [Line 214, 233–238]
- Use in context: "is usually associated with testing fibre reinforced materials but in fact it was first developed for use with metals and can be used with unreinforced plastics." [Line 214–217, 238]

## 4. Chapter Connections

Preceding (tensile/compressive → shear): Chapter 8 opens by explicitly positioning shear against the normal-stress families covered earlier: "Tensile and compressive forces are normal to the plane on which they act but shear forces are parallel to the plane." [Line 8–9]. This framing reuses the same stress/strain vocabulary (σ, ε from tensile chapters) but reorients the load axis, making Chapter 8 a necessary continuation of the short-term mechanical test taxonomy.

Concurrent (bending → shear, Chapter 9): Chapter 9 (Bending) is invoked bidirectionally. The chapter warns that bending tests normally avoid shear — "the objective is normally to choose a geometry that makes shear stresses negligible" [Line 94] — but that the same four-point fixture can be repurposed to enforce shear via a 6× span-to-thickness ratio for interlaminar shear strength. This creates a strong cross-reference with Chapter 9: the geometry choice (span/thickness) decides whether a fixture measures flexural or shear modulus.

Following (long-term & viscoelastic): The torsion section's note that "the response is often not linear with strain" [Line 132] and that creep is explicitly excluded by a 5-second measurement window [Line 136] signals that long-term (creep/relaxation) shear behaviour is outside this chapter's scope — pushing the reader toward the book's long-term testing chapters.

Standards ecosystem (ISO/ASTM/BS mesh): Chapter 8 is unusually standards-dense (ISO 458-1/2, ISO 1827, ISO 14130, BS 2782 340A&B/341A, ASTM D732, EN ISO 14130), and each standard maps to a specific geometry. This creates the structural spine for the adjacent chapters' standard lists (e.g., tensile → ISO 527/ASTM D638, flexural → ISO 178).

Composite-materials bridge: Section 8.3 is devoted almost entirely to fibre-reinforced materials (Hedner [14], Iosipescu, ISO 14130, BS 341A). This bridges the chapter to the book's composite-focused chapters and shows that most shear research effort, despite the opening admission that "very little shear testing is carried out except on fibre reinforced materials" [Line 25–26], concentrates there.

## 5. Business Connections（卫材行业应用价值）

卫生用品（卫材）产业链的四大基材——芯体 (core absorbent sheet)、SAP (superabsorbent polymer)、无纺布 (nonwoven fabric)、胶黏剂 (adhesive)——在服役过程中普遍承受面内剪切或层间剪切载荷；本章的测试方法学可直接映射到每个环节：

### 5.1 芯体 (Core) — 层间剪切主导的失效模式

芯体由多层 SAP/绒毛浆/无纺布热粘合而成。在吸收与膨胀阶段，层间相对滑移产生剪切。本章的 short-beam interlaminar shear test (ISO 14130, 跨厚比 6:1) [Line 96–98] 与 BS 2782 341A [Line 277–278] 是评估芯体层间结合强度的直接工具；失败模式从"剥离"到"层间剪切断裂"可通过改变跨厚比来人为筛选，从而在设计阶段验证芯体配方。

### 5.2 SAP 颗粒/凝胶 — 扭矩刚度与低温变硬

SAP 吸水后形成凝胶体。本章的 ISO 458-1 Clash & Berg 扭转变形法 [Line 116–138] 正是为"低温下柔性材料刚度"设计的："evaluating the effect of sub-ambient temperatures" [Line 118–119]。卫材冷链/冬季仓储场景下，SAP-芯体的低温回弹性退化可通过此标准量化，成为采购验收的技术指标。

### 5.3 无纺布 (Nonwoven) — Punch Shear 表征热合点强度

热黏合无纺布的节点结合强度是面内抗剪切的关键。本章 BS 2782 340B / ASTM D732 punch shear [Line 145–155, 186–190] 的"冲头-凹模"构型，可直接用于评定热轧、超声波焊接点的抗剪切强度；ASTM D732 的厚度宽容度 (0.125–12.5 mm) [Line 187] 覆盖了从纺粘/熔喷到多层复合无纺布的全谱系。

### 5.4 胶黏剂 (Adhesive) — Lap Shear 是核心验收项

卫材胶黏剂（热熔胶、压敏胶、水性胶）的服役正是"两平面相对滑移"的剪切工况。本章 lap / sandwich shear geometry [Line 49–56] 是胶黏剂行业最传统的测试模式，但作者直陈其瓶颈："adhesion to the metal plates is likely to be the limiting factor" [Line 56]——即胶黏剂验收常测到的是"板-胶"界面而非胶自身强度，这是卫材采购谈判中经常产生的分歧点。改用 Iosipescu notched test [Line 233–238] 可获得"纯剪切、均匀应力"的真实胶层性能。

### 5.5 采购成本 (Procurement Cost) — 用测试标准化约束报价

本章反复强调标准间的口径差异（ISO 458-2 的 55°–65° 与 ISO 458-1 的 10°–100° 不一致 [Line 130–142]；BS 2782 圆盘 25.3 mm 对 ASTM D732 圆盘 50 mm [Line 186–187]）。卫材采购在跨供应商比对标书时，若不对"按何标准、何种几何、何种载荷速率（15–45 s / 180 s conditioning）"作出约定，则报出的 shear strength 数字不可比。建立统一的剪切验收矩阵（SAP 用 ISO 458、无纺布节点用 D732、胶层用 Iosipescu、芯体用 ISO 14130）可使采购谈判回归真实材料性能，直接降低质量退货与索赔成本。
