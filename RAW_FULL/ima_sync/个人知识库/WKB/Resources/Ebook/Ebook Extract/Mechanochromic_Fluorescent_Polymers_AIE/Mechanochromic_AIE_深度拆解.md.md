## Module 1: Core Arguments

Mechanochromic polymers are defined by an optical read-out of mechanical stress, and this optical output—when fluorescent—must be understood as "mechanochromic fluorescence." "Mechanochromic polymers are defined as materials that are able to detect a mechanical stress through an optical output" (L12–L13); "Being fluorescence, the optical output mechanochromism should be therefore intended as mechanochromic fluorescence" (L81–L82). The functional value claimed is early-stage, non-invasive failure detection: mechanochromism "enables the identification of the failure of in situ materials also at an early stage of damage, thus suggesting applications as anti-counterfeit systems and self-diagnostic materials" (L78–L81).

AIE solves the fundamental bottleneck (ACQ) that had limited all previous solid-state fluorescent sensors. Planar aromatic fluorophores "usually suffer from emission quenching phenomena in concentrated solutions and in the solid state, known as aggregation-caused quenching (ACQ), which strongly limits the number of accessible fluorophores" (L36–L38); AIE, discovered by Ben Zhong Tang in 2001, "arises from the restriction of fluorophore intramolecular motions (RIM)" (L41–L44) and "by allowing light emission in the aggregate and solid state, AIE fluorophores (AIEgens) show a striking impact on energy, optoelectronics, life science and the environment" (L47–L49).

The core mechanistic claim: mechanical force changes the aggregation extent / packing mode of AIEgens inside the matrix, and this is what generates the optical signal. "optical responses towards mechanical stimuli can be greatly amplified upon changes in the aggregation extent of AIEgens in the polymeric matrix due to variations of possible interphase interactions and supramolecular conformations" (L107–L110); "The fluorescent mechanochromism involves the progressive disruption of the fluorogenic aggregates as a function of the external stress that is transferred by the polymer matrix to the dispersed aggregates" (L603–L605).

Two incorporation strategies form the paper's organizing axis — physical dispersion (cheap, commodity-plastic compatible, but segregation-prone) vs. covalent bonding (homogeneous, migration-proof, but synthetically demanding). "the dispersion of chromogenic probes within polymers is certainly a preferred procedure for commodity polymers, even supposing that efforts are needed to prevent fluorophore segregation" (L112–L115); "the covalent bonding of chromophoric units with the macromolecular chains has the advantage of obtaining a material with homogeneously-distributed fluorescent probes whose molecular diffusion and eventual segregation is mostly prevented with time and use" (L116–L118); the covalent route "is however hindered by the need for properly designed reactive moieties on both macromolecular chains and fluorescent probes" (L123–L124).

OFF-ON (turn-on) response is judged superior to ON-OFF, because ON-OFF signals are often invisible to the naked eye. "The examples that have been proposed so far are related to the mechanochromic fluorescent response with the typical ON-OFF behavior. This feature is not considered so straightforward by the scientific community, since the optical response appears sometimes scarcely detectable by the naked eye" (L432–L435); "Both ON-OFF and OFF-ON chromogenic behaviors were taken into account, albeit this latter was considered promising for the development of highly sensitive mechanocromic fluorescent polymers" (L632–L633).

The author's verdict on the current best architecture: AIEgen dissolved in core–shell microcapsules, giving a quantitative ("ratiometric") auto-diagnostic system. "The extreme sensitivity of the AIEgen core to the minimum deformation extent combined with their encapsulation within ductile microcapsules appear so far the best solution for the preparation of auto-diagnostic polymer systems with a ratiometric response that gives access to quantitative assessment" (L634–L638). Forward-looking design rule: "Innovation would be in the chemical transformation of the typical ACQ fluorophores into AIE emitters by functionalizing the planar chromophoric nuclei with twisted propeller-shaped moieties" (L641–L643).

## Module 4: Connections — links to other stimuli-responsive systems and prior art

Multi-stimuli cross-talk is intrinsic, not incidental. The very first AIE-mechanochromic polymer film (cyano-distyrylbenzene/PMMA) responds to heat, pressure and VOC vapors in one material (L237–L239, L248–L254). The PDMS-TPE elastomer responded to VOC + temperature but not demonstrably to stress (L387–L389). Implication for any real sensor: mechanochromic read-out must be de-convoluted from thermochromism and vapochromism. The author's own prior art on this is ref [9] "Fluorescent vapochromism in synthetic polymers" (Minei & Pucci, Polym. Int. 2016, 65, 609–620, L669).

Continuity with the Weder aggregachromic lineage. Mechanochromic polymers were "extensively investigated since the pioneering work proposed by Weder in 2002 [107] and funded on the color changes associated with the formation of chromogenic aggregates based on cyano-substituted oligo(p-phenylene vinylene)" (L599–L601); the same cyano-OPV chemistry had already been microencapsulated by Weder et al. (ref [102], L448–L450). AIE is thus presented as a sensitivity upgrade to an existing platform, not a new platform.

Self-healing + self-reporting coupling. Refs [103]/[104] merge mechanochromic fluorescence with extrinsic (microcapsule) self-healing: the same UV source reads the damage and drives the cure, and emission intensity tracks the mechanical recovery (storage modulus → 10⁵ Pa) (L497–L507, L517–L522). This is the clearest example in the paper of an optical signal used as a process-control variable, not just an alarm.

Crystallinity as the universal switch. Three distinct systems converge on the crystalline/amorphous transition: piezochromism of AIE crystals (L172–L183), semicrystalline thermoplastic plastic-deformation regime where "macromolecular slippage of the crystalline region triggers an aggregates break up" (L374–L376), and the TPE-4N crystalline→amorphous ISC switch (L556–L569). Any semicrystalline film/fiber (PE, PP, PET, PLA) therefore has an intrinsic morphological lever for mechano-optical response.

Nanocarbon analogue. "red emissive carbon dots (R-CDs) that showed pressure-triggered aggregation-induced emission enhancement [80]" — aromatic rings undergo "dynamic intraparticle oscillations" that compression "greatly impeded … thus favoring luminescence emission" (L211–L215). Confirms RIM is a general, molecule-agnostic principle extendable to nanoparticles.

Bio-inspiration and physical structural color. "the color response towards external stimuli can be gathered both from the physical phenomena, such as diffraction and the interference of the incident light [56], and/or from the presence of dyes, fluorophores or pigments" (L67–L69) — an explicit bridge to photonic-crystal / structural-color mechanochromism (a competing non-molecular route).

Polymer-architecture advantages over small molecules. "AIE polymers have many advantages over low mass AIEgens, such as processability, easy functionalization, good thermal stability and possess structural complexity and phase ordering that can be easily modulated by external solicitations [53,91,92]" (L222–L225).

## Module 5: Business Connections — hygiene/sanitary materials (SAP, nonwovens, adhesives, smart packaging, sensing)

A. The single strongest transfer: SBS. The paper's benchmark dispersion system is TPE at 0.01 wt % in SBS styrenic block copolymer (L276–L281, L288). SBS/SIS styrenic block copolymers are precisely the elastomer backbone of hygiene hot-melt adhesives (HMA) and of elastic strands/laminates in pant-style diapers and adult incontinence products. The mechanism relies on SBS's block morphology: "The block morphology of SBS actually enabled the presence of glassy, styrenic island-like phases characterized by more emitting features compared to the corresponding amorphous but no glassy character of the poly(styrene-co-butadiene) random (SBR) copolymer" (L281–L287). Business reading: an AIE tracer at ppm-level in a construction HMA or elastic film could report bond-line strain, elastic-strand over-stretch, or phase-morphology change (i.e., adhesive ageing) without destructive peel testing. The response was fully reversible over cycles (L338–L340) — compatible with repeated donning/wear cycles.

B. Damage/leak self-diagnosis in laminates via microcapsules. The core–shell architecture (1 wt % TPE in hexyl acetate, double-walled PU/poly(urea-formaldehyde) shell, 112 ± 10 µm, stable to 220 °C, dosed at 10 wt %) turns ON at damage with 10 µm resolution and resolves defects of 94–376 µm (L450–L473, L485–L489). Two direct hygiene applications: (i) pinhole/微孔 detection in breathable backsheet films and film–nonwoven laminates — a scratch/perforation lights up, giving an inline optical QC method for leak-path defects; (ii) thermal-stability head-room to 220 °C is above typical HMA application temperatures (~150–170 °C), so capsules could plausibly survive slot-coating/spray application — this is the single most manufacturing-relevant number in the paper. Caveat: capsule size 112 µm is comparable to nonwoven fiber diameters and would need down-sizing for thin (<50 µm) films.

C. OFF-ON is the only commercially viable read-out for consumer products. The author's own conclusion that ON-OFF responses are "sometimes scarcely detectable by the naked eye" (L432–L435) while OFF-ON is "promising for the development of highly sensitive" devices (L632–L633) maps directly onto hygiene indicator design: a wetness/saturation/leak indicator must appear, not disappear, because absence-of-signal is indistinguishable from product failure or a dead sensor. The TPE-4N result — turn-on at ε = 0.13 %, 7× at ε = 65 % (L564–L567) — is a benchmark for "strain-threshold" indicators (e.g., signalling that a pant-type product has been stretched beyond its designed hip range, or that an elastic waistband has lost recovery).

D. Ratiometric = quantitative grading, not binary alarm. "fluorescence intensity that accordingly depends upon the TPE concentration and incorporation of more microcapsules. This feature allowed also for a quantitative assessment of the extent of the mechanical solicitation" (L469–L471), and the two-emitter yellow/blue crack-vs-healed scheme (L517–L522). For hygiene: a two-dye ratiometric scheme could grade saturation levels (dry / partial / full) rather than a single wetness stripe, using one excitation source — relevant for adult-incontinence care-home monitoring and for camera/NIR-based smart-diaper readers.

E. Anti-counterfeit / brand protection & smart packaging. Mechanochromism suggests applications "as anti-counterfeit systems and self-diagnostic materials" (L78–L81), readable with "a low cost UV long range lamp at 366 nm" (L368–L369). For hygiene brands facing counterfeiting in emerging markets, an AIE tracer in the release liner, printing ink, or the polybag/packaging seal gives a tamper/opening-history indicator at negligible dye cost (0.01–0.1 wt %).

F. SAP-specific hypotheses (extrapolation — the paper does not mention SAP). The mechanism "restriction of intramolecular motions" means AIE emission reports on local viscosity/free-volume and network constraint: AIEgens "start to emit radiation when constrained in an aggregate supramolecular state or in high viscosity environment" (L612–L614). A crosslinked polyacrylate SAP particle transitions from glassy-dry → highly swollen low-modulus gel on absorption; an AIE probe covalently attached to the SAP network would in principle turn OFF on swelling (loss of constraint) and could visualise gel-blocking, swelling gradients, and un-utilised core capacity. This is a research hypothesis, not a claim in the source — [原文未涉及 SAP/吸水树脂，属外推].

G. Covalent vs. dispersion decision rule for regulated skin-contact products. Because hygiene products are skin-contact and dye migration/extractables are a safety and staining risk, the paper's covalent argument is decisive: covalent bonding gives "homogeneously-distributed fluorescent probes whose molecular diffusion and eventual segregation is mostly prevented with time and use" (L116–L118). Practical route already demonstrated in a polyurethane at 0.02–0.1 wt % TPE-diol using HDI + BDO + PCL-diol (L396–L401) — chemistry fully compatible with PU elastic films/strands used in hygiene. Conversely, dispersion is "certainly a sustainable procedure and largely applied to commodity plastics" (L628–L629) — acceptable for packaging, not ideal for skin-facing layers.

H. Risk / gap list (what the paper does NOT provide). No data on: humidity/water/urine-electrolyte interference; biocompatibility, skin sensitisation or regulatory status of TPE/TPE-4N; cost; photostability over shelf life; response in nonwovens or fibres (all systems are films, coatings or bulk resins); and no analytical stress–optical constitutive equation — [原文未给出定量应力-光学模型/公式缺失].
