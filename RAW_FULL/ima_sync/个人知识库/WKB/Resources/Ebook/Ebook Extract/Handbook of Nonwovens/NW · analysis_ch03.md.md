# Chapter 3 — Wet-laid web formation

## 1. Chapter Overview

Chapter 3 (C. White) covers wetlaid nonwovens — the papermaking-derived branch of the three-pillar taxonomy (Ch.1). It opens with the standards definition (EN 29092 / ISO / EDANA) that separates wetlaid nonwovens from wetlaid papers, then develops the fluid-mechanics of slurry deposition: the Bernoulli balance giving slice velocity v = √(2gH) for an open headbox and v = √(2(ΔP/ρ + gH)) for a pressurised one, and the two deposition regimes (filtration vs. thickening). The chapter then surveys the fibre palette (cellulose, Tencel/Lyocell with fibrillatable wet strength, SWP synthetic wood pulp, deep-grooved PET, polyolefins) and the bonding toolbox (cellulose hydrogen-bonding, latex, thermal bicomponent PP/PE or PET/CoPET, PVA, hydroentanglement). Throughout, hygiene and wipes appear as named end-uses. For the hygiene R&D expert this chapter is the home of wetlaid cellulose and Tencel wipes, synthetic wood-pulp binders, and the deep-grooved PET fibre whose spontaneous fluid transport is "particularly interesting in webs used in absorbent products" (p.122).

## 2. Key Viewpoints

### Viewpoint 1 — The wetlaid/paper legal boundary

- Page: p.112
- Quote: > "To distinguish wet-laid nonwovens from wet-laid papers, a material shall be regarded as a nonwoven if: more than 50% by mass of its fibrous content is made up of fibres … with a length to diameter ratio of greater than 300; or … more than 30% by mass … and its density is less than 0.40 g/cm³."
- Interpretation: Same L/D > 300 test as Ch.1 — but here it is the operational separator from paper. Directly relevant when a wipe or absorbent pad is formulated at the paper/nonwoven boundary (e.g. high-frequency wetlaid cellulose wipes).
### Viewpoint 2 — Slice velocity is set by head (or head + pressure)

- Page: p.115 / p.116
- Quote: > "This gives the familiar equation relating the slice velocity v to the height H (the 'head') of the flow box pond: v = √(2gH)." (p.115) … "v = √(2(ΔP/ρ + gH))" (p.116, pressurised/air-pad headbox).
- Interpretation: The forming jet speed must match wire speed to avoid disrupting the web (p.115). For the engineer this is the first process-control lever: machine speed ↔ pond height/pressure via a clean square-root law. The pressurised form is how modern high-speed machines run.
### Viewpoint 3 — Two deposition regimes govern uniformity

- Page: p.116
- Quote: > "There are two principal modes of deposition, namely filtration, where the fibres are deposited more or less individually, and thickening, where the fibres have formed agglomerates before deposition is complete."
- Interpretation: Filtration → individual fibres → uniform web; thickening → agglomerates → non-uniform. The choice of whether fibres drain individually or flocculate before landing is the central uniformity control and the root of many wetlaid defect modes.
### Viewpoint 4 — Tencel/Lyocell brings wet strength + fibrillation

- Page: p.121
- Quote: > "and their key characteristics are the wet strength, which is higher than viscose rayon together with good water absorbency, and the ability to fibrillate … Wet-laid webs based on Tencel fibres may be easily hydroentangled and web strength may be increased by the use of longer fibres."
- Interpretation: Lyocell is the regenerated-cellulose fibre whose high wet modulus and fibrillation make strong, absorbent, hydroentangle-friendly wipes — the material backbone of the premium spunlace-wipe segment (and a reason viscose share is rising, Ch.1 p.10).
### Viewpoint 5 — Deep-grooved PET spontaneously transports fluid in absorbent products

- Page: p.122
- Quote: > "a polyester fibre with an eight-leg cross-section known as 'deep grooved polyester fibre'. These special shaped fibres exhibit a high surface area and provide bulk, whilst the grooves are claimed to transport fluids spontaneously within the web. This characteristic makes them particularly interesting in webs used in absorbent products."
- Interpretation: A structure–function gem: capillary grooves built into the fibre cross-section give passive, directional fluid transport — exactly the mechanism an ADL or acquisition layer wants, achieved at the fibre level rather than by web engineering.
### Viewpoint 6 — Thermal bonding via bicomponent PP/PE or PET/CoPET, with no hole

- Page: p.136
- Quote: > "it is now generally preferable to use specific binder fibres such as copolyesters or bicomponents based on the PP/PE or PET/CoPET structure. With bicomponent fibres of the side-by-side or sheath/core configuration, the lower melting point polymer is the polymer that forms the bond."
- Interpretation: Bicomponent thermal bonding is the route to bonded wetlaid structures without latex. The sheath/core or side-by-side low-melt component bonds while the higher-melt component "remains an integral part of the web" — preventing wet-web collapse (p.136), a key property for absorbent pads.

## 4. Argumentation Process

- Define the wetlaid/paper boundary (p.112).
- Derive jet speed from energy balance (p.115–116) → set machine speed.
- Classify deposition (filtration vs thickening) → uniformity lever (p.116).
- Enumerate fibres (cellulose, Lyocell, SWP, deep-grooved PET, polyolefins) → match to properties (p.121–122).
- Enumerate bonds (H-bond, latex, thermal BICO, PVA, hydroentangle) → match to product (p.135–137).
- Conclude with application map including hygiene/wipes (p.139–140).
The logic is formability-first: can the fibre be slurried, deposited, and bonded without collapse or defect?
## 5. Role in the Book

Ch.3 is the wetlaid pillar of the Ch.1 taxonomy, the papermaking sibling of Ch.2 (drylaid) and the slurry sibling of Ch.4 (extrusion). Its bonding section (latex/thermal/hydroentangle, p.135–137) hands directly to Ch.5 (hydroentanglement as a bonding method). The deep-grooved PET and SWP fibres reappear as hygiene-relevant structures later.

## 6. Strengths & Weaknesses

✅ Strengths: Rare first-principles content (Bernoulli eqns) in a handbook otherwise machine-descriptive; explicit hygiene framing of deep-grooved PET (p.122) and SWP/Tencel wipes; clear bonding taxonomy.
⚠️ Limitations: The Bernoulli model is idealised (neglects fibre crowding, turbulence, flocculation dynamics); deposition "filtration vs thickening" is described qualitatively without a quantitative dewatering model; application list (p.139) is a bare enumeration without performance data.

## 7. Key Terms / Concepts

- EN 29092 / ISO 9092 wetlaid test: (p.112) >50% fibres at L/D > 300, or >30% at density  g/cm³.
- Slice velocity: (p.115) v = √(2gH) (open); v = √(2(ΔP/ρ + gH)) (pressurised).
- Filtration vs thickening deposition: (p.116) individual-fibre vs pre-flocculated landing.
- Tencel/Lyocell: (p.121) regenerated cellulose, high wet strength, fibrillatable, hydroentangle-friendly.
- SWP (synthetic wood pulp): (p.122) PE/PP flash-spun fibrillated fibre, used as thermal binder.
- Deep-grooved PET: (p.122) eight-leg cross-section, grooves transport fluid spontaneously; of interest in absorbent products.
- Bicomponent thermal bond: (p.136) PP/PE or PET/CoPET, low-melt component bonds, no hole left.
## 8. Micro close-reading (ONE key paragraph)

"These special shaped fibres exhibit a high surface area and provide bulk, whilst the grooves are claimed to transport fluids spontaneously within the web. This characteristic makes them particularly interesting in webs used in absorbent products." (p.122, deep-grooved polyester)

- Function: Introduces a fibre-level capillary-transport structure for absorbent webs.
- Technique: Causal chain — cross-section shape → high surface area + grooves → spontaneous fluid transport → absorbent-product relevance.
- Keywords: "eight-leg cross-section," "high surface area," "transport fluids spontaneously," "absorbent products."
- Logic relation: The claim elevates transport from a web-design problem to a fibre-design problem, implying performance can be engineered into the filament geometry.
- 💡 Standout line: "particularly interesting in webs used in absorbent products" — the author explicitly tags the hygiene relevance rather than leaving it implicit.
## 9. Concept deep-dive

Core concept: the Bernoulli slice-velocity law and its deposition consequence.
- Definition (p.115–116): energy balance on the flow-box pond gives v = √(2gH); with pressurisation, v = √(2(ΔP/ρ + gH)).
- Operational definition? Partly — it is a design equation (set pond height/pressure for target wire speed) but assumes ideal, fibre-free water; real slurries deviate via flocculation/drainage.
- Usage in THIS chapter: Used to (a) match jet to wire speed and (b) motivate the open vs closed (pressurised) headbox choice for high-speed machines.
- Relation to other concepts: Slice velocity sets drainage rate, which selects filtration vs thickening deposition (p.116) → uniformity → bonding route.
- Academic origin: Classical fluid mechanics (Bernoulli); textbook derivation in nonwovens context.

## 10. Academic critique & open questions

- Idealised fluid model. The law ignores non-Newtonian slurry behaviour, fibre crowding at the slice, and turbulent jet break-up; real forming windows are empirically corrected (p.115 "small variations … can be used to adjust formation").
- Deposition gap. "Filtration vs thickening" lacks a quantitative dewatering/flocculation model; the chapter stops at nomenclature.
- Open questions: (1) How does deep-grooved PET groove geometry quantitatively map to in-plane permeability and acquisition rate in a real ADL? (2) Can SWP fibrillation be tuned to replace latex entirely in a wetlaid wipe while keeping dry/lint performance?
## 11. Hygiene-material relevance & action cards

Relevance rating: ★★★★★ — wetlaid cellulose/Tencel wipes, SWP binders, deep-grooved PET for absorbent products, and thermal-BICO bonding are all directly hygiene-relevant; the chapter names "Hygiene products," "Kitchen wipes," and "surgical clothing and drapes" (p.139–140).

ACTION CARD 1 — Wetlaid vs drylaid wipe fibre choice.
- Situation: Specifying a cellulose/Tencel wetlaid or airlaid wipe.
- Apply: Use Tencel/Lyocell for high wet strength + fibrillation + hydroentangle compatibility (p.121); use SWP as a thermally bondable synthetic wood-pulp analogue (p.122).
- Effect: Strong, absorbent, latex-free wipes.

ACTION CARD 2 — Fibre-level fluid transport.
- Situation: Designing an acquisition/distribution layer.
- Apply: Consider deep-grooved PET (p.122) for spontaneous in-web fluid transport instead of relying solely on web porosity.
- Effect: Faster, more uniform acquisition without extra chemical treatment.

ACTION CARD 3 — Latex-free bonding.
- Situation: Regulatory/skin-contact pressure to remove latex.
- Apply: Switch to bicomponent PP/PE or PET/CoPET thermal bonding (p.136), which leaves no hole and prevents wet-web collapse.
- Effect: Bonded web with intact bulk and no latex allergen.
