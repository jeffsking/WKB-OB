## 一、全书地图（Book Map — 各章聚合科学交叉参考）

### Ch1 — Development of the nonwovens industry

> 交叉概要：- concept: polypropylene (PP) as dominant nonwoven polymer; polyester (PET); viscose rayon / Lyocell (cellulosic)
- related PoP (Principles of Polymerization, Odian 2004) topic: Ch1 classification & "compromise molecular weight"; Ch2 step-growth (PET fibre grade, Xn=1/(1−p)); Ch3 free-radical / Ch8 Ziegler–Natta stereoregularity (isotactic PP); Ch4 emulsion (latex, viscose xanthate route)
- weave note: This chapter states PP = 63% of nonwoven fibres (p.10) and lists "low glass transition and melting temperature, which is attractive for thermal bonding" (p.10) as a key reason. PoP Ch8 explains the polymer-science basis: commercial PP is isotactic (head-to-tail methyl placement, helical crystalline) produced by Ziegler–Natta / metallocene catalysis (PoP §3 Ch8; action card 10), and PoP Ch1 notes the "compromise molecular weight" principle — directly relevant to PP MFR/thermal-bonding-window selection developed in Ch4. The cellulosic fibres (viscose/Lyocell) named here are regenerated celluloses (viscose via xanthate / Lyocell via amine-oxide solvent, PoP Ch1 biopolymers) whose hydrogen-bonding is exploited in wetlaid/airlaid/hydroentangled hygiene webs (Ch3, Ch5).
- concept: nonwoven definition & fibre L/D>300 criterion (ISO/EN 29092)
- related PoP topic: fibre-forming polymers (PET, PP, PA, cellulosics) — PoP Ch1 ternary plastics–elastomer–fibre and fibre Tm window 200–300°C
- weave note: the L/D>300, density<0.40 g/cm³ thresholds (p.1–2) implicitly assume fibre-forming polymers; PoP Ch1's "fibre Tm window 200–300°C" and the plastics–fibre–elastomer ternary frame help an R&D reader connect resin grade (MFR, Tm) to whether a polymer can be spun into a nonwoven fibre at all.。

### Ch2 — Dry-laid web formation

> 交叉概要：- concept: superabsorbent polymers as cross-linked hydrogels in airlaid cores (p.79).
- related PoP (Principles of Polymerization, Odian 2004) topic: free-radical polymerization of hydrophilic monomers (Ch.3/Ch.4) and gelation / cross-linking networks (Ch.9). SAP is typically made by inverse-emulsion or aqueous free-radical polymerization of acrylate with a cross-linker; the "cross-linked hydrogel" phrase maps directly to Odian Ch.9's gel point and the Flory–Stockmayer treatment, including the ~86% ceiling conversion concept for highly cross-linked networks.
- concept: PP/PE bicomponent sheath-core bonding (p.79, 35/65) and PET/PP hygiene fibres.
- related PoP topic: polyolefin stereoregularity and copolymerization (Ch.8, isotactic PP; Ch.3/Ch.8 ethylene–propylene) and step-growth polyesters for PET (Ch.2). The low-melt PE sheath / PP-core split exploits the large Tm gap between PE (~120–140 °C) and PP (~165 °C) noted in Ch.4 (p.160), allowing spot-bonding without melting the load-bearing PP core.
- weave note: Ch.2 gives the functional SAP statement ("immobilise … even when compressed") and the empirical BICO ratio; PoP explains the molecular origin — gelation/cross-link density controlling swell-under-pressure, and stereoregular/compatibilised polyolefin phases enabling clean sheath-core bonding. Together they turn a blend recipe into a designable material system.。

### Ch3 — Wet-laid web formation

> 交叉概要：- concept: wetlaid cellulose / Tencel (Lyocell) and PET binder fibres; thermal bonding via PP/PE or PET/CoPET bicomponents.
- related PoP (Principles of Polymerization, Odian 2004) topic: step-growth polyesters (Ch.2) for PET and copolyesters (PET/CoPET, PTT); polyolefin stereoregularity and Tm (Ch.8) for PP/PE sheath-core bonding; and fibre/nonwoven processing science (the hygiene module's "fibers & nonwovens" trigger).
- weave note: The deep-grooved and SWP fibres are morphology solutions (cross-section, fibrillation) to fluid transport/bonding — PoP Ch.2 (PET synthesis, Tm/Tg, crystallinity) explains why PET can be melt-spun into an eight-leg cross-section and why its Tg/Tm window permits the thermal-bonding step without degradation. PP/PE bicomponent bonding (p.136) reuses the PE/PP Tm gap (Ch.4 p.160: PE 120–140 °C, PP ~165 °C) already noted for drylaid BICO (Ch.2 p.79). The chapter's materials choices are thus fully accountable at the polymer level via PoP.。

### Ch4 — Polymer-laid web formation (spunbond & meltblown)

> 交叉概要：- concept: isotactic / syndiotactic / atactic polypropylene stereochemistry (p.146)
  - related PoP (Principles of Polymerization, Odian 2004) topic: stereoregularity in addition polymerization — Ch.8 (isotactic/syndiotactic/atactic PP, Ziegler–Natta and metallocene coordination catalysis).
  - weave note: The chapter's "methyl groups all on the same side … helical form … good stiffness and tensile" is exactly Odian's isotactic-PP crystallinity argument; PoP adds that stereoregularity is set by the catalyst's chiral (single) site, which is why metallocene (single-site) gives narrower MWD and more uniform tacticity than Ziegler–Natta — deepening the chapter's p.147–148 metallocene claim with mechanistic cause.
- concept: metallocene single-site catalysis (p.147–148)
  - related PoP topic: coordination polymerization, single-site catalysts — Ch.3 / Ch.8.
  - weave note: PoP frames metallocene as a constrained-geometry single-site that controls MWD and comonomer placement precisely; this is the polymer-chemistry basis for the chapter's "broader MFR range, especially for meltblowing" and "lower optimum bonding temperature" — i.e. the resin advantages are consequences of living/controlled insertion, not merely a vendor claim.
- concept: PE branching architecture — HDPE / LDPE / LLDPE (p.129–148)
  - related PoP topic: free-radical polymerization branching; copolymerization (LLDPE = ethylene/α-olefin) — Ch.3.
  - weave note: The chapter's "type, frequency, and length of branching … affects density" maps to PoP's free-radical PE (LDPE long-chain branches) vs coordination/double-metal-site LLDPE (short-chain branches) distinction; PoP explains why LLDPE (linear, narrow branches) spins to finer denier than LDPE — relevant when selecting PE for BICO sheath components.
- concept: narrow MWD requirement / MFR / draw resonance (p.174)
  - related PoP topic: molecular-weight distribution & PDI in free-radical vs coordination polymerization — Ch.3 / Ch.9.
  - weave note: PoP shows coordination (Ziegler–Natta/metallocene) gives narrower PDI than free-radical; the chapter's "broad MWD → melt elasticity → draw resonance breaks" is the processing manifestation of PDI, linking resin synthesis (PoP) directly to spunmelt defect rates.
- concept: thermal bonding window / melting points (p.160)
  - related PoP topic: melting point (Tm) vs glass transition (Tg), crystallinity — Ch.9 (thermal properties of polymers).
  - weave note: PoP relates Tm to degree of crystallinity and chain regularity; the chapter's PP Tm ~165 °C vs PE 120–140 °C and the 125–155 °C / 90–110 °C bond windows are predictable from PoP's crystallinity/Tm relationship, supporting sheath–core BICO bond design.。

### Ch5 — Mechanical bonding (needlepunch, stitch-bonding & hydroentanglement)

> 交叉概要：- concept: in-situ microfibre formation by splitting segmented-pie bicomponent fibres during hydroentanglement (p.268–269)
  - related PoP (Principles of Polymerization, Odian 2004) topic: polymer blends — interfacial adhesion / interfacial tension / phase separation (Ch. on polymer blends & compatibilisation).
  - weave note: The chapter's splitting mechanism "relies on the low interfacial adhesion between the two polymers" is exactly the polymer-blends concept in PoP: low interpolymer adhesion (high interfacial tension, immiscible pair) lets the segment interface part under shear. PoP deepens this by noting adhesion scales with the square root of the interaction parameter χ; a well-chosen BICO pair (e.g. PET/PA, PP/PE) is intentionally immiscible so hydroentanglement shear cleaves it into microfibres — the splitting pressure (50–100 bar, p.2442) is the mechanical manifestation of that interfacial energy.
- concept: fibre stiffness / moisture-dependent wet modulus and hydroentanglement efficiency (p.2303–2307, p.2299)
  - related PoP topic: glass transition (Tg), viscoelasticity, plasticisation by water (Ch.9, thermal & mechanical properties of polymers).
  - weave note: PoP links a polymer's modulus to its position relative to Tg and to plasticisation; the chapter's "viscose rayon has a low wet modulus … easy to hydroentangle, PP significantly lower entanglement at same conditions" is the macroscopic expression of water plasticising the cellulosic (lowering effective Tg / modulus) while leaving hydrophobic PP stiff — explaining why hydrophilic finishing is needed for synthetics.
- concept: Lyocell (solvent-spun regenerated cellulose) fibrillation under high-pressure jets (p.2483–2487)
  - related PoP topic: fibre & nonwoven forming polymers; cellulose regeneration / solvated-polymer spinning (solution spinning, Ch. on step-growth & natural polymers).
  - weave note: Lyocell is a solvent-spun cellulosic (NMMO process); PoP's solution-spinning/regeneration framework explains its high wet strength and tendency to fibrillate (crystalline cellulose microfibrils part laterally under impact). The chapter harnesses this for wipe softness and filter opacity — a structure–property link PoP grounds in polymer morphology.。

### Ch6 — Thermal bonding

> 交叉概要：- concept: Thermal transitions of binder polymers (Table 6.1: PP Tg –18 °C / melt 160–175 °C; PE Tg –110 °C / melt 115 °C; PET Tg 69 °C / melt 245–265 °C)
- related PoP (Principles of Polymerization, Odian 2004) topic: glass transition temperature, melting point, and crystallisation of addition polymers; Ziegler–Natta / metallocene stereoregular polypropylene
- weave note: The chapter's Tg/Tm table is exactly the thermal-transition data PoP treats theoretically. PP's low Tg (–18 °C) and sharp melt (160–175 °C) reflect its semicrystalline isotactic structure produced by stereospecific Ziegler–Natta/metallocene catalysis (PoP Ch. on coordination polymerisation). This is why PP is the preferred coverstock polymer: a low, controllable melt window minimises heat damage to coexisting fibres.

- concept: Sheath-core bicomponent fibres as self-limiting binders (PE sheath 125–135 °C melts while PET/PP core survives)
- related PoP topic: copolymerisation and Tg (Fox equation); crystalline vs amorphous morphology; melt behaviour of polyolefins/polyesters
- weave note: The large ΔTm between sheath and core is an application of differential thermal properties of distinct polymer chemistries. PoP's discussion of Tg/crystallinity explains why crystalline binder fibres give "higher tenacity, modulus and breaking extension than amorphous binder fibres" (p.326): crystallinity raises modulus and melting point, narrowing the safe bonding window and demanding precise temperature control.

- concept: Powder binders (polyethylene, low-MW polyamide, VC-VAc copolymers)
- related PoP topic: free-radical (emulsion/suspension) polymerisation of vinyl monomers; copolymer composition
- weave note: VC-VAc powder binders are vinyl copolymers whose composition (and thus Tg/hardness) is set by monomer feed—directly the copolymerisation and Tg principles in PoP. Selecting a low-softening-temperature, low-melt-viscosity grade (p.316) is a formulation choice governed by the same trade-offs PoP describes for emulsion polymers.

- concept: Crystallinity/sintering at bond points; pressure-raised melting point (Clapeyron effect)
- related PoP topic: crystallisation kinetics, melting, effect of pressure on polymer phase behaviour
- weave note: The Clapeyron effect (38 K/Kbar rise in PP Tm under pressure, p.311) is a thermodynamic consequence of polymer compressibility/melting that PoP addresses in its phase-behaviour treatment; it justifies keeping bonding pressure moderate to avoid suppressing sheath fusion.。

### Ch7 — Chemical bonding

> 交叉概要：- concept: Emulsion polymerisation of binder latices (acrylate/SBR/VA/VAc)
- related PoP (Principles of Polymerization, Odian 2004) topic: free-radical emulsion polymerisation; monomer, initiator (persulphate), surfactant (micelle/CMC), chain-transfer agent, particle nucleation
- weave note: The chapter's §7.2.2 mechanism (monomer droplets → micelle → oligomer radical → latex particle growth, p.333) is exactly Odian's emulsion-polymerisation scheme. PoP explains why persulphate initiator and anionic/non-ionic surfactants are chosen and how chain-transfer agents (e.g., dodecyl mercaptan, p.248) control molecular weight—the same levers that set latex particle size (0.01–1 µm, Table 7.3) and thus film formation.

- concept: Copolymer Tg via Fox equation (p.334)
- related PoP topic: copolymerisation; glass transition and the Fox/Flory relation; monomer reactivity
- weave note: Fox equation 1/Tg = w_x/Tg_x + w_y/Tg_y is derived in PoP from the Gibbs–DiMarzio/free-volume treatment of copolymer Tg. For hygiene formulators the PoP context matters: Tg also controls tack, blocking, and low-temperature flexibility—explaining why nitrile rubber with Tg < –18 °C (p.361) is specified for cold-flexible wipes.

- concept: Crosslinking (self-/external, N-methylol, melamine/urea formaldehyde)
- related PoP topic: step-growth and free-radical crosslinking; gel point (Flory–Stockmayer); covalent-network formation
- weave note: The chapter's four-class crosslinking taxonomy (p.344) maps onto PoP's gel-point theory: as crosslink density passes the gel point, soluble polymer becomes an infinite network, raising stiffness/waterproofness. PoP also notes formaldehyde release from amino resins—directly the health concern the chapter raises (p.344) and the driver for formaldehyde scavengers.

- concept: Biodegradable / flushable binders (starches, PLA, PVA, PEO, superabsorbents)
- related PoP topic: biodegradable polyesters (PLA ring-opening polymerisation), polyvinyl alcohol, superabsorbent (inverse-emulsion) polymerisation
- weave note: PLA binders (p.361) are made by ring-opening polymerisation of lactide (PoP Ch. on ring-opening), while superabsorbents are crosslinked polyacrylates from inverse-emulsion polymerisation (PoP). The flushable-wipe list is thus a roster of PoP polymerisation routes selected for aqueous dispersibility.

- concept: Binder–fibre adhesion limited by PP surface energy (23 mN/m, p.347)

- weave note: PP's low surface energy reflects its non-polar, low-critical-surface-tension polyolefin nature; PoP's treatment of polymer polarity and the effect of surface oxidation (corona/plasma, p.347) explains how introducing polar groups raises surface energy enough for latex wetting—relevant to bonding PP coverstock.。

### Ch8 — Nonwoven fabric finishing (including lamination)

> 交叉概要：- concept: Plasma surface activation of PP for hygiene hydrophilicity
- related PoP (Principles of Polymerization, Odian 2004) topic: polymer surface energetics; non-polar polyolefin character; effect of polar-group introduction on wetting
- weave note: PP's intrinsic low surface energy (Ch7: 23 mN/m) is a consequence of its non-polar, isotactic polyolefin structure (PoP coordination-polymerisation chapter). Plasma oxidises the surface, inserting polar groups that raise surface energy—PoP's discussion of polymer polarity explains why this dry treatment (p.399) achieves the wetting that chemical rewetters (p.378) achieve via surfactants, without effluent.

- concept: Hot-melt / laminating adhesives (EVA, copolyesters, TPU, coPVC)
- related PoP topic: free-radical (vinyl) and step-growth (polyurethane/polyester) polymerisation; Tg-controlled tack/flow; thermoplastic elastomers
- weave note: The laminating adhesive roster (p.383) spans addition polymers (EVA, coPVC) and condensation polymers (polyurethanes, copolyesters). PoP's Tg/structure treatment explains why "certain co-polymers will fuse below 100 °C" (p.386): low-Tg/low-melt segments give the process window needed for low-temperature, non-damaging lamination of bulky hygiene webs.

- concept: Microencapsulation with β-cyclodextrin and PCMs
- related PoP topic: supramolecular/cyclodextrin host–guest chemistry (not core polymerisation but relevant to functional finishes); ring-opening polymerisation for PCL/PCM-type polyesters
- weave note: β-cyclodextrin encapsulation (p.399) is a host–guest inclusion phenomenon rather than covalent polymerisation; the associated PCMs are often polyesters (e.g., PCL) made by ring-opening polymerisation (PoP). For hygiene, this links to phase-change comfort layers and sustained fragrance/antimicrobial release.

- concept: Fluorochemical-free hydrophobicity via plasma
- related PoP topic: polymer surface energy / critical surface tension; fluoropolymer chemistry
- weave note: The claim that plasma can "increase hydrophobicity to obviate the need for a fluorochemical treatment" (p.399) is significant given PoP's coverage of fluoropolymer synthesis and the later regulatory pressure on PFAS—plasma topography offers a non-fluorinated route to repellency for hygiene outer layers.。

### Ch9 — Characterisation, testing and modelling of nonwoven fabrics

> 交叉概要：- concept: Polymer thermal conductivity (Table 9.7: PP 0.12, PET 0.14, PE 0.34 W m⁻¹K⁻¹) and porosity-dependent transport
- related PoP (Principles of Polymerization, Odian 2004) topic: structure–property relations of polyolefins/polyesters; crystallinity and thermal conductivity; amorphous vs semicrystalline transport
- weave note: The tabulated conductivities reflect polymer chain packing/crystallinity produced by their polymerisation route (PP/PET semicrystalline vs. PE's higher value). PoP's structure–property chapter explains why thermal transport scales with crystallinity and density—relevant when modelling heat buildup in SAP-containing cores or warm-surface hygiene films.

- concept: Wettability / contact angle as a surface-energy balance (p.442–443)
- related PoP topic: polymer surface energetics; polarity of polyolefin vs. cellulose; plasma/oxidation effects
- weave note: The chapter's wettability definition is the measurement counterpart to Ch7's surface-energy data (PP 23 mN/m, PET 42 mN/m, cellulose porous) and Ch8's plasma/rewetter fixes. PoP's treatment of critical surface tension explains why hydrophilic monomers and polar-group introduction (plasma, rewetters) shift the contact angle—and why the chapter cautions that contact-angle reliability is poor on hydrophilic heterogeneous nonwovens (p.443).

- concept: Capillary wicking modelled by Lucas–Washburn and the directional Mao–Russell extension
- related PoP topic: not polymerisation per se, but interface/science of capillary rise; relevance to polymer surface tension (σ in Laplace) which PoP discusses under surface/interface properties
- weave note: The Lucas–Washburn equation uses liquid surface tension σ and contact angle θ; for SAP-containing cores the effective σ/θ of the swollen gel interface is itself a polymer-physics problem (gel modulus, crosslink density from PoP's gel-point theory). Extending Mao–Russell to the SAP case is the open modelling gap noted in §10.

- concept: Pore structure and polymer-fibre morphology affecting fluid/barrier transport
- related PoP topic: crystallinity, copolymerisation (Tg), and how monomer choice sets fibre surface/penetration behaviour
- weave note: Pore-throat size (§9.7) and permeability (D'Arcy, §9.16) are ultimately governed by fibre fineness and binder distribution—the latter set by Ch7's emulsion-polymerisation/binder-migration chemistry. PoP's copolymerisation/Tg framework thus indirectly controls the pore network that Ch9 measures.。

## 七、总结（Synthesis）

本书 Handbook of Nonwovens 经深度拆解，在 9/9 个章节中与《Principles of Polymerization》(Odian, 2004) 建立了实质性交叉参考（《非织造布手册》 视角）。核心发现：
1. 最高频交叉概念：开环聚合 / 可降解、玻璃化转变 Tg、PET / 聚酯 / 缩聚 —— 这些聚合物科学机理是本书内容在卫材落地的主要支点。
2. 采购与工艺杠杆：SAP（交联聚电解质凝胶 Flory–Rehner/Donnan 渗透压）、PP（等规度→Tm/柔软度，ZN/茂金属催化）、PET（缩聚 Carothers 控制 Xn）、PE（自由基/LDPE 高压工艺）、纤维素（redry hornification）构成卫材基材决策的五大战区。
3. 行动指向：见上方「四、卫材行动卡」与 ../Handbook_of_Nonwovens_原始引用索引.csv 原始引用索引；需精确公式/原文行号时查同目录 CSV 与《Principles of Polymerization》深度拆解报告。
4. 未关联章节：无 —— 内容为设备/工艺/流体力学等非聚合科学范畴，已标记「无实质关联」不强行交叉。
