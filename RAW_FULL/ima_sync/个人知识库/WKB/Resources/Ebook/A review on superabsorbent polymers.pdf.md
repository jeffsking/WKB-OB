## Abstract

Superabsorbent polymers (SAP) are polymer networks that are gaining momentum due to applications in various industries due to their higher liquid absorption capacity and highly porous structure. Apart from their hydrophilic nature which is being exploited in hygiene sector, the structural, thermal, and rheological peculiarities are explored in numerous sectors such as biomedical, hygienic, agriculture, slurry dewatering, and cement and concrete. The current review focuses on the application and the environmental and economic profiling of SAP products. The wide spectrum application of SAP products interferes with the normalization of the associated data. The current review is a maiden attempt to explore the mechanism of absorption and discusses in detail the possible reuse -recycle options. This review further ventures to plot a general trend of SAP on the impact factors drawn from the Technoeconomic analysis (TEA) and life cycle assessment (LCA) studies of existing literature. The review highlights the differences in synthetic and bio-SAP usage in terms of economic and environmental footprint with respect to their area of application. It can be comprehended that both TEA and LCA depend on the life span of the product which in turn is a function of the field of application. The cost of production and environmental impact are discussed as a function of raw materials and unit operations involved in the production process. Hence, it was observed that for applications that have shorter life span synthetic SAP is suitable and for longer span applications bio-SAP can be used.

# 1 Introduction

Superabsorbent polymers (SAPs) or Superabsorbent materials (SAMs) are characterized by their ability to absorb 10–1000% of water or aqueous solutions per dry weight without losing their structural integrity under pressure [1]. SAPs are basically hydrophilic hydrogels with strong physical and/or chemical crosslink structure that makes them unable to dissolve in aqueous solutions. The presence of functional groups such as carboxylic, hydroxyl, amine, imide and carboxamide are found to be responsible for this high degree of hydrophilicity. There have been constant efforts to elevate the water absorbency of SAP while minimizing the economic and ecological footprint. Sanitary products have been the major application of SAPs for decades; however, researchers have been trying to broaden their application to various fields such as agriculture, construction, food processing, wastewater treatment, and biomedical industry. Acrylamide and acrylic acid monomers have found a wide range of applications in SAP industry due to their high-water absorbency, rapid polymerization, biodegradability, and low cost. A different horizon in SAP development is towards copolymerizing biopolymers with synthetic polymers and elevate their characteristics and expand applications. The present review is discussing some of the applications of SAP followed by economic and environmental profiling. The review critically analyses the economic and environmental feasibility of SAP usage in various fields of applications and attempts to coordinate and compare techno-economic and life cycle assessment strategies.

## 1.1 Mechanism of water absorption and retention of SAP

SAPs can be classified into different groups based on specific criteria as illustrated in Fig. 1. Water absorption in SAP is primarily driven by diffusion and hydrophilic attractions. A schematic representation of response of SAP crosslinked structure to liquid and time of exposure has been shown in Fig. 2. Porosity and the ability to form intra and inter polymeric dynamic bonding are the key factors driving water absorption in SAP. The presence of hydrophilic and ionic functional groups in the chemical structure and the addition of crosslinkers to form highly porous and dense polymeric network influence the absorption capacity. The concentration gradient of water created between soaked solution and the inside of the SAP molecule guides the water molecules to diffuse through the mesh network. The hydrophilic groups in the crosslinked structure of SAP molecules create osmotic pressure which will drive the water molecules to move inside the matrix. Furthermore, the electrostatic repulsion between hydrophilic groups will force the individual units to move away from each other. # 1.2 Reusability and recyclability

Reusability of SAP depends on the potential of SAP structure to regenerate after its assigned purpose. Reusability of SAP greatly depends on where it was being used. For 'leave-on' applications such as in agriculture and construction, reusability is not an option. In the case of dewatering for non-toxic unreactive slurries the reusability can be significant. However, for biological and toxic fluids, reusability is limited due to the need of intensive work force and cleaning strategies whereas the possibility of recyclability still stands [16]. Chazovachii et al. [17] have reported the possibility of recycling the used and disposed baby diapers for pressure sensitive adhesives by depolymerization of the SAP and Yu et al. [18] reported the use of porous carbon produced from used SAP as inert support for hydrodesulfurization catalyst. Whereas, Al-Jabari et al. [19] discussed the potential of recovered SAP from baby diapers to be repurposed in agriculture for better irrigation management. Yunkai et al. [20] observed with ESEM characterization that after reusing polyacrylate SAP for 3 times, the internal structure of SAP changed from discrete gridding pattern to a more bigger porous network structure. This has significantly reduced the water retention capacity and physical strength. This structural deterioration can be reduced by selecting SAP polymers which are porous yet insoluble in water to a limit or the dewatering techniques can be made less toxic to the polymers. Desorption of water from SAP must deal with two kinds of bound water molecules; loosely bound and strongly bound. Loosely bound water molecules are the physically trapped ones inside the matrix structure of SAP while strongly bound water molecules will be retained inside the SAP due to their strong hydrophilic interactions with the functional groups present. SAP molecules are responsive to external stimuli like pH and temperature. Loosely bound water molecules can be extracted by changing the pH of the solution to acidic range and/ or creating a concentration gradient or simply drying in the air. Strongly bound water molecules can be removed by washing the SAP back to pH 5–6 with water and oven dry at 55°C [21]. Joseph-Soly et al. [22] has shown

# 2 Applications of SAP

Hydrogels and SAPs are similar polymeric materials, with an indefinite separation in their water absorbance capacity. The earlier conception of SAP was that they can be used at places where hydrogels fail to meet the absorbance criteria. However, new out-looks on this area of research have shown a myriad of applications. The primary design and requirements of SAP are different in each field of application. Hence, the tailored designs are expected to meet the demand in the field of application in terms of structural and functional requirements including size, absorbency, mechanical strength, thermal characteristics, and additional active properties along with the overall compatibility with the environment of application and requirement of degradability. For example, biodegradability of SAP is an essential criterion in the biomedical and hygiene applications whereas the degradation rate is a quality under question for a leave-on product such as in agriculture, cement and concrete applications. A comprehensive review on the application of SAP in different fields is as follows.

## 2.1 Biomedical application

Due to their liquid absorptivity and retention capacity biocompatible SAPs have spurred medical biologists into developing SAP based products for drug delivery, tissue engineering, and wound dressing. Liquid absorbing capacity of pharmaceutically active or non-toxic polymers can be enhanced by crosslinking, surface modification or copolymerization to make them superabsorbent. Such polymers can be used to remove bodily fluids during or after surgery. Exudating wounds after surgery, leg ulcer or edema blisters are difficult to nurse due to their highly prone nature to contamination. In these cases, SAP based wound dressings have replaced fluffy cotton-based dressing materials. Even though exudate removal is required, it is also important to keep the wound moist to promote fast healing. Hence, SAP dosage must be determined according to the nature of the wound. Veličković et al. [21] reported that SAP wound dressings have enhanced the healing rate of venous leg ulcer by 2.58% when compared to foam dressing over an observation period of 6 months. This is backed by Veličković et al. [22] where the number of dressings changed per week for foam dressings was 4 while that of SAP dressings were 2.8; stating that SAP dressings are more functionally and economically efficient than conventional foam counterparts. The absorption capacity of SAP is utilized

# 2.2 Hygienic sector

The ability of SAP to absorb solubilized salts and molecules in urine and blood in addition to water are being utilized in incontinence products such as baby and adult diapers and feminine hygiene products [26]. Two types of SAP; fluffy SAP and gel SAP are used in incontinence products. Brazzelli et al. [27] reported that from their clinical study people who used SAP based incontinence product experienced lesser skin problems than those who used fluff pulp-based products. Even though both the products are known to retain moisture; gel SAP products have better water retaining strength compared to fluff pulp products and this can make the moisture content from fluff pulp products to interact with skin more rapidly compared to SAP products. The gel formation in SAP during hydrogen bonding with water molecules prevent the leakage in hygiene products. The stability of the formed gel depends on the density of hydrophilic groups and mechanical strength of the gel under fully absorbed conditions. Hence, a thorough characterization of SAP in dry condition and absorbed condition are essential before its application in hygiene product. In addition to the functional standards, safety and environmental standards must be complied with incontinence products. An administrative level standardization ensures safety and compliance with regulatory standards. These standards include sustainability of raw material sources, emissions and lifecycle compliance of the production process, and functionality compliance and biodegradability of the product. The common certification frameworks followed in the hygiene industry are Ecolabel by European Union [24], Technical specifications by UNICEF [25], Guidelines by U. S Food and Drug Administration [26], and Product manual by Burau of Indian Standards (IS 5405:2019). These frameworks help to ensure the industrial, user, and environmental safety. Scientific community has always been concerned about the single use of SAP in this sector and are in search for better disposal strategies. This curates the permanent changes biological fluids can make to the SAP characteristics as discussed earlier. Even though SAP in incontinence products is one of the first widely accepted applications, the search for betterment in terms of functionality and disposal is still under consideration.

# 2.3 Agriculture industry

Agriculture needs water in voluminous amounts at different stages of farming. However, the current trend in climate change is challenging the farmers to stick to the conventional methods of farming. Even though researchers are looking for draught withstanding varieties of crops; another set is focused on the optimization of water usage and better rainwater harvesting strategies in farming. These strategies aim to reduce the impact of water stress on harvest and crop yield and thereby improve the water productivity of crops, which means 'high crop yield with minimum water usage' [38]. Application of SAP can change the physical properties of soil such as soil permeability, soil bulk density and porosity and have shown to improve the structural and thermal stability of soil. Hou et al. [39] reported that 90 kg/ha application of SAP in dry soil has reduced the soil bulk density by 6.4% and porosity was increased by 12.7%. # 2.4 Cement and concrete application

Cement is one of the foundation materials in the construction and building industry. The duration and economy of a building construction unit can be explained as a function of cement and its curing process. Curing cement takes time and needs a high-water content. Research has shown that cement mixtures with low water to cement ratio (w/c ratio) are more susceptible to self-desiccation leading to autogenous shrinkage and thereby causing cracks. A w/c ratio of 0.42 is minimum as per Justs et al. [44]. Therefore, it is important to develop methods and procedures which will keep the cement moist during the curing process. SAPs have found scope in the industry as a supplementary cementitious material (SCM) and several studies have found that the addition of SAP has provided self-healing ability and reduced shrinkages and they acted as a barrier for otherwise damaging ions like $Na^+$, $Ca^+$, $Cl^-$, etc. The economic aspect of cementitious materials lies in crack management. When SAP is present, the water penetrating through the cracks will be absorbed by SAP to expand and thereby block the spread of cracks. In addition to crack mitigation, SAP influences the rheological properties of concrete [13]. Chen et al. [44] have reported the use of SAP as rheology modifier in 3D printable cementitious material and has resulted in an elevated apparent viscosity and dynamic yield stress but has a compromised compressive strength.

# 2.5 Slurry dewatering

Considering the rapidly growing demand for metals and minerals in construction, manufacturing, electronics and technology industries, mining is obligatory. The pressure to meet the demand has had adverse effects on high-grade ore deposits; and this has forced the mining industry to rely on low grade mines. One of the challenges in the operation of low-grade mining is the copious amount of water generated, and this subsequently affects the economics of process. SAP assisted dewatering has several advantages over conventional methods such as gravity-assisted and flocculant-assisted method. The primary advantage is the reusability of SAP and provision to collect and reuse the extracted water. SAPs can be directly added to the slurry; however, the recovery can be a hurdle again. Whereas SAP sachets are easier to use and economic in terms of the work force required to separate them after dewatering and process them to reuse. Some of the case studies on the use of SAP for slurry dewatering are listed in Table 3. pH and ionic strength of the slurry are two important aspects to be considered while using SAP for dewatering. Even though there is no significant effect of pH on water absorption capacity can be found; sodium polyacrylate has shown higher water absorptivity at pH 7 [18]. Since SAP efficiency depends on the pH of the slurry being used, care should be taken while selecting the SAP for a specific slurry. Joseph-Soly et al. [45] reported that when sodium polyacrylate SAP was used for dewatering of sodium-montmorillonite and saprolitic nickel laterite slurry, the water recovery and SAP regeneration were affected by montmorillonite particles, which can easily get absorbed and retained in the SAP structure.

During manufacturing, water usage for washing and purification, high energy consumption due to extreme process conditions and purification, and byproduct generation are to be considered. The management of waste streams and energy requirement are directly linked to environmental impact. Even though all these component operations are valid in the case of bio-SAP production, the mild processing conditions during raw material sourcing, copolymerization and modification and formation of less toxic, easily manageable byproducts can reduce the environmental burden. The variation in the material and energy flow for each unit operation involved in the overall production process seeks suitable waste management and safety measure strategies at different stages of production. Generally, synthetic SAP refinery might not be included in the system boundary. The production unit gets the raw material or the platform chemical in the refined form and the system directly starts with the SAP synthesis process. Whereas in the case of bio-SAP the refinery will be within the system boundary. This can make the LCA of bio-SAP tedious and time consuming. However, since most of the synthetic SAP products are non-biodegradable in nature, incineration and landfilling are the only options for end-of-life scenario if cradle-to-grave approach has been chosen. Whereas, bio-SAP can have incineration, landfilling, and biodegradation as an end-of-life scenario. These selections are crucial while interpreting the LCA results.

Several studies show that bio-SAP is causing more environmental impact than its fossil-derived counterparts during their production and application. This can be related to the lower swelling capacity of bio-SAP and thereby higher quantity of bio-SAP needed to obtain the same swelling capacity as that of fossil-based SAP. Analysis on individual impact categories reveals that the total energy input for the production stage of bio-SAP is higher than fossil-based SAP. But GWP is higher for fossil-based SAP than their

bio counterpart. This points to the importance of LCA approaches cradle-to-gate and cradle-to-grave. Since SAP is mainly used for absorption; its reuse or recycling is limited and hence cradle-to-cradle approach for LCA is rare. Capezza et al. [60] conducted an LCA comparison study by replacing commercial SAP in baby diaper by a bio-SAP based on modified wheat gluten. It was reported that during production stage, greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions from bio-SAP were 2.25 times higher than fossil-counterpart. On the other hand, in a cradle-to-grave approach the emission was approximately 1.7 times higher for bio-SAP than that of fossil-based SAP where incineration was the end-of-life scenario. This difference indicates that if the incineration (thermal degradation) had been replaced with compost (biodegradation) the emissions would have been lower. In case of a leave-on application such as bio-SAP in concrete or agricultural applications, it will biodegrade. Meanwhile, for most hygiene or medical applications SAP should be incinerated and hence the life span is shorter. Life span plays an important role in the environmental profiling of bio-SAP. Since trees grow slower, the product life should be high enough so that the time span can minimize the effect of biogenic emission during bio-SAP synthesis. Otherwise, bio-SAP cannot afford an energy input for an end-of-life scenario with respect to sustainability. Hence, selection criteria for the field of application of SAP should include its biodegradability profile. The choice of SAP for any application must consider the volume and lifespan of the product required in the field along with other functional criteria. Bio-based products in long-term applications can offer less pollution and reduced persistence eventually, benefiting the environmental disburden [62, 63]. Hence during the environmental and economic footprint analysis, it is essential to accommodate the future projections in policy changes and technology advances in production, consumerism, and waste management sectors.

Concerns over polyacrylate-based SAP arise due to the toxicity of acrylic acid monomers and the presence of unused initiators and catalysts. Acrylic acid is commercially produced from glycerol, propylene, ethylene oxide, and acetylene. The sources of these raw materials and auxiliary chemicals required in the production process are mostly fossil based. The quest for renewable origin and biodegradability of polyacrylate or polyacrylamide SAP can be achieved by the synthesis of acrylic acid from bio-origin raw materials, replacing certain downstream processes with greener options, and identifying and using certain microorganisms which can degrade these SAP materials. Biotechnology approaches for polyacrylate-based polymers start with the synthesis of the platform chemical acrylic acid. Certain chemicals such as glycerol and 3-hydroxypropionic acid can be catalytically converted to acrylic acid and there are well studied fermentation pathways to convert sugars to 3-hydroxypropionic acid. The fermentative route to acrylic acid synthesis is a comparatively new horizon that is gaining researcher's and industrialist's attention. The use of biocatalysts can reduce the environmental burden not just from the raw materials perspective, it can be beneficial from the emission point of view too. A comparison study on the production of sodium polyacrylate from propylene (synthetic route) and 3-hydroxypropionic acid (fermentation route) by cradle-to-gate approach shows that there is a reduction of 20% in total emissions in bio-based production compared to fossil-based production [64]. In contribution analysis, it was found that in the synthetic route 47% of the emissions were from acrylic acid production stage and in fermentation route 75% of the emissions were contributed from fermentation stage [64]. The important point to note is that in synthetic route the emissions are mainly primary

or direct. But, in the fermentation route, most of the emissions are secondary, and they will be in terms of energy inputs for feedstock processing and maintenance. Hence, it is important to find cheaper alternatives and circularities in bio-SAP systems for better environmental handprint. In addition, particular composite compositions can present the potential to maintain the functionality while reducing the environmental impact. For example, in the case of cellulose-based hydrogel modification as composite with kaolin clay has proven to maintain the functionality and reduce cost [44]. An analysis of environmental footprint of kaolin processing by Memon et al. [65] showed that kaolin mining imparts less environmental impact per kg of kaolin compared to cellulose extraction from sugarcane trash [66]. From a comparison study, it can be said that adding kaolin to the cellulose mixture may reduce the overall footprint of cellulose-kaolin composite SAP production. Thereby, composites present a new horizon to reduce environmental impact without compromising functionality of SAP.

# 4 Conclusion

The availability and low cost of fossil-based synthetic and semi synthetic SAPs can be considered as the reasons for their major share in the global SAP market. However, a detailed study on the long term economic and environmental impact of SAP based products is a need of the hour. Researchers are shifting their focus to biobased SAP to reduce the load on fossil fuels and ensure sustainability. Even though the ecological footprint of bio-SAP can be higher than synthetic ones, in the long run biobased products are an asset. Low yield and compromised physical strength in the applied sector can be viewed as a bottleneck for industrial scale production of bio-SAP. The general trend on the “replacement of fossil fuel-based products” is towards biorefinery from agricultural, food or food processing wastes rather than relying on food crops directly and the SAP sector needs more studies to make SAP production economic. Since functional advancements, economic improvements and environmental sustainability are expected to meet the demand, R&D, Investment approaches, and policy making plays a significant role in SAP design, production, and application. Even though some bio-SAPs provide environmental benefits without compromising functionality, scale up and mechanization of production process require high financial and work force investments which are not feasible in a short span of time. Hence, it is recommended to upgrade the production process gradually by replacing conventional steps with greener options one by one. Invention of new bio-SAP materials is promising in various sectors of application due to their upper hand in being environmentally friendly and circular in the long run.
