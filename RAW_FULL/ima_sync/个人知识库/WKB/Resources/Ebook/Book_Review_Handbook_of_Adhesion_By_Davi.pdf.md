# Nanoscale Assembly

Chemical Techniques. Springer, New York 2005, viii + 244 pp., hardcover, $99.00, ISBN 0-387-23608-2.

[图片: nnNANOSTRLCTURE SCIENCE AND TECHNOLOGYScries Edioe Dexid1.LockoodNanoscaleAssemblyTechniqueEdited bWilhelm T. S. Huck]
To say it upfront, I highly recommend this book to all scientists active in nano-technology research and to anybody seriously interested in the supramolecular-scale organization of soft matter. It is a fascinating read, up to date, and deserves wide dissemination. As part of a quite comprehensive series in Nano-structure Science and Technology, this volume focuses strongly on molecular and polymeric materials and their nano- and micrometer-scale organization via noncovalent interactions. Personally, I find the scope of this book a bit too limited to do justice to the fairly general

and overarching title, and it is perhaps worth pointing out here that it certainly will not serve as an encyclopaedic reference for the chemical assembly of nano-structures. For example, virtually nothing is said about the chemical assembly of metal, semiconductor, and metal-oxide nanostructures, their applications, and the many different methods by which such assemblies can be achieved. These potential shortcomings notwithstanding, the subjects covered are all expertly written up by leading scientists in the field and provide the reader with immediate access to the current state-of-the-art.

The book contains nine independent chapters each generally focusing on the recent achievements of the respective contributors and approximating the style and scope of an Accounts of Chemical Research article. Although there is neither a clear relation between the individual chapters nor a progression in complexity, reading through the book page by page is an inspiring and very educating experience. It is particularly satisfactory to note that all the chapters are written from a truly interdisciplinary point of view, that is, this is more than just another chemistry book. In particular, the subjects covered include polymer nano- and microstructures, self-assembled monolayers (electronic transport and molecular recognition), molecular assembly of nanostructures from solution, molecular electronics, and colloidal crystals. The final chapter by Whitesides and coworkers differs from the others by its generality and breadth of scope. It is a concise and to-the-point summary and rationalization of nanoscale fabrication by using self-assembly. Rather than appearing as an afterthought, it could also have served as an excellent introduction if the book had been conceived to cover the full breadth of the subject.

In summary, one should congratulate the volume editor, Wilhelm Huck, on his selection of topics and contributors. The book will be an inspiration to scientists in the field, and hopefully, further, similarly well-written and put together volumes will appear in the future to

cover the many remaining aspects of chemical nanoscale assembly.

## Handbook of Adhesion

John Wiley & Sons, Chichester, UK 2005, liv + 672 pp., hardcover, $270, ISBN 0-471-80874-1.

[图片: D.E、PACKHAMHANDBOOK OFADHESIONSECOND EDITIONWILEY]
The handbook of adhesion is a comprehensive collection of short articles related to the science and technology of adhesion.

Adhesion describes the common phenomenon of sticking or gluing. It is a truly multiscale phenomenon involving microscopic interactions between surfaces due to the action of molecular forces, as well as macroscopic mechanical properties, such as shear strength, peel strength, and tack. Adhesion and adhesives are relevant in different fields ranging from the basic sciences (physics, chemistry, and biology) to materials science and engineering, as well as medicine. Industrial applications of adhesion are found in composite materials, textiles, printing, paints, and many other areas.

A broad and diverse range of topics related to adhesion are covered in this book, which includes more than 250 articles written by more than 60 experts. The handbook provides easily accessible information for readers from different backgrounds, including technical staff involved in research and development in industry and academia, undergraduate and research students with different backgrounds, and professionals from different disciplines that utilize adhesion as a tool.

The extensively cross-referenced articles comprising the book are focused and self-contained. Overall the book surveys fundamental issues related to

the thermodynamics of adhesion, surface forces, wetting, polymers, fracture mechanics, theories of adhesion, quantitative measurement of adhesion, and adhesive bonding. Aspects of adhesion relevant to the use of adhesives as sealants and mastics, welding and engineering of structures, and adhesion in composite materials, paints, and rubbers are reviewed as well. Special attention is paid to environmental considerations in adhesion technology, sustainability, and the role of renewable sources in adhesion. Overall this handbook is a valuable source of reliable well-presented information. It contains both an alphabetical and a classified list of topics, pro-

viding a key for locating specific information on the one hand, and enabling the reader to obtain an overview of a topic on the other.

This book is highly recommended as a source of reliable, concise information and successfully achieves its declared intention of filling the gap between the simplified treatment offered by student textbooks and the thorough but focused treatment of research monographs and review articles.
