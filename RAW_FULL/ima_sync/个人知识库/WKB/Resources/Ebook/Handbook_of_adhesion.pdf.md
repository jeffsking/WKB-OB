Muiticomponent Polymer Systems. Edited by I. S. Miles & S. Rostami, Longman Scientific and Technical, Harlow, UK. 1992, 435 pp., 0 582 03785 9 (hardback).

This is the first in a Polymer Science and Technology series which aims  to deal with property-structure relationships in a thorough and systematic  way. There are contributions from a number of very notable names in the  field which cover polymer blends, filled polymers, fibre reinforced materials and coatings. There are also chapters on polymer interfaces, block  copolymers, rubber toughened polymers, phase behaviour in ionomers  and the book starts with an overview. It is overall a most comprehensive  volume.

Several chapters have sections and/or make reference to characterisation and testing methods but overall testing is not prominent. Nevertheless  it will be of value to many in the testing community by providing an  understanding of the properties which are required to characterise the  materials and how they may be obtained.

Roger Brown

Handbook of Adhesion. Edited by D. E. Packham. Longman Scientific and  Technical, Harlow, UK. 1992, 570pp. +xli, 0-470-21870-3 (hardback).

This is another book in the Polymer Science and Technology Series. It is  intended as a reference work in the field of adhesion and has the slightly  unusual format of being composed of some 200 articles contributed by 50  or so authors. It is broad in its scope, dealing with aspects of adhesion  relevant to sealants and mastics, paints and coatings, printing and composite materials as well as adhesives, and also includes the background  science.

Book reviews

This would seem an editor's nightmare and to offer a recipe for  framented confusion; as if in anticipation a How to Use the Handbook is  printed inside the front cover. In fact the editor has achieved a remarkably  usable structure; you think of a topic you are interested in, look it up in  the list of articles or the index, and cross references then take you on to  further reading. A classified list of articles gives those covering broad  topics and provides a structured reading list.

One of these is testing and has 21 articles. It was unfortunate that my  dipping into the book instantly found a completely out of date reference  and omission of a test geometry but generally it admirably does what it  intends to do: provide quick answers to a large variety of questions. For  testing people it could be particularly valuable in providing information  on all the other aspects of adhesion effectively on demand.

Roger Brown
