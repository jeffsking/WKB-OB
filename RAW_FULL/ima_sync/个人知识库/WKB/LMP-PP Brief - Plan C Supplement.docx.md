# LMP-PP Resin Development Brief · Supplement: Plan C Technical Key Points

Date: 2026-09-21 Subject: Ultra-low melting point PP resin for the sheath layer of all-PP sheath-core bicomponent fibers (EPC type), melting point ≤135°C

# 1. Positioning of Plan C

Plan C does not develop a low-melt PP single-component fiber. It develops an ultra-low melting point PP resin for the sheath layer of sheath-core bicomponent fibers — a domestic substitute for the low-melt component currently used in ES fiber systems.

In the sheath-core structure, the low-melt sheath is the “glue” and the high-melt core is the “skeleton”: during thermal bonding only the sheath melts, forming polymer fusion bridges at fiber crossover points while the core remains unmelted, preserving fiber morphology and bundle strength. The melting-point differential window between sheath and core is the entire value of the structure (e.g., PE/PP combination: PE sheath melts at 130°C while the PP core at ~165°C stays intact).

Plan C targets the all-PP sheath-core (EPC type): sheath melting point ~140–155°C, core of conventional PP (~165°C). Versus the PE-sheath solution (110–135°C), a PP sheath bonds at higher temperature but is chemically identical to the core with better interfacial compatibility, and the fiber is 100% polypropylene — favorable for all-PP nonwoven recyclability.

# 2. Three Stacked Tm-Suppression Levers (Resin Side)

|Lever|Parameter|Rationale|
|---|---|---|
|Comonomer at upper limit|~10 wt% (ethylene + butene; butene lowers Tm more efficiently)|Comonomers disrupt crystalline perfection; the 0.8–10 wt% range alone drops Tm from 160–165°C to 153–158°C; the upper limit pushes further down|
|Low-isotacticity fraction|5–30 mass% (isotactic pentad 30–60%)|Weakly crystallizing fraction — second Tm lever, also improves low-temperature flow and softness|
|Reverse formulation: no nucleating agent|No sodium benzoate / calcium stearate / sorbitol types (normally 10–2000 ppm for shrinkage reduction)|Nucleating agents accelerate crystallization, refine lamellae and reduce shrinkage — opposite of the low-Tm goal; crystallinity pressed below 60% (drawn-fiber baseline 60–75%); the less perfect the lamellae, the lower the Tm|

# 3. Four Process Pitfalls

1.Xylene solubles runaway (biggest risk): Solubles rise in tandem with low crystallinity, causing tackiness, poor spinning operability and web dimensional instability. Air-through lines run at 100–180°C — if the sheath gets tacky during spinning/web forming, the line cannot run. Hold solubles <6 wt% (copolymer control line): the low-isotacticity fraction must be achieved via catalyst-based blocky incorporation, not random dilution.

2.Insufficient quenching: Low-crystallinity-tendency materials crystallize slowly. Commercial practice is controlled temperature/speed air quenching with the final temperature held between Tg ≈ -10°C and Tg +15°C to maximize drawability; chill roll + intensified quench air recommended, otherwise filament breakage occurs in the drawing zone.

3.Extremely narrow heat-setting window: Setting temperature must be above the stress-relaxation requirement but below the fiber melting point, or the structure collapses. For a 135°C sheath resin the usable window is far narrower than conventional PP; setting temperature shifts down and standard line settings cannot be copied.

4.Re-calibrated draw ratios: The standard two-stage drawing (Stage 1: 110–160°C, 4×–14×; Stage 2: 80–135°C, 1.2×–3×) was designed for homopolymer; the low-melt sheath is already near its softening zone at 110°C, so Stage 1 lower temperature limits and ratios require a fresh DOE.

# 4. Bicomponent Structure as a Buffer (Relieving Resin-Side Difficulty)

•Lower sheath ratio: Commercial sheath-core ratios commonly run 50:50 or 30:70, down to 10:90 at the extreme. Reducing the sheath share (e.g., to 30:70 or below) cuts low-melt resin consumption per kg of fiber and relaxes the Tm requirement — the sheath need not hit 130°C; 145–150°C works as long as a sufficient window to the ~165°C core is maintained.

•Additive zoning: The sheath carries functional additives (e.g., 0.1–0.6 mass% barium sulfate for antistatic) while the core stays high-purity for mechanical load; hydrophilic finishes are applied to the sheath to avoid disturbing core crystallization. The resin development target can thus be relaxed one tier — advancing on both “structure design” and “resin design” legs.

# 5. Acceptance Criteria and Benchmark

|Item|Specification|
|---|---|
|Bonding validation|Air-through line: oven temperature and residence time are the primary variables (typical dwell 3–8 s); insufficient → low strength, excessive → flattened core and harsh hand feel|
|Fiber tenacity|4–6 cN/dtex (standard-grade system)|
|Elongation at break|10–30%|
|Thermal shrinkage|&lt;5% (under tensile stress at 117°C; standard for well-set fibers)|
|Application spec|1.5D–3D fine denier, 38–51 mm cut length, hydrophilic finish (standard hygiene bicomponent staple spec)|

One-line positioning: Plan C is not a “more aggressive Plan B” — it is a different track: from “low-melt PP single-component fiber” to “sheath resin for all-PP sheath-core composite fibers”. The resin alone is the hardest, but risk is manageable when combined with bicomponent structure and sheath-ratio design; it directly matches the raw-material demand of domestic low-melt fiber producers (Yizheng Chemical Fibre, Shanghai Defulun), giving a clear validation path.
