Meeting notes
Pattern Design 
Claire described two leading pattern designs from the pilot machine line: the leaf pattern and 
the Equipment pattern, evaluated using ﬂat testing and U-shaped intake testing
Pattern-to-pattern alignment remained an unresolved follow-up question, alongside open 
questions about fan size, ﬁber porosity, and pressure loss
Claire said parallel jobs needed to use the same size, with the design requiring pad-to-pad 
alignment without white cutoﬀs
Participants discussed whether the embossing should remain consistent across all 11 sizes, 
but no ﬁnal decision was stated
Claire explained that the two-pattern, two-layer direction was driven by business objectives 
and had been aligned with the China leadership team; diﬀerent options and samples had 
been shown to the marketing and leadership teams
Layer Conﬁguration 
The trial using a leaf-pattern top layer with a plain bottom layer did not perform well, so the 
design would continue using two layers with special pattern designs
Participants discussed that the two-layer channel pattern performed better than the one-
layer pattern in the cited testing, with increased void volume proposed as a possible 
explanation. The reason remained under discussion
For phase one, Claire said the team was focusing on a ﬁve-layer material structure using 100% 
SAP, speciﬁcally SAP one and SAP two

Claire explained that the two SAP layers were intended to serve diﬀerent functions: the skin-
side layer required conditioning and distribution, while the second layer supported rapid 
absorption; using the same SAP amount on both sides could sacriﬁce absorbency
Claire explained that the line-side and polish-side SAP layers were expected to serve diﬀerent 
functions: the line-side layer required high AUL, high GBT, and larger particle-size distribution 
for intake, while the polish-side layer required high CR, high Vortex, and relatively smaller 
particle-size distribution to reduce hydrogen-block issues
Claire described an ultimate SAP density split of 70% versus 30%, with approximately 30% of 
the high-AUL SAP placed in the top layer to balance absorbency levels
Claire interpreted the proposed structure as using a higher basis weight of high-CRC SAM in 
the bottom layer and high-AUL SAM in the top layer, rather than the same basis weight in both 
SAM layers
Claire explained that using two SAM delivery systems would enable diﬀerent SAP inputs and 
coverage on each layer
Claire said the process would use two SAP dosing systems plus an additional dosing system 
for functional granules, which could be placed in either the LSS or PSS for some chemicals
Claire explained that front and middle sections of one product could use diﬀerent SAP-head 
volumes, and that the required speed would be calculated from the relevant dimensions, 
RPM, and PLS relationship
Claire described a two-layer formulation in which the ﬁrst layer combined 2D, EDGE, and 3D 
components, while the second layer used functional ﬁbers including BCTMP side-by-side and 
PVA
Claire said the current approach used a tropical liner without ADL coverage and considered 
adding more tri-level ﬁber to increase performance
Claire described a two-layer conﬁguration in which one layer used approximately 10% of the 
discussed material and the other used up to 50%
Product Structure 
The structure was described as ﬁve layers, including two patterned SAM layers matched to 
each other and an uncut Aurelia layer; bonding was considered necessary to preserve 
integrity and prevent the SAM from swelling into the voids
Claire said the initial focus was on using 100% SAP on the poly side, while patterning on the 
other side was being considered for a later phase
Participants agreed that REL cutting was not needed for bonding, although cutting could be 
used later to enable ﬂash-situation distribution
Claire described a redesigned ﬁber structure using 2D and 3D binder components for bonding 
and strength, with hollow ﬁbers intended to improve resilience by acting like springs
Commercialization Timeline

## Page 3

Claire said the commercialization timeline remained 2029. China leadership wanted 
commercialization as soon as possible, but commercialization next year was not considered 
feasible; the team would try for 2028 without committing to it
Claire said the intended product volume was 7,000 products in 2029, while the design speed 
would be higher because the motors could operate at higher speed; she also referenced 20% 
over-banking
Claire said commercialization was being prevented by the need for a second draw
Process Direction 
Claire said the team wanted to decide by the middle of November whether to retroﬁt the 
existing ND33 or install a new pilot machine in the Beijing machine bay
Claire said the team might accept compromises on the data target while focusing on the 
larger pants-boring requirement, and that the 900 PPM target could be modiﬁed after a 
deeper discussion by mid-November
Process Design 
Claire said the process objective was to deliver ﬂexible SAP density ADF placement regardless 
of the absorbent-core pattern or shape
Claire said the process should support diﬀerent SAP densities across zones and allow 
multiple absorbency sections when the material could be segmented accordingly
Claire clariﬁed that patterns should be treated as a special case for areas with speciﬁc SAP 
density requirements; technically, the process should support any designed SAP density at 
any desired location
Claire said the channel pattern needed clear basic geometry with low SAP density, accurate 
pattern alignment and registration, and control of pathway deviations at high speed
Claire described 11 pattern variations using diﬀerent MD lens and CD width conﬁgurations, 
with the designs intended to maintain the same overall density target as the global V6
Participants noted that the equipment discussion was separate from the two-SAM 
development work because the equipment already had two SAM feeders; the feeder 
assignments and a maximum target of 260 still needed to be speciﬁed
Claire clariﬁed that a deﬁned total SAP add-on for each target did not prevent zoning or using 
diﬀerent SAP densities across layers
Claire said the pilot line was intended to provide more ﬂexibility for testing conﬁgurations 
rather than function as a production machine
Claire said the machine could be built to support the proposed two-system conﬁguration, 
while the detailed implementation remained an engineering solution
Claire said engineering needed to determine airﬂow velocity, the appropriate lens and height 
for mixing SAP and ﬂuﬀ, and how SAP particle-size distribution aﬀected the forming process
Claire said the forming fan would generate pressure and vacuum, with forming-bed RPM and 
controlled open air aﬀecting SAP density in speciﬁc pad areas

Claire explained that battery volume aﬀected acid density, and said the 82.5 option had been 
rejected because it was not ﬂexible enough to support diﬀerent MD and CD conﬁgurations
Participants discussed a risk that a single SAP layer could block airﬂow through the screen 
when vacuum was applied only from the bottom
Participants discussed a layered screen design with vacuum applied from the bottom and 
sides of miniature sand pockets as a possible way to address the airﬂow limitation
Claire identiﬁed the relationship between job, drum, and OD sizes as a key process risk: 
diﬀerent sizes could change the nip gap and alignment, while larger jobs could increase 
thermal deformation and COV
Participants discussed that the current adhesive allocation was average, while adding 
adhesive at speciﬁc positions would require a way to adjust the adhesive amount by location
Claire said a trial had been completed the previous weekend and that the drums had been 
cleaned four times; applying adhesive to the drum surface was easy, but it created web-
handling problems
Claire said the proposed job size was only an idea and still needed veriﬁcation by the 
engineering team
Claire described a two-job process for the 11 diﬀerent sizes under consideration
Claire said the channel was designed at 8 millimeters for both the main and side areas, with a 
3-millimeter embossing planned in the middle of the 8-millimeter channel
Participants discussed testing diﬀerent open-air settings to determine whether they produced 
distinct SAP densities, including whether areas with 10% and 15% open air would perform 
diﬀerently
Claire discussed verifying whether diﬀerent SAP densities could be produced using the same 
hole diameter, or alternatively by varying the hole diameters
Claire described a third design path using a concave 3D screen, where gravity combined with 
open-air pressure was expected to help retain more SAP; the detailed design was still pending
Claire discussed a fourth path using a ﬂat screen with diﬀerent open-air settings and a block 
approximately 3 millimeters high around the channel, with embossing at the same time being 
considered
Participants discussed varying the SAM feed rate across diﬀerent zones within one product 
rather than maintaining a continuous feed rate
Participants discussed coordinating SAM feed timing with product or part position, including 
the latency involved when the feed speed is turned on or slowed down
Participants discussed whether vacuum could move the SAM and whether controlling the 
material’s position or bounce-back could reduce the timing needed to receive the SAP
Claire said the SGCH-related process used around 10% ﬂuﬀ and did not require stuﬃng, while 
the alternative process required stuﬃng
Claire identiﬁed maintaining the SAM placement as a major challenge and discussed how 
distribution and proﬁle constraints could aﬀect material retention

would be addressed in phase two
Participants discussed core winding and surface winding as possible approaches for

Claire clariﬁed that the proposed guidelines were intended for process evaluation rather than

Claire described the work as an initial process evaluation using 3D training parts to test

Claire raised engineering concerns about equipment requirements, available space, structural

Absorbency Development 
For phase two, Claire said the team was considering adding a minimum ﬂuﬀ layer to the SCP2 
absorbency layer to enable capillary absorbency, with a target of around 30 GSM or roughly 1 
gram of material
Claire said phase two was expected to consider adding ﬂuﬀ on the poly side to support 
absorbency, while the current process layered materials directly from layer to layer
Claire described the ideal position as combining ﬂuﬀ with SAP, with the possibility of 
increasing the ﬂuﬀ proportion in some countries based on absorbency requirements
A trial planned for next month at KCPX was expected to use two diﬀerent types of SAM across 
multiple conﬁgurations to evaluate the resulting structures
Claire said the process discussion involved evaluating a 10% ﬂuﬀ addition, whereas prior 
additions had been at least 30%
In a comparison with other commercial materials, the tested IO material was reported to 
contain 62% SAP versus approximately 20% in the other materials; participants were still 
evaluating whether the higher SAP content could occupy the RDL void volume when the 
particles swell
Claire said the phase-two work was still waiting for test results from the QA ﬁve-one pipeline
Equipment Procurement 
Claire clariﬁed that purchasing from HCH would provide equipment for a pilot line, not 
technical support, so the team would handle development internally
Claire said equipment selection depended on the production inputs, and that some input 
information would need to be shared with HCH
Participants discussed deﬁning design criteria so vendors could demonstrate the 
equipment’s ability to produce the stated requirements; equipment that met the criteria 
could then be accepted
Claire said the team did not yet know whether the proposed nozzle existed as available 
equipment, and that the issue could be critical because no machine was currently available

Vendor Negotiation

Equipment Design 
Claire said drum runout needed to be evaluated at approximately 150 RPM, noting that heat-

Claire said the two-drum schematic shown was a conceptual theory rather than the actual

Claire discussed 11 sizes with diﬀerent circumferences and widths, including widths of 100,

The required SAM feed and vacuum arrangement remained dependent on the gap between

Participants noted that forming-drum and forming-chamber design would need to account
