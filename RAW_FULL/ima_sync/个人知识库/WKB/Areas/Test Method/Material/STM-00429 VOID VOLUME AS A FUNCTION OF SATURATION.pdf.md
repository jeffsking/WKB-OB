# 1.0 PURPOSE

1.1 This test is used to determine the void volume within a specimen based on the saturation level of that material. This information is necessary in designing absorbent products based on requirements of capacity and permeability that can be calculated using the void volume information.

1.2 Available void volume is space within the test material that is available to fluid insults. A measure of this space provides an estimate of how well the test material will perform as an intake material and how much testing fluid insult the test material can accept.

Note 1: If a requester requires alternate saturation levels, it is the responsibility of the requester to provide these levels. A customized spreadsheet may be needed for testing.

Note 2: If a full product is to be tested, the requester should also indicate if liner and surge materials should be included in the testing or removed prior to testing. The outer cover facing and inner cover film will be removed prior to testing.

Note 3: Requestor should also indicate where the 76 mm circle should be cut from the absorbent. Requestor should consider if some cut locations would have enough absorbent core width to perform the test.

# 1.3 Definitions

## 1.3.1 Intake Material

A material designed to take in fluid from a fluid insult. The material should have sufficient permeability, capillary tension, or absorbent rate and capacity to hold or absorb fluid.

For this test, the material may be a spunmelt body side liner, a bonded carded web surge material to temporarily handle a fluid, a wrapsheet around the absorbent core, or combinations of above. If intake materials are part of the specimens, the requestor should percent composition and

component density for of the intake materials to obtain a more accurate void volume calculation.

## 1.3.2 Superabsorbent

A particulate or fibrous material generally having absorbent capacity above 15 g/g.

## 1.3.3 Pulp

A fibrous absorbent material usually mixed with the superabsorbent to form an absorbent core. Usually wood fluff pulp, but could be rayon, cotton, or other absorbent fibers.

## 1.3.4 Binder

Not usually present in airformed absorbent cores made of superabsorbent and pulp.

For airlaid materials, the bicomponent fiber added to give the absorbent core dry and wet integrity.

For absorbent cores at nearly 100% superabsorbent content, the adhesive binding the superabsorbent in the absorbent core.

## 1.3.5 Saturation level

In this test, saturation level is defined as fluid amount in sample/mass of dry sample (g/g).

## 1.3.6 Void Volume

The portion of the material volume that contains air. (Void Volume = Total material volume - Volume of solids.)

# 3.0 TEST SPECIMEN PREPARATION

Note 20: The test material supplied by the requester should be identified by code number.

Note 21: The requester may require a different number of specimens to be tested. Cut the specimens as directed by the requester.

Note 22: The material should be measured after AMBIENT conditions have been met.

3.1 Cut 18 specimens, 76 mm (three-inch) diameter, from each code of the test material.

Note 23: Eighteen specimens equal one set.

Note 24: Specimens of each code of test material should be kept separate prior to testing.

3.2 Remove inner and outer cover from each specimen with minimal disturbing of the absorbent core. Use freeze spray as needed and if allowed.

3.3 If requested, remove liner and surge from each specimen with minimal disturbing of the absorbent core. Use freeze spray as needed and if allowed.

3.4 Set up 18 petri dishes at 80-84 mm diameter.

Note 25: Prior to testing, the petri dishes being used must be measured to ensure the diameter is 80 – 84 mm. If the diameter of the dishes does not fall within this parameter, inaccurate testing results may be obtained.

# 4.0 PROCEDURE

4.1 Press the RESET button on the thickness tester gauge to zero the gauge

4.2 Measure the thickness of the petri dish. Immediately record the thickness of the petri dish to the nearest 0.01 mm in the appropriate cell on the spreadsheet.

Note 26: To ensure a true 0.00 thickness reading, before zeroing the thickness tester gauge and before taking each thickness measurement, make sure all SAM and fluff are removed from underneath the thickness tester platen and the surface of the thickness tester.

4.3 Tare the balance.

4.4 Place a specimen on the balance and weigh it to the nearest 0.01 gram. Record the dry weight of the specimen in the appropriate cell on the spreadsheet.

4.5 Place the weighed specimen in the petri dish.

Note 27: The petri dishes should be used in numerical order; e.g., 1, first; 2, second.

4.6 Press the RESET button on the thickness tester gauge to zero the gauge.

4.7 Place a petri dish with a specimen on the base of the thickness tester. Center the petri dish with the specimen below the platen.

Note 28: To ensure a true 0.00 thickness reading, before zeroing the thickness tester gauge and before taking each thickness measurement, make sure all SAM and fluff are removed from underneath the thickness tester platen and the surface of the thickness tester.

4.8 Lower the platen.

4.9 Immediately record the combined thickness value of the petri dish and the specimen to the nearest 0.01 mm in the appropriate cell on the spreadsheet.

4.10 Remove the petri dish with the specimen from the base of the thickness tester and set it aside.

Note 29: The petri dishes with the specimens should be set aside in the order in which they were tested.

4.11 Repeat 4.1 – 4.10 for each specimen in the set.

4.12 Place the petri dish containing the specimen that was tested first on the balance.

4.13 Tare the balance.

4.14 With the petri dish and specimen on the balance, insult the specimen with the required amount of testing fluid as specified in the void volume spreadsheet ± 0.2 gram.

Note 30: Ensure the insulting is performed uniformly over the surface of the specimen.

|[图片]  Kimberly-Clark|VOID VOLUME AS A FUNCTION OF SATURATION|||
|---|---|---|---|
|Document#:|STM-00429|Revision:|2|

Note 31: A 60 ± 5-second interval should be maintained between the insulting of each specimen.

Note 32: The specimens must be insulted to accuracy as quickly as possible or a time conflict may result between insulting specimens and taking thickness readings.

Note 33: When viewing the spreadsheet, the amount of testing fluid required for each insult will appear in blue type under the column heading "Calculated Weight of Fluid to Add".

Note 34: It may be necessary to use both the pipette and the finnpipette to obtain the appropriate insult amount.

4.15 Immediately start the timing device tracking the specimen for a minimum 20-minute wait time, not to exceed 21 minutes. Simultaneously start a second timing device for a 60 ± 5-second interval.

4.16 Record the amount of fluid insulted to the nearest 0.01 gram in the appropriate cell on the spreadsheet.

4.17 Remove the petri dish containing the insulted specimen from the balance and set it aside.

Note 35: The petri dishes containing the insulted specimens should be set aside in the order in which they were insulted.

4.18 Repeat 4.13 – 4.17 for each remaining specimen in the set.

Note 36: To ensure a true 0.00 thickness reading, before zeroing the thickness tester gauge and before taking each thickness measurement, make sure all SAM and fluff are removed from underneath the thickness tester platen and the surface of the thickness tester.

4.19 After the wait time has elapsed, press the RESET button on the thickness tester gauge to zero the gauge.

4.20 Place the petri dish containing the specimen that was insulted first on the base of the thickness tester. Center the petri dish containing the specimen below the platen.

4.21 Lower the platen.

4.22 Immediately record the combined thickness value of the petri dish and the insulted specimen to the nearest 0.01 mm in the appropriate cell on the spreadsheet.

Note 37: A 60 ± 5-second interval should be maintained between taking the thickness measurement of each specimen.

4.23 Remove the petri dish containing the insulted specimen from the balance.

Note 38: Ensure the thickness tester platen is dry.

4.24 Place the next successive specimen on the base of the thickness tester. Repeat 4.19 - 4.23 for each remaining insulted specimen in the set.

# 5.0 RESULTS

Note 39: The date of testing should be included with the data.

Note 40: Modifications to the test method should be recorded.

Note 41: Warning: Specimens that are tested under different conditions may not be directly comparable.

5.1 Report the testing information listed in 3.2.3.

5.2 Report the thickness of the petri dish “Dish Thickness” to the nearest 0.01 millimeter.

5.3 Report the dry weight of the specimen "Specimen Dry Weight" to the nearest 0.01 gram.

5.4 Report the combined thickness value of the petri dish and the dry specimen “Dry Thickness (Specimen and Dish)” to the nearest 0.01 millimeter.

5.5 Report the amount of fluid insulted onto the specimen "Fluid Added Amount" to the nearest 0.01 gram.

5.6 Report the combined thickness value of the petri dish and the insulted specimen “Wet Thickness (Specimen and Dish)” to the nearest 0.01 millimeter.
