# ABSTRACT

This test method measures the compression load/ thickness curve of absorbent materials in either the wet or dry state. The generated data is designed to be used in both absorbent and mechanical modeling of K-C products and sub-systems.

This method is designed for research purposes and is therefore very flexible in regards to test parameters. The user of this method will need to specify the tensile machine crosshead speeds (typical values are one cm per minute), Maximum compression stress levels (typical values are 10 to 20 kPa), specimen diameter and thickness, data acquisition rates, wetting amount and specific procedures, number of cycles, and, optionally platen sizes and surfaces. Output consists of the compression load/thickness points comprising the specimen compression curve. Additional summary statistics are neither calculated nor provided. Data analysis methods are independent of this test method and are not supplied here.

1.3 Upper compression platen; Typically 3.5 inch diameter, capable of handling the chosen compression stress without measurable deflection.

The surface of the bottom of the cup and the base of the cylinder should be flat and parallel to better than 0.005 inches, and preferably, to better than 0.002 inches.

1.7 Large syringe(s) to with draw excess fluid from testing cell after swelling.

1.8 Miscellaneous beakers, etc, for handling fluid

1.9 Appropriate wetting fluid as specified by the requester (typically 0.9 to 2.0 weight percent saline although the test has been run with saline concentrations as high as 20 percent).

# 3.0 SAMPLE PREPARATION

3.1 Cut a circular specimen, typically using a 1.88 inch circular die. Specimens should generally be taken from areas of a material that do not have folds, tears, wrinkles or other defects unless other sampling is specifically requested.

3.2 Wetting (optional).Follow procedures including wetting procedure and waiting times specified by the requestor.

3.3 Record specimen weights and thicknesses as needed or as specified by the requestor.

# 4.0 SETUP PROCEDURE

4.1 Follow the instructions for calibrating the load cell for the tensile tester being used.

4.2 Apply upper and lower platens to the tensile frame and set surfaces to be parallel using the appropriate adjustment techniques. (Normally, the lower platen is free-floated and

then the upper platen is carefully brought into contact at maximum load cell force. The lower platen is then position locked.)

4.3 Set the appropriate gage length on the tensile frame. (typically 3 cm for wet specimens and 1.5 cm for dry specimens)

4.4 Set the tensile frame load and position safety limits to values that just exceed test requirements.

# 5.0 TEST PROCEDURE

5.1 If doing wet testing, place the sample cell containing specimen onto the lower tensile frame platen. The samples cell should be approximately centered on the lower platen and the specimen should be approximately centered in the cell. If only testing specimens dry, the testing cell may be omitted and the specimen may be placed directly onto the platen. Centering is still required.

5.2 Start the test. Typically three cycles of compression and retraction data will be automatically collected.

5.3 Save the raw data in a text file form using an appropriate file name.

5.4 Export the raw data to the requestor.

# 6.0 PRECISION AND ACCURACY

6.1 Due to the nature of the measurements obtained, this test is not subject to accuracy evaluation.
