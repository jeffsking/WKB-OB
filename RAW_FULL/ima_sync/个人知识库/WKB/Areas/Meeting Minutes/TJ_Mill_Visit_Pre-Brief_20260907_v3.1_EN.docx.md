# 2. Module 1: 09:30 GRID Project Review (GRID = Topical Liner × Kirigami Surge)

# 2.1 Where GRID Sits in the Product Architecture

•KC Open Diaper architecture: T4 and below use a Surge/Liner two-piece set; T5 and above upgrade to Support Layer / Topical liner (3-Piece Liner); the line includes a Double Turn Bar for T5 (Topical liner) station.

•SOAR4 (waist elastification + topical liner) is a T5+ configuration; same tier has New 3D Emboss + Apertured Liner (all sizes).

•In the Intake Material Development Pathway, GRID* (asterisk = in development) is listed under the Surge 3D/Apertured branch alongside Textor AC4, Naxos, CleanShield and DEFENDER.

# 2.2 Kirigami Surge: the Linked Technology

Project positioning (KC R&amp;D project page): - Drivers: consumer needs for better urine/runny-BM management and skin dryness + improved breathability + rising POME Topical Surge cost — the need for a more cost-effective alternative. Kirigami was initiated as a cost-down alternative to POME topical surge. - Mechanism: cut-&amp;-stretch (kirigami) forming semi-circular apertures and micro-topography (physical sample: SemiCircle 10 mm), creating 3D channels — versus flat materials’ smooth surface, few liquid channels, slow distribution, high rewet risk and low breathability. - Value proposition: without adding material basis weight, better liquid management, breathability and dryness; good consumer acceptance; potential to reduce dependence on POME Topical Surge.

Maturity &amp; roadmap position: - TRL ladder: KIRIGAMI Surge (GRE) at TRL 3–4; peers: U-Bonder, 3D Sensation, 3D Bico (GNW); CLEANSHIELD at TRL 5+. - 3D Liner/Surge supplier sample library: KIRIGAMI listed alongside CleanShield, YanJan Naxos/Diamonds/Wave, Berry Apertured, Shalag Hexagon, Avgol Waveform. - 2026 IPC BCC FEI: Kirigami and laser cutting listed as the two solutions for waistband breathability; claim space TBD. - Product calendar: COCO V7 KIRIGAMI; also a “Kirigami Electric Nano Lin” entry. - FemCare line: Responsive Dri Fit / Kirigami Stretch (2025+). - Internal ideation: Kirigami apertured material shortlisted as a “targeted through-flow” visual cue candidate (combined with colored underlayer for visual differentiation).

# 2.3 Topical 3D Evolution Background

Flat Liners (SMS/SB/BCW) → 3D Visual Cues (texture/apertures) → 3D Skin Health (CleanShield BM-off-skin; actives — ZEN, BITS etc.; dryness TBD) → 3D Enhanced Cues (TBD: printing on non-skin-facing side for depth perception).

Innovation requirements (official): - Near term: drier 3D liner &amp; topical surge (urine scenario), target rewet 0–0.3 g; applications Active Baby, POISE Ultra Thins. - Long term: printing solutions for 3D liner &amp; topical surge; barriers: high-speed print quality, ink build-up, one-side visibility. - Core question from the deck: “Do we need liner &amp; topical surge innovation to also achieve superior protection beyond perception?”

# 2.4 BM Control Mechanism

Topical liner design core = apertures + underlying void: no apertures/void → BM pools on top; apertures + void → BM passes into the lower layer, cleaner skin (validated with CleanShield mannequin, 30 ml liquid BM).

# 2.5 Key Risks

1.Apertures/channels are fluid reservoirs: high-suction apertured networks (40–50 kPa) that lose to the lower acquisition layer store fluid in the liner and rewet under compression; margin narrows as the core approaches saturation.

2.Cooling/wetness paradox: cooling approaches on liners/surge may increase perceived wetness (known concern in the 2022 Intake Strategy).

3.Basis-weight down-gauging risk: UBK Maxi / POISE UT liner planned down to 18 gsm; diaper liners below 12 gsm raise GOS risk — the interaction of cut-&amp;-stretch ratio with basis weight must be asked.

4.Switch economics under POME cost pressure: whether the Kirigami raw-material + processing cost vs. POME total-cost comparison is closed.

# 3.2 Coform Technology Status

•Forming principle: “Sheet formed by mixing air streams containing pulp and polymer” — pulp and polymer streams blended by air.

•Structure: TripleClean™ = meltblown / pulp / meltblown sandwich, embossed after forming.

•Functional division — the one-liner: polymer fibers provide wet strength, pulp fibers provide absorbency.

•Per-sheet material use: pulp 1.34 g (~2 million fibers) + PP 0.5 g (~35 miles of fiber); sheet dry weight 1.84 g; measured pulp share 72.8% (above the nominal 2/3).

•Global scale: 10 coform lines / 20 banks / ~40 billion sheets per year; implying ~53.6 kt/yr pulp and ~20 kt/yr PP.

# 4. Module 3: 13:00 Gasket Process Deep-Dive

Scope correction: gasket (垫片) = leak-barrier gasket, referring to waist/leg/flap gaskets and gasketing effectiveness. Original pad-forming prep is in the appendix.

# 4.1 Where Gasketing Sits in Urine Management

KC urine-leak management five pillars: Target Zone Capacity → Intake at Rate of Insult → Free Fluid Control → Bucket Volume → Gasketing (effective barrier system).

Definition: Gasketing = material properties, placement, and attachment to other components, such that uncontrolled urine is prevented from escaping via least-resistance paths (tunnels, channels, holes, gutters, seams). Concretely the waist / leg / flap gaskets.

# 4.2 Test Method System

•Gap Test (fluid column height): measures the height of the fluid column a gasketing element holds before failure; output = column height; use = evaluating gasketing design changes and surface-tension effects.

# •Clear mannequin: identify failure modes.

# •Flap hydrohead: flap hydrostatic resistance.

# •Outercover pinhole test: outer-cover pinhole leakage.

# •MOCON: breathability.

•SAM poke-through: SAM puncture resistance (within the gasketing effectiveness test pack).

Test fluid — key point: 0.9% NaCl banked saline + 0.1% sodium taurocholate (STCH) to bring static/dynamic surface tension to urine levels — STCH is mandatory for gasket-containing tests (TL-16147).

# 4.3 Typical Failure Modes (KC internal naming)

# •Flow Underneath: flow along the underside of the skin-facing layer.

•Tunnel Leakage: channel leakage — “No adhesive to stop fluid flow”; missing adhesive is the root cause.

# 4.4 Elastomer &amp; Patent Side

•Leg gasket-cuff elastic-zone design can reference patent JP07308341: curved elastic zone (polyurethane foam / Spandex elastic sheet bonded to the gasket-cuff).

•Waist elastification (SOAR4, T5+) and its interface with the gasket is a T5 upgrade item.

# 4.5 Discussion Points

1.Current TJ-line leg / waist / flap gasket elastomer selection (elastic spec, spandex/elastic sheet) and adhesive application paths and bonding parameters.

2.Failure-mode distribution: Flow Underneath vs. Tunnel Leakage split? Gap Test fluid-column baseline?

3.Missing adhesive (Tunnel Leakage root cause): spray glue vs. ultrasonic, glue-amount monitoring, in-line inspection.

4.Whether gasket/leak tests use STCH-spiked standard fluid; frequency of clear-mannequin failure analysis.

5.Cross-topic (link to morning GRID): if Kirigami/GRID apertured zones extend toward leg- or waist-gasket areas, do they create new least-resistance paths? Capillary channeling at aperture edges vs. gasketing.

6.SAM poke-through: compression concentration in gasket zones — SAM and underlayer puncture performance.

# Appendix: Pad Forming Backup (if the gasket session means shims / core forming)

# A.1 Acceptance targets

High pad integrity / low SAM damage / correct basis weight (incl. zoned pads) / low CoV / no clumps or hard spots.

# A.2 Fiberization equipment capacity

# •PCMC / Williams hammermill: 4,000 lbs/hr

# •JOA fiberizer (recommended standard): 3,000 lbs/hr

# •Kamas defibrator: 2,200 lbs/hr

# •JOA disc mill: 500 lbs/hr

1 g of fluff pulp ≈ 4 million fibers.

# A.3 DBK module trade-off

Smaller DBK gap (center) → higher top-roll pressure → better pad integrity, but worse softness and higher SAM crush risk; gap set by shims at center/side; new felt roll improves DBK gluing. Integrity / softness / SAM integrity — pick two.

# A.4 SAM dosing

Pneumatic conveying (4,500 ft/min; compact footprint but damages SAM) vs. gravity drop (SAM-friendly but occupies mezzanine space).

# A.5 FPC cutting (1,000 DPM example)

Cutter line speed 500 m/min; XXL pad 420 mm / 420 m/min; Knife Roll 6.27 in / Anvil Roll 12.48 in; MAX pad design length must be shorter than the anvil arc length.
