TBCW material variability:


Selected material: TBCW 33 gsm


TBCW shows GSM fluctuation, with differences between MD and CD directions


The operations team adjusts online based on stretchability


Strategy: one-step stretching is not feasible; a two-step (or multi-step) stretch with online tension monitoring is required. E.g., for a 10% total stretch target, the first step applies only 3–4%


Open area correlates positively with stretch ratio: higher ratio = larger crescent (half-moon) open area, but too high causes breakage


Consumer visibility:


The open area must be large enough for consumers to perceive it (visual selling point)


Openings must be large but must not become through-holes, which consumers would reject


R&D is responsible for enlarging the open area, which is not easy to achieve initially


Process confidentiality:


The pattern design is highly confidential, known only within R&D and process personnel


Key management principle: even starting from FEI, the design rationale is never explained to the engineering team — only requirements are communicated. This preserves the engineering team's innovation space and avoids bias; R&D makes the decisions at the FEI stage


Internal side discussion (China team): the engineering department head SH is a long-time friend of over ten years. Design dimensions can be given directly to engineering — if they have the capability to modify, let them do it — but R&D should still control the details. Transparency from BS is high (~80%)


Ultrasonic vs. adhesive — technical rationale:


Adhesive was dropped after the second trial; the ultrasonic option was retained


Reasons: adhesive is difficult to apply in very slim lines; ultrasonic LSE builds strength while cutting, ensuring even line strength in the CD direction


After stretching opens the holes, build-up occurs on the tooling roll; the ultrasonic approach tolerates build-up issues better


Improvement plan milestones relate to September 23rd
