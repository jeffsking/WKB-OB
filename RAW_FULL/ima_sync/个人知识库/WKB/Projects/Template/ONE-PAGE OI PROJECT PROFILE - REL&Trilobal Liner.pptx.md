STATUS & CHALLENGE
Situation: the liner is bought from Yanjan at a high unit price — no answer for cost-sensitive markets. Round fibres touch only at points, so liquid moves by pore capillarity and pools at the impact point.
Hypothesis: trilobal grooves form axial channels that align with MD after carding → liquid is drawn along the machine direction instead of stacking up.
Trial verdict: hypothesis holds on spread — Cradle spread +17.9% vs the same core with the standard liner, above the +15% design target. Price: GB rewet doubles and lateral intake slows ~33%.

ANALYSIS · WHAT THE DATA SAYS
①  Mechanism works: Cradle spread 205 → 241.6 mm (t = 15.9), clearing the +15% target; L-H +5.5% (t = 4.0) points the same way but weaker.
②  Bigger win is capacity, not speed: 2nd / 3rd-shot intake +36% / +35% at equal pad weight.
③  Two costs: GB rewet 0.49 → 0.99 g (+101%) and lateral intake 7.8 → 10.4 s (+33%); 1st STT +3.3% is n.s. Rewet is the gate.
RISKS & NEXT GATE
DESIGN / PERFORMANCE
· Rewet now the binding constraint: GB rewet +101% while spread improves — must be traded explicitly
· Higher trilobal share → interlayer bonding strength still unknown (top technical gap)
· Skin-side trilobal raises hand harshness; worse with homo-PET
PROCESS / EQUIPMENT
· ≥142 °C rounds off the profile → trilobal "added for nothing"; trials held below 139 °C
· Pressure bonding does not work; no LSE — linear horn ≈US$70k, 500 m/min but needs floor space
· HK copy of the Herrmann module: power compatible, voltage/frequency differ → horn life at risk
SUPPLY / CONVERTING

· PET-containing liner acts as unbonded filler in ultrasonic seal lanes → strength drops without warning (HIGH)
