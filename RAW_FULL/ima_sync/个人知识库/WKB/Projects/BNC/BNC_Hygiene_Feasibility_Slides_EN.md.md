# BNC for Hygiene Products — Technical Feasibility Deck

Slide-ready outline. One section = one slide. Import into PPT template or paste slide by slide.

## Slide 1 — Title

Bacterial Nanocellulose (BNC) in Disposable Hygiene Products
Technical Feasibility Assessment

Prepared for: Pre-visit Technical Due Diligence — Hainan Bai Kerui BNC Co., Ltd.
Date: 2026-08-24
Scope: Baby diapers & wet wipes material applications

## Slide 2 — Executive Summary

Four application entry points, ranked by feasibility:

- Wet wipes / premium topsheet substrate — High technical feasibility; first pilot target
- Aerogel core / ADL reinforcement — Medium-high; drying cost is the decisive variable
- BNC-grafted biodegradable SAP — Medium (lab-proven only); long-term option
- Fluff pulp replacement — Low / NOT recommended — fermentation economics cannot match pulp scale
Bottom line: BNC is not a bulk-material substitute. Its value is premium differentiation — purity, skin-friendliness, biodegradability.

## Slide 3 — What BNC Actually Is

Source: Absorbent Technology (Elsevier, 2002), Ch. 12

- Produced by Acetobacter xylinum (also Rhizobium, Agrobacterium) on simple glucose media
- Extruded as organized ribbons: 40–60 nm wide, built of 50–80 microfibrils of 3–4 nm
- Pure Cellulose I, high molecular weight — no lignin, no hemicellulose
- Ribbons entangle into a continuous nano-network (pellicle film) — not discrete fibers
- Textbook verdict: "of potential industrial importance" — ranked #1 biotech route for absorbent products
## Slide 4 — Performance Implications

What the nano-structure gives us — and what it does not:

- Purity: no bleaching residuals → low-irritation claims, "zero chemical treatment" positioning
- Nano-network: huge specific surface area → water holding & film forming strength
- High crystallinity: high modulus — but hydrophilic, NOT superabsorbent
- Native cellulose retains water by hydrogen bonding + micro-capillarity (< 0.02 μm pores), max ~15% moisture at 90% RH
- Key gap vs SAP: adsorption mechanism ≠ osmotic swelling. Do not buy the "superabsorbent" pitch
## Slide 5 — Reference Product Architecture

Standard diaper structure (EDANA AHPs White Paper, 2022):

- Topsheet — PP/PE nonwoven, skin contact, optional lotion
- ADL — modified cellulose + polyester; rapid acquisition & lateral distribution
- Core — fluff pulp + SAP; pulp for fast wicking, SAP for retention under load
- Backsheet — breathable PE film
- Core blend typically 80/20 – 90/10 cellulose/SAP
## Slide 6 — Entry Point 1: Wet Wipes / Premium Topsheet (BEST FIT)

- Direct skin-contact layer demands: soft, low irritation, low transfer
- BNC fit: pure Cellulose I + biodegradability → flushable/compostable trend (target 3–12 months composting)
- Wet-strength paths are textbook-mature:

- HDI/IPDI emulsion impregnation — 0.3–1.5% add-on, 100–130 °C cure, 40–60% wet strength retention
- E-beam crosslinking — 10–50 kGy, +30–60% wet strength, no chemical residue (baby/medical grade)
- Risks: cost gap vs ES/spunlace; possible "clammy" feel in hot-humid climate — must field test
## Slide 7 — Entry Point 2: Aerogel Core / ADL Reinforcement

- BNC aerogel = high-porosity, high-surface-area lightweight structure
- Drying route — not formulation — determines nano-structure (Industrial Drying Handbook data):

- Same formula, oven-dried vs ethanol-exchanged + supercritical dried: particle size ×5, surface area ×23 (4.88 vs 113.8 m²/g)
- Three process routes:

- Freeze drying — preserves pores; high energy, low throughput
- Solvent exchange + supercritical CO₂ (Tc 31 °C / Pc 7.29 MPa) — zero capillary stress, best quality, high CAPEX
- Solvent exchange + surface modification, ambient drying — cheapest, but TMCS-type agents not acceptable for baby products
- BNC pellicle is ~99% water — dehydration is THE cost bottleneck for every application route
## Slide 8 — Entry Point 3: BNC-Grafted Biodegradable SAP (Long-term Only)

Lab evidence is solid, industrialization is not:

- Acrylic-grafted cellulose: 1,125 g/g (DI water); HEC-g-pPAM up to 3,000 g/g (DI water)
- Biodegradation: 46.7% mass loss, 150 days in 20%-moisture soil
- Fatal flaw — salt sensitivity: ~100 g/g in 1% NaCl; urine is ~0.9% saline
- Textbook judgment: cellulose grafting "has not been practiced on an industrial scale except in isolated cases"
- Realistic positioning: partial swap of 10–20% SAP for a "compostable core" claim on premium lines
## Slide 9 — Entry Point 4: Fluff Pulp Replacement — REJECTED

- Fluff pulp value = global scale (~24 Mt/yr supply) + cost + long-fiber resilience/loft
- Fermentation BNC cannot match pulp economics at any reactor configuration
- BNC network lacks long-fiber bulk — core loft relies on fiber stiffness and curl
- Only exception: localized functional zones (e.g., male front-dribble pad) as composite reinforcement
- Do NOT open a project on this
## Slide 10 — Safety & Compliance Checklist

- Skin-contact exposure: EDANA conservative transfer factor — 7% for diapers, higher for wipes; migration assessment required even for "natural" BNC
- Nanoparticle dust: inhalation/dermal exposure control required in fermentation & refining steps
- Purity as claim: no ECF-bleaching residuals, no hemicellulose degradation products — direct marketing asset
## Slide 11 — Due Diligence Question List (Ask at Bai Kerui)

Capacity & Cost (Procurement — Priority 1):

- Fermentation capacity — static trays vs stirred/airlift reactors; expansion plan
- Price basis — wet pellicle vs dry powder, per-ton and per-gram pricing, yield
- Carbon source structure — glucose vs molasses; local Hainan sugar-industry byproduct advantage?
- Dehydration/drying route — oven / freeze / solvent exchange / supercritical?
- Batch consistency — CV of pellicle thickness, crystallinity, MW
Application Development (R&D):

- BNC re-dispersion into nonwoven/pulp slurry — precedents & data
- BNC-SAP composite experience (aerogel carrier / fine-particle fixation)
- Medical/food-grade certifications — reusable biocompatibility data package?
- Modification capability — carboxymethylation / esterification / grafting
- Existing downstream customers & case studies (food film? wound dressing? cosmetics?)
IP: 11. Own patents (strain, process, application) + FTO disclosure

## Slide 12 — Recommended Roadmap

- Short term (1 month post-visit): sample wet-wipe substrate trials (30–60 g/m² basis weight) + wet-strength validation (HDI vs E-beam) + skin patch pre-screening; request cost breakdown quote
- Mid term (3–6 months): if cost acceptable — BNC aerogel ADL pilot (freeze-dried lab samples), map basis-weight vs performance curve
- Long term (12 months+): BNC-grafted SAP joint development — partner provides modification capability, we provide application validation
- Red line: no "replace fluff pulp / SAP" projects — neither mechanism nor cost structure supports it
## Slide 13 — Information Boundaries

This assessment is built on:

- Absorbent Technology (Elsevier, 2002) — BNC chapter data is 20+ years old
- EDANA AHPs White Paper (2022)

- Industrial Drying Handbook analysis
Not covered by current knowledge base: BNC industrialization progress of the last 20 years; Bai Kerui's actual capacity, pricing, certifications — all captured in the Slide 11 question list for on-site verification.

## Slide 14 — Closing: Three Things to Remember

- BNC = purity + nano-network, not superabsorbency — evaluate on its true strengths
- The single most valuable question at the visit: what is your drying route?
- Commercial logic: premium differentiation (wipes → ADL → degradable SAP), never bulk replacement
