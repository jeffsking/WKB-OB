THE GOVERNING PHYSICS

# Two capillary paths in parallel — and why the slow one wins late

### Groove capillary pressure

$$ \Delta p = 2 \gamma \cos \theta / r_{\text{groove}} $$

$\bullet$ r_groove ≈ 2-3 µm for a 3.3 dtex trilobal, against 40-80 µm for the inter-fibre pore.

$\bullet$ With a wetting finish ($\theta \to 20^\circ$) and $y \approx 66$ mN/m, groove suction lands near 40–50 kPa versus roughly 1–2 kPa in the pore network.

### Wicking front

$$L(t)=\sqrt{(\gamma r\cos\theta\cdot t/ 2\mu)}$$

$\bullet$ Big pores move the front fast but cannot hold it — they drain to the core first.

$\bullet$ Grooves start slower, then carry the front on alone. That late-stage tail is exactly the MD length you are trying to buy.

CONSTRAINT MAP

# Four things that break if you simply swap fibre

## 1 Bond density

Mono PET carries no low-melt sheath. Every bond point in the web comes from the ES fraction, and PE-to-PET adhesion is only about half of PE-to-PE.

Bond count falls faster than the ES fraction does.

## 3 CD tensile

MD alignment on the card is what buys MD wicking — and it is also what removes CD-oriented fibre spans.

The two objectives pull against each other on one drum.

## 2 Fibre stiffness

Bending stiffness scales with E·d$^{4}$. PET modulus is roughly 10× PE, and the trilobal lobes push mass away from the neutral axis.

A trilobal PET fibre at the skin is felt, not just measured.

## 4 Converting

PET is abrasive, static-prone, and does not soften at PP/PE ultrasonic settings.

Failure shows up at the slitter and the seal, not the unwind.

DESIGN PRINCIPLE

# Decouple the functions across the two cards

The MD/CD conflict is only unavoidable if both cards do the same job. They do not have to.

SOFTNESS

# Why the skin stratum stays 100% ES

## Bending stiffness $\propto$ E $\cdot$ $d^4$

$\bullet$ PE sheath modulus ≈ 0.3 GPa; PET ≈ 4 GPa — more than a decade of difference.

$\bullet$ Trilobal lobes sit far from the neutral axis, adding a further 1.3–1.6× at equal denier.

$\bullet$ Lobe tips make point contacts on skin — a prickle risk no hand test will catch.

## Mitigations built into Option B

$\bullet$ Zero PET in A1 — the contact surface is chemically identical to today.

$\bullet$ Hold caliper: do not let oven compensation over-bond and collapse loft.

$\bullet$ Keep A2 PET at 2.2-3.3 dtex so any fibre migrating upward is a fine one.

CONVERTING AT 600 M/MIN

# Five failure modes, and what to do about each

[图片: HIGH]
### Ultrasonic seals

PET does not soften at PP/PE ultrasonic settings. In a cuff or side-seam lane it behaves as unbonded filler and seal strength drops without warning.

Verify seal strength before anything else. If the liner crosses an ultrasonic lane, keep PET out of that lane or move to thermal or adhesive bonding there.

[图片: MED]
### Edge tear & CD strain

Reduced CD tensile shows up as edge nicks propagating at spreader bars and bowed rolls, not as a web break at the unwind.

Reduce wrap angles, keep unwind tension at or below 2.0 N/cm, run tapered tension, inspect slit edges at every roll change during trials.

[图片: MED]
### Lint and fuzz

A higher mono-fibre fraction plus faster blade wear means more free fibre ends at the folding boards and glue applicators.

Add vacuum lint extraction at the slitter and ahead of the glue station; track
applicator fouling interval as a trial metric.

[图片: MED]
### Static

PET tribocharges far more than PE at 600 m/min — web flutter, misregistration and fibre fly around core placement.

Static bars before the glue applicator and before core placement; specify antistat in the PET spin finish.

[图片: LOW]
## Adhesive bonding

PET surface energy (about 43 mN/m) sits above PE (about 31), so hot-melt wet-out and green strength should improve slightly.

No action expected — confirm peel strength in the trial and take the upside.

The ultrasonic item will not appear in any lab test of the nonwoven — it only shows up on the converting line, at speed, as a seal failure.

THE COUNTER-RISK

# High-suction grooves can hold fluid you wanted in the core

### The mechanism

A groove network at 40–50 kPa suction is a genuine reservoir. If it out-competes the layer beneath it, fluid stays in the liner instead of reaching the core — and returns under load as rewet. More PET is not monotonically better.

### Why Option B stays safe

A swollen SAP core develops suction well above the groove range, so the core still wins the tug-of-war. That margin narrows as the core approaches saturation — which is exactly the third-insult condition to test.

COMMERCIAL VIEW

# A performance play — but not a cost penalty

ES bico 2.2 dtex

$2.30-2.60 /kg

ES bico 4.4 dtex

$2.10-2.40 /kg

Trilobal mono PET

$1.55-1.95 /kg

1. Material delta

Roughly -$0.006 to -$0.008 per m$^{2}$ of liner. Mono PET is cheaper per kg than ES bico, so a 26% substitution is mildly accretive.

2 Offsetting costs

Higher oven energy from the added dwell, faster blade consumption, and added static and lint hardware. Expect the net to land close to zero.

3 Where the value is

MD spread that lets the core work over its full 385 mm. That shows up as leakage rate, and as an option to remove basis weight or SAP later — not as a fibre saving.

4. Supply risk

Trilobal mono PET at hygiene-grade finish and low shrink has a narrower supplier base than round ES. Qualify two sources before committing the line.

IN ONE PAGE

# The recommendation

1. Keep the skin stratum pure

12 gsm of 100% ES 2.2 dtex at A1. Softness is protected by construction, not by compromise in a blend.

2. Put the PET where it works

25 / 40 / 45% trilobal PET across A2, B1 and B2 — 26% of the web, all of it below the contact surface.

3. Split the card duties

Card 1 low draft for CD tensile, card 2 high draft for MD orientation. This is what makes both targets reachable at once.

4. Spec the fibre properly

Low shrink, durable multi-insult hydrophilic finish, long cut, low crimp. Three of the four are commonly forgotten.

5 Test the ultrasonic seal first

The only failure mode the nonwoven lab cannot see and the converting line will not forgive.

Expected outcome: +30% MD distribution · CD tensile 9.3 N/50 mm against an 8.0 N floor · unchanged skin contact · 45 gsm held
