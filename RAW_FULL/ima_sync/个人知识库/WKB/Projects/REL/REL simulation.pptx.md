# Carding Architecture – 3-Card In-Line Stratified Layup

Each stratum is carded SEPARATELY and stacked on the forming belt in deposition order (bottom card first). No cross-lapper — keeps strata distinct and MD-oriented for converting strength.

## Card Clothing (Wire) Matched to Each Stratum's Denier

|Stratum / Fiber|Denier|Wire / Clothing|Why|
|---|---|---|---|

MD orientation for converting: Single-cylinder / tandem cards lay fibers mostly along machine direction (MD:CD = 4:1-6:1). This high MD tensile is exactly what high-speed unwind & ADL placement need — another reason to avoid cross-lapping.

# PVA at the Bottom — Keeping Strength for High-Speed Converting

The conflict: 4D PVA does NOT melt in the air-through oven, so a PVA-rich bottom face would be poorly bonded — weak, fuzzy and lint-prone exactly where the converting line grips and tensions the web. Pure PVA at the very bottom surface fails at speed.

## Five-part solution — PVA stays functionally at the bottom, strength is preserved:

1

Co-blend an ES carrier into the PVA stratum

Card the bottom stratum as ~65% PVA + ~35% 2D ES. The melting ES sheath bonds at crossover points and mechanically traps/anchors the non-bonding PVA. (Min binder for integrity ≈ 20-35%.)

2

Add a thin ES-rich bonding skin as the true bottom face

A 2-4 gsm fine-ES veil laid beneath the PVA stratum becomes the actual machine-contact surface: fully bonded, clean, lint-free, high-grip — PVA sits just above it, still the lowest functional layer.

3

Keep fiber MD-oriented, don't cross-lap or needle

Cross-lapping lowers MD strength and folds the strata; needing collapses loft and drags PVA up through the web. In-line MD stacking + through-air bond only.

4

Air-through direction: top $\rightarrow$ bottom

Blow hot air from the fine top face down onto the belt. This presses strata together, melts ES throughout the thickness, and seats the PVA stratum onto the ES skin rather than lifting fines.

5
Optional light bottom compaction

Optional light bottom compaction

If converting tension demands more, a very light bottom-side emboss/calender (e.g. ~110°C, low nip) raises MD strength & anti-lint with minimal loft loss — applied to the ES skin, not the loft core.

# Special Process Key Points – Critical Control & Risks

[图片: !]
### PVA is non-thermoplastic

4D water-insoluble PVA will NOT bond thermally — it acts as a reinforcing filler. Keep ≤8-10% so enough ES sheath remains to bond the web. Over-loading $\rightarrow$ weak web / fiber shedding.

[图片: +]
### Rapid cooling locks structure

Forced cooling right after the oven freezes the crimp and bonded loft. Slow cooling lets the web relax & flatten — caliper can drop 15-25%. Add a chill-drum / cold-air zone.

[图片: >]
### Maintain the capillary gradient

Web-laying order matters: fine web (2D/3D) faces the upper SAP, coarse/hollow web faces the lower SAP. Cross-lapping must not fully homogenize the two carded layers.

[图片: *]
### Activate PTT/PET latent crimp

7D side-by-side develops its 3D spiral crimp only above ~135°C. Zone 2 must reliably reach 138–143°C or loft/resilience under-develops. Biggest loft lever.

[图片]
### Durable hydrophilic finish

Standard hydrophilic finishes wash off after 1-2 insults. Specify durable / sheath-incorporated hydrophilic ES so 2nd & 3rd-insult intake stays fast — critical for intake.

[图片]
### Avoid over-bonding

Excess temp / dwell / nip fuses too many crossover points $\rightarrow$ loses void volume, permeability and softness. Tune to the minimum bond meeting wet-tensile spec (≥75 N/m).

# Composition Sensitivity – Tuning Levers & Trade-offs

## If you need MORE...

### Faster intake

$\uparrow$ 2D ES to 33-35% (finer capillary); durable hydrophilic finish

### Wider distribution

$\uparrow$ 3D CoPET/PET to 10-12%; add slight cross-lap angle

## More loft / cushion

$\uparrow$ 7D PTT/PET to 28-30% + raise Zone 2 to 142°C

### Higher permeability

$\uparrow$ 6D Hollow ES to 33%; lower basis weight to 22 gsm

### Greater wet strength

$\uparrow$ 4D PVA to 10% (cap) or raise basis weight to 26 gsm

## Trade-offs to watch

### Too much 2D ES

Over-bonds web $\rightarrow$ loft & permeability fall; intake gains plateau

### Too much PVA

Insufficient binder $\rightarrow$ fiber shedding, weak web, dusting

## Too much 7D PTT/PET

Coarser surface, higher cost, weaker fine-capillary intake

### Basis weight too high

Thicker pad, slower z-flow, higher cost per diaper

### Over-bonding (temp/nip)

* Collapsed voids $\rightarrow$ worse distribution & rewet despite strength
