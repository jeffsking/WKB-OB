# Blizzard and ActiveTech Technologies

## Executive summary

ActiveTech is the manufacturing platform and process capability for producing thin, fluffless absorbent cores online. Blizzard is the resulting core construction: typically a fully wrapped, 100% superabsorbent material (SAM) core stabilized with adhesive. ROTC (Real Online ThinTech Core) is a product implementation of this platform.

## 1. Why move beyond a conventional SAM/fluff core?

In a conventional core, fluff pulp provides capillary wicking and temporary void volume. This supports rapid initial fluid intake before the SAM swells and immobilizes the fluid.

A Blizzard core reduces or eliminates fluff. The opportunity is a thinner, softer, more flexible absorbent pad with stronger pad integrity. The engineering trade-off is that SAM, adhesive, facings, and wrap construction must now carry much more of the fluid-management load.

## 2. Blizzard core construction

A typical Blizzard construction is:

Top facing / wrap → SAM + adhesive layer → Bottom facing / wrap

- SAM is the primary absorbent and fluid-locking component.
- Adhesive immobilizes the SAM and supports pad integrity. One cited configuration uses adhesive at approximately 5% of SAM weight.
- A full wrap contains the absorbent layer, limits particle migration, and maintains the thin-core geometry.
Blizzard is therefore not simply “a core without fluff.” It replaces fluff’s structural role through deliberate control of SAP distribution, adhesive bonding, and wrap/facing construction.

## 3. What ActiveTech does

ActiveTech is the process platform that enables continuous, online formation of Blizzard-type cores. In the ROTC implementation, this includes an ACF module, SAP feeding and metering, vacuum-assisted formation and transfer, wrapping/folding, and core sealing.

Critical process controls include:

- SAP metering and distribution. ROTC uses volumetric feeding and can employ two feeders.
- Vacuum and web transfer. Low-fluff or fluffless structures are sensitive to vacuum conditions and pad formation.
- D-wrap folding and containment. SAP loss or “flying” at conveyor and folding zones must be controlled.
- Adhesive selection and add-on. Too little adhesive risks poor integrity; too much can reduce accessible void volume and slow intake.
## 4. ROTC: the product application

ROTC means Real Online ThinTech Core. It applies Blizzard/ActiveTech capability to an online absorbent-core architecture.

Two common configurations are:

- ROTC Slim / Fluffless: an all- or nearly all-fluffless core designed for maximum thinness and flexibility.
- ROTC HBC (Hybrid Core): a bottom 100% fast-SAM ROTC layer combined with a SAM/fluff top layer and pad channel. This restores capacity and fluid-handling margin while retaining much of the thin-core benefit.
## 5. Performance balance

The technology is a classic trade-off: it can improve thinness, flexibility, high-pressure rewet, and pad integrity, but a pure fluffless approach may compromise initial intake speed or leakage robustness if the material and structure are not optimized.

In an adult-care ROTC HBC comparison, thickness decreased from 5.5 mm to 4.4 mm and high-pressure rewet declined from 2.67 g to 0.40 g, while retention capacity remained around 700 g. The third intake was slower, however.

In baby-care ROTC Slim evaluations, thickness and rewet improved as expected, but cradle intake slowed and urine leakage trended upward. The team therefore retained a hybrid architecture with pad channels for ROTC 2.0.

## Engineering takeaway

The value of Blizzard/ActiveTech is not merely removing fluff. It is the ability to manufacture a thin, flexible, structurally robust SAP/adhesive core online. The hard part is preserving intake speed, fluid distribution, and leakage protection without surrendering the thinness advantage.
