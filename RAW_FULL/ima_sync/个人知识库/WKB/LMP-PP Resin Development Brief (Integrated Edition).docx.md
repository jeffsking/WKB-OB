# Development Brief: Low Melting Point Polypropylene (LMP-PP) Resin (Integrated Edition)

Date: 2026-09-22 Sponsor: Hygiene Materials Innovation R&D Document Note: This brief integrates the main Development Brief and the Plan C Technical Supplement, and adds the Plan B catalyst route decision point (based on the mPP technology platform assessment).

# 1. Background and Objective

Thermal-bonding components for hygiene nonwovens (air-through / thermal calendering / spunbond) have long relied on imported low-melting-point PP or sheath-core bicomponent solutions based on low-melt PE. This project aims to develop a domestic fiber-grade low-melting-point PP resin, filling the gap in the domestic resin supply (existing domestic fiber-grade PP is all homopolymer, melting point 164–165°C).

# 1.1 Technical Principle

Comonomers (ethylene or C4–C10 α-olefins, 0.8–10 wt%) interrupt isotactic sequences and reduce lamellar crystalline perfection, lowering the melting point from 160–165°C (homopolymer) to the target range.

# 1.2 Why This Is a Domestic Blank (Project Justification)

•Catalyst layer: The mainstream technical carrier for low-melt PP is metallocene mPP (single-site catalyst: uniform comonomer insertion, narrow molecular weight distribution). Metallocene catalyst technology has long been monopolized and restricted by ExxonMobil, Dow, Basell and Mitsui Chemicals; the cocatalyst MAO/MMAO still cannot be produced domestically (import price RMB 3–4 million/ton); continuous plants are prone to agglomeration and blockage with high fines content.

•Market layer: mPP has many grades but small volume per grade — switching costs are prohibitive for domestic megaton-scale plants; no trial customers → no machine data → no iteration → no users (dead loop); downstream fabric mills are price-insensitive with entrenched import channels.

•Industrial resonance: Resin blank → fiber blank (domestic ES bicomponent fiber is dominated by PE/PP at 110–130°C; the PP/PP 140°C tier is essentially absent) → invisible demand → no resin-plant incentive. Japanese players (JPP, Chisso) are vertically integrated from catalyst to polymerization to fiber to nonwoven, so this dead loop does not exist there.

•Opening window: Sinopec BRICI’s metallocene PP catalyst has completed industrial trials (both homo- and random-copolymer grades); PetroChina’s PMP-01 and MPP-S02 catalysts broke through successively; Guangzhou Petrochemical has produced mPP pilot batches with its in-house metallocene catalyst (“narrow MWD, low melting point, good spinnability, widely usable in filament and nonwovens”); high-end polyolefins are listed in the “14th Five-Year Plan” new chemical materials breakthrough priorities; domestic mPP consumption is growing ~15% annually (vs 6% globally).

# 2. Key Technical Cognition: Catalyst Route Is a Prerequisite (Must Be Decided Before Starting Plan B)

mPP is not a fourth independent route — it is the underlying technology platform of Plans A/B. Catalyst-level comparison:

|Item|Ziegler-Natta (multi-site)|Metallocene (single-site)|
|---|---|---|
|Comonomer distribution|Varying reactivity ratios across sites; non-uniform insertion|Statistically uniform insertion; controllable stereo-defect distribution|
|MWD|Broad|Narrow (1.8–2.2)|
|Melting point adjustment|Limited (JPP terpolymers 125.5–137°C, for heat-seal film)|Precisely tunable 130–165°C|
|Solubles / extractables|High (tackiness, extraction, roll contamination)|Low (low odor, low fogging, minimal contamination — critical for hygiene)|
|Heat-seal initiation temperature|Medium-high|mPP random copolymers as low as 105–115°C|
|Cost / availability|Low, domestically mature|High; MAO imported; domestic catalyst just breaking through|

Decision point: For Plan B, choose “mPP + high comonomer” (performance ceiling: 135–150°C, low solubles, low extractables; higher cost, domestic catalyst newly available) or “ZN + high ethylene” (low cost, existing plants; higher solubles requiring downstream control). This is the first decision before Plan B launch; recommended approach is to run small-lot samples of both and decide based on hygiene-line machine performance.

# 3. Three-Tier Development Plan

# Plan A: Conventional Low-Melt Grade (150–158°C) — lowest risk, early validation

|Parameter|Target|
|---|---|
|Comonomer|Ethylene 0.8–5 wt% (random copolymer, RCP)|
|MFR|20–40 g/10min (within the spunbond range 6–150; achievable via peroxide-initiated controlled rheology from base resin)|
|Isotacticity|≥95%|
|Xylene solubles|&lt;6 wt% (copolymer control line)|
|Applicable process|Direct switch on existing spunbond/thermal-bonding lines|

# Plan B: Intermediate-Temperature Binder Grade (135–150°C) — core development target

|Parameter|Target|
|---|---|
|Comonomer|Ethylene + butene total 5–10 wt% (butene lowers Tm more efficiently)|
|Low-isotacticity fraction|5–30% (deliberately introduced via catalyst system; further suppresses Tm, improves low-temperature flow and softness)|
|MFR|30–50 g/10min (thermal calendering / air-through bonding)|
|PDI|2.8–4.5 (dual control: hydrogen regulation + metallocene catalysis)|
|Xylene solubles|&lt;6 wt%|
|Catalyst route|See Section 2 decision point (mPP vs ZN); must be decided before launch|

# Plan C: Ultra-Low-Melt Sheath-Resin Grade (≤135°C) — sheath resin for all-PP sheath-core bicomponent fibers (EPC type)

# 3.1 Positioning

Plan C does not develop a low-melt PP single-component fiber. It develops an ultra-low melting point PP resin for the sheath layer of sheath-core bicomponent fibers — a domestic substitute for the low-melt component of ES fiber systems. The benchmark is the EPC grade of Japan’s Chisso (now JNC/CHANTO) ES series: PP/PP sheath-core structure, sheath melting point 140°C, high tenacity, designed for bonding to PP substrates. This fiber form has been commercial for years, yet both the domestic PP/PP 140°C fiber tier and its corresponding sheath resin are blank in China.

In the sheath-core structure, the low-melt sheath is the “glue” and the high-melt core is the “skeleton”: during thermal bonding only the sheath melts, forming polymer fusion bridges at fiber crossover points while the core remains unmelted, preserving fiber morphology and bundle strength. Versus the PE/PP solution (110–135°C), a PP/PP sheath bonds at higher temperature (140–155°C) but is chemically identical to the core with better interfacial compatibility, and the fiber is 100% polypropylene — favorable for all-PP nonwoven recyclability.

# 3.2 Three Stacked Tm-Suppression Levers (Resin Side)

|Lever|Parameter|Rationale|
|---|---|---|
|Comonomer at upper limit|~10 wt% (ethylene + butene; butene more efficient)|The 0.8–10 wt% range alone drops Tm from 160–165°C to 153–158°C; the upper limit pushes further down|
|Low-isotacticity fraction|5–30 mass% (isotactic pentad 30–60%)|Weakly crystallizing fraction — second Tm lever; improves low-temperature flow and softness|
|Reverse formulation: no nucleating agent|No sodium benzoate / calcium stearate / sorbitol types (normally 10–2000 ppm for shrinkage reduction)|Nucleating agents accelerate crystallization and refine lamellae — opposite of the low-Tm goal; crystallinity pressed below 60% (drawn-fiber baseline 60–75%)|

# 3.3 Essential Difference from the Single-Component Route (Constraint Comparison)

A single-component low-melt PP fiber demands that one fiber, one resin simultaneously carry four burdens: “low melting point + high tenacity + spinnability + heat-settability” (the crystallinity contradiction between strength and melting point has no structural outlet), with the commercial melting point floor stuck around 150°C. The sheath-core structure splits the contradiction between two resins: the core carries strength (ready-made homopolymer, zero development), the sheath focuses solely on “low melting point + bicomponent spinning compatibility” — all other specifications are exempt. The sheath resin is therefore a more loosely constrained, more attainable development target.

# 3.4 Four Process Pitfalls

1.Xylene solubles runaway (biggest risk): Solubles rise in tandem with low crystallinity, causing tackiness, poor spinning operability and web dimensional instability. Air-through lines run at 100–180°C — if the sheath gets tacky during spinning/web forming, the line cannot run. Hold solubles <6 wt%: the low-isotacticity fraction must be achieved via catalyst-based blocky incorporation, not random dilution.

2.Insufficient quenching: Low-crystallinity-tendency materials crystallize slowly. Commercial practice is controlled air quenching with the final temperature held between Tg ≈ -10°C and Tg +15°C to maximize drawability; chill roll + intensified quench air recommended, otherwise filament breakage occurs in the drawing zone.

3.Extremely narrow heat-setting window: Setting temperature must be above the stress-relaxation requirement but below the fiber melting point. For a 135°C sheath resin the usable window is far narrower than conventional PP; setting temperature shifts down and standard line settings cannot be copied.

4.Re-calibrated draw ratios: The standard two-stage drawing (Stage 1: 110–160°C, 4×–14×; Stage 2: 80–135°C, 1.2×–3×) was designed for homopolymer; the low-melt sheath is already near its softening zone at 110°C, so Stage 1 lower temperature limits and ratios require a fresh DOE.

# 3.5 Bicomponent Structure as a Buffer (Relieving Resin-Side Difficulty)

•Lower sheath ratio: Commercial sheath-core ratios commonly run 50:50 or 30:70, down to 10:90 at the extreme. Reducing the sheath share (e.g., to 30:70 or below) cuts low-melt resin consumption and relaxes the Tm requirement — the sheath need not hit 130°C; 145–150°C works as long as a sufficient window to the ~165°C core is maintained.

•Additive zoning: The sheath carries functional additives (e.g., 0.1–0.6 mass% barium sulfate for antistatic) while the core stays high-purity for mechanical load; hydrophilic finishes are applied to the sheath to avoid disturbing core crystallization. The resin target can be relaxed one tier — advancing on both “structure design” and “resin design” legs.

# 3.6 Spunbond Compatibility (PP/PP Bicomponent Spunbond)

PP/PP sheath-core already has commercial bicomponent spunbond lines (BICO type): sheath-core ratios 50/50, 20/80 (coaxial or eccentric), products 15–60 g/m², strength 10–25% higher than standard spunbond. Key difference from PE/PP: the sheath-core melting-point differential window is only 15–30°C (vs ~40°C for PE/PP), demanding higher precision in calender temperature, line speed and pressure; under high-speed spunbond drawing (3000–6000 m/min) the low-melt sheath endures thermal stress, requiring higher melt strength than the staple fiber route. Recommended sequence: validate on air-through lines (staple fiber) first, then move to bicomponent spunbond pilot lines.

# 4. General Process and Formulation Key Points

1.Antioxidant system: 0.1–0.5 wt% antioxidant is mandatory within the 200–320°C extrusion window; the higher the comonomer content, the closer to the upper limit (0.5%).

2.Drawing / heat-setting re-tuning: The lower temperature limits of two-stage drawing shift downward for low-crystallinity materials; heat-setting temperature must be lowered in line with the melting point, otherwise the low-melt component softens during setting.

3.Solubles control: Xylene solubles rise as melting point falls and directly correlate with fiber strength. All plans must hold solubles <6 wt% to keep strength above the standard-grade floor.

4.Enhanced quenching: Low-crystallinity-tendency materials require higher quench rates (chill roll / quench air) to prevent surface tack and roll sticking.

# 5. Acceptance Criteria

|Item|Specification|
|---|---|
|Fiber tenacity|4–6 cN/dtex (standard grade, 350–525 MPa)|
|Elongation at break|10–30%|
|Thermal shrinkage|&lt;5% (under tensile stress at 117°C)|
|Melting point|Per target range of Plan A / B / C|

|Application spec (Plan C)|1.5D–3D fine denier, 38–51 mm cut length, hydrophilic finish (standard hygiene bicomponent staple spec)|

# 6. Benchmarking and Validation Path

•Benchmark grades: TotalEnergies Lumicene MR 2002 (mPP, MFR 15, melting point 151°C, dedicated spunbond hygiene nonwoven grade) is the ready-made resin-side reference; Chisso EPC (PP/PP sheath-core, 140°C) is the fiber-side benchmark; JPP terpolymers (125.5–137°C) and TotalEnergies 8573/Z9450 serve as heat-seal-film references (parameters must not be applied directly).

# •Implementation path:

# 1.Plan A: minor plant modifications; samples in 1–2 months;

2.Plan B: complete the Section 2 catalyst route decision (mPP vs ZN small-lot comparison) before development launch;

3.Plan C: air-through line (sheath-core staple) validation first → bicomponent spunbond pilot → progressively lower melting point combined with sheath-ratio design.

•Pilot resources (resin end): Sinopec system (Zhenhai Refining, Maoming, Changling Refining; Guangzhou Petrochemical has mPP pilot capability), PetroChina (PMP-01/MPP-S02 metallocene catalyst platforms), private plants (Fujian Zhongjing Petrochemical, etc.).

•Modification partners (MFR adjustment / compounding): Beijing Yishitong New Materials (drafting unit of national standard GB/T 30923; peroxide-controlled rheology), Hunan Shengjin New Materials (leading meltblown/spunbond specialty resin maker; customers include Berry, TEDA, Xinlong, JOFO, Hengtian Jiahua, Bidefu). Note the boundary: modification houses only perform post-polymerization molecular-weight treatment (MFR adjustment, compounding); melting-point reduction must happen in the polymerization reactor.

•Application validation partners (fiber end): Yizheng Chemical Fibre (low-melt fiber series 110–230°C, raw material supplied internally by Sinopec), Shanghai Defulun (low-melt specialty fibers, small-batch sampling); PP/PP fiber candidates: Botao Synthetic Fiber (polypropylene staple 150,000+ t/a), Mengtai Hi-Tech (polypropylene filament 50,000+ t/a, listed company) and other PP fiber producers with existing spinning lines.

•SABIC channel risk note: SABIC’s hygiene fiber-grade portfolio (511A/519A/510A/519P/514M12, etc.) is all homopolymer (melting point 158–160°C); its bicomponent solution uses a PP + POP plastomer sheath — no commercial fiber-grade low-melt PP resin exists in its portfolio. If a channel offers “SABIC low-melt PP”, it is most likely grade 620P (random copolymer, melting point 142°C, but MFR only 1.6, packaging grade) being passed off; procurement should screen accordingly.

# 7. Risk Notes

1.Heat-seal film grade ≠ fiber grade: Heat-seal-film low-melt PP grades (JPP terpolymers, TotalEnergies 8573/Z9450, etc.) generally have MFR <10; parameters must not be applied directly — a complete resin redesign to fiber-grade MFR 20–50 is required. Domestic high-speed spunbond lines prefer MFR 35–40; do not copy the benchmark grade’s MFR 15.

2.Grade-name pitfalls: The market term “low-melt copolymer” (e.g., K8303) is a colloquial name for impact copolymers with melting points still above 160°C — do not confuse during grade selection.

3.Supplier melting-point nomenclature: Some suppliers’ stated “melting point” is actually the sheath softening / heat-bonding initiation temperature, not the DSC melting peak; acceptance testing shall be based on DSC measurement.

4.Single-component route not recommended as the primary target: Low melting point and high tenacity have no structural outlet in a single-component fiber, with no successful commercial precedent; reserve for Phase 2 exploration.

5.Supplier audit: Capacity and customer information above comes from company statements and industry media; conduct standard supplier audits (medical-grade GMP and quality system verification) before cooperation.
