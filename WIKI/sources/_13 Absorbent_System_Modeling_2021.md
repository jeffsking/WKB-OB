#   Urine Leakage Design Principles (ULDP)

#   Urine Leakage Design Principles (version 2)

#   Intake Urine at the Rate of Insult:

#   Liquid Penetration

Absorbent materials should allow urine to enter quickly over a wide range of consumer conditions (velocity of insult, volumetric rate, user position, multiple insults, etc).

![](1/parseimages/figure8845e61d7747448cbb5ad96772873089_0_5.png)
![](1/parseimages/figurede3241815f4e46138263f09c7432a926_0_6.png)
#   Void Volume

There should be enough accessible void volume during the delivery of the insult to accommodate each consumer representative urination.

#   Temporary Containment of Urine:

#   Bucket Volume

There should be enough containment space above the body side absorbent materials to temporarily hold and contain urine in various product positions until it is absorbed.

![](1/parseimages/figure61ef65179e644c41ae0c787b11eea7ac_0_12.png)
![](1/parseimages/figure0bd8146c65e048fd9a2699aaa5bb3b24_0_13.png)
#   Effective Barrier System

Material properties, placement, and adhesion to other components should prevent uncontrolled urine from escaping the product via a path of least resistance (tunnels, channels, holes, gutters, seams, etc)

#   Lock-up Urine:

#   Functional Capacity

There should be enough available capacity in the insult and surrounding area to lock-up urine in various consumer representative conditions pressures.

![](1/parseimages/figure069a204427684af8abeba149d2232b13_0_19.png)
Modeling is one way you can check to see if a design meets the absorbent based design principles

Why modeling: It is a means to leverage the ULDP

$\bullet$ Calculate void volume: of different materials, under pressure, available under partial saturation, ...

$\bullet$ Estimate capacity: product, for region of product (target area), after two insults, ...

$\bullet$ Estimate fluid intake: surge density, contact angle, position (sitting, prone, supine)

$\bullet$ Locations where fluid not yet locked up in SAM & at risk for leaks
