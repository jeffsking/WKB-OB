#   Objective of Forming Technologies

The goal of forming technologies is to achieve a uniformly mixed absorbent core of fluff and SAM which

$\bullet$ Has high pad integrity

$\bullet$ Has little SAM damage

$\bullet$ Has correct basis weight (including zoned pads)

$\bullet$ Has low weight variability (CoV)

* Is free of clumps and hard spots

#   Pulp Unwind

What are some common issues?

$\bullet$ Improper alignment of the pulp roll on the core shaft, and/or improper core shaft positioning relative to the pulp tracking system cause non-uniform pulp feed rate

$\bullet$ Splice has 3 sheets of pulp instead of 2 resulting in a short period of heavy pads as well as higher risk of scarfing plugups

#   Pulp Fiberization

$\bullet$ PRIMARY DESIGN PRINCIPLE:

Fluid Intake Rate

![Fluid Intake Rate\n✓\n×\n×\nFluid intake rate rate equal consumer insult rate over a range of insult rates.\n](1/parseimages/figure9ce221fbf7954cfe97aafd6b01b6983d_0_3.png)
Fluid intake rate should equal consumer insult rate over a range of insult rates.

$\bullet$ Objective of fiberization- Break Hydrogen fiber bonds with the least amount of fiber damage in order to enhance absorbent quality.

$\bullet$ Proper air flow during the fiberization process is critical to control variability and avoid fiber damage

#   Pulp Fiberization

Pulp fiberization can be achieved by the use of a Fiberizer or a Hammermill. Fiberizer is the recommended standard, but both can be seen throughout facilities in North America and KCl.

(1) Gram of Pulp has Approximately (4) Million Fibers

(2) Each gram of pulp at 1200ppm is 160 lbs/hour

(3) Pulp roll is 1 ton

* Pulp Fiberization Rates

* 4,000 lbs/hr PCMC and Williams Hammermills

* 3,000 lbs/hr JOA Fiberizer (Rotor Mill)

* 2,200 lbs/hr Kamas Defiberizer

* 500 lbs/hr JOA Disk Mill
