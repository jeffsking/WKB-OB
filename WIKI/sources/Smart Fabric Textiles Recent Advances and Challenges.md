## Scope and Structure 范围与结构

1. **Introduction** — why natural and chemical fibres no longer meet user requirement; the passive / active / ultra-smart taxonomy; history from the late-1980s Japanese shape-memory silk thread; global smart textiles market at **25% CAGR 2021→2031, US$23.82 Bn by 2031**.
2. **Brief History** — wearable computation traced to the 1850s (corsets, belts), first wearable computer 1955, integrated electronics from the early 1990s; three generations: wearable computing → fabric as circuit element → fibre-level smart textiles.
3. **Fabrication Methods** — three routes: (i) conductive yarns (metal filaments, metal-coated polymer, wrapped metal foil; conductivity from 0.5 Ω/m to several kΩ/m, with limited elasticity causing knots and breaks, and heat-cutting incompatible with metal wires); (ii) weaving and knitting including 3D structures; (iii) post-fabrication integration via conductive adhesive, LEDs on narrow woven fabric, printed PEDOT strain-sensor lines.

## Key Findings 关键发现

1. **The three-tier taxonomy is the organising idea.** Passive smart textiles sense; active ones sense *and respond*; ultra-smart ones sense, react and adapt. This is a useful way to grade how ambitious any proposed hygiene-product feature is.
2. **Seven chromic mechanisms named.** Photochromic, thermochromic, electrochromic, solvatochromic, **halochromic (pH)**, tribochromic (friction), mechanochromic (pressure). Colour reverts when the stimulus is removed because the original state is more stable.
3. **Halochromic fabrics already work as real sensors.** PA6 fabric dyed with Bromocresol Green changes colour immediately under acidifying air pollutants (acid rain pH 3.6–5.6 vs. normal rain >5.6); separately, silk protein ink suspended with pH indicators and lactate oxidase read wearer fatigue. This is the closest precedent in this source to a **urine wetness indicator** — pH-driven colour on a polyamide substrate.
4. **Thermochromy can do mechanical work.** Beyond colour, thermochromic dyestuff fibres shorten at high temperature, opening fabric pores to admit air and cool the body, and elongate at low temperature to close pores and retain warmth. Colour change and structural actuation are the same event.
5. **Waterproof-breathable is a pore-size gap, quantified.** Pores are engineered ~20,000× smaller than a liquid water droplet but still ~700× larger than a water molecule — hence liquid is blocked and vapour passes. Desired property list given explicitly: heat and moisture regulation, surplus-heat absorption, waterproofness, air and vapour permeability, rapid drying, dimensional stability when wet, lightweight and soft handle.
6. **Shape memory behaves as a self-adjusting barrier.** A shape memory polymer laminated between two fabric layers opens its porous structure above a switching temperature to dump heat and closes to a tight structure below it, blocking heat, water and wind. Activation temperatures are reported as close to body temperature; one commercial demonstration is a non-iron shirt triggered above 35 °C.
7. **Electroconductive textiles fall into seven material classes** — metal monofilaments, co-spun polymer–metal yarns, metal-coated filaments, metal-filled filaments, carbon allotropes, intrinsically conductive polymers, and conventional fibres coated with conductive polymer.
8. **Scalability is the honest bottleneck.** The review states most smart textile materials are hand-made; there is a practical limit on how fine electronic fibres can be drawn; compatible-material selection and versatile manufacturing methods are named needs.
9. **Standardisation gap.** No universal test exists for demonstrating warming/cooling effect; smart textiles are washed per ISO 6330 but washability depends jointly on conductor track type and textile substrate, so "no global conclusions can be drawn". Recyclability research is explicitly called *very scarce*.
10. **Antimicrobial route.** Antibacterial, antifungal and antiviral finishes are used in air filters, personal hygiene, ventilation and water purification; the review warns synthetic chemicals and nanoparticles lack environmental-impact data and that a robust solution must be planned in advance to avoid repeating the plastic-waste problem.

## Relevance to Hygiene R&D 与卫材研发的关联

**Moderate 中等关联** — there is no absorbent-core content here at all, and nothing on acquisition, rewet or SAP. Its value is two specific mechanism transfers plus one caution.

- **Wetness indication.** Finding 3 is the most transferable item: halochromic colour change demonstrated on polyamide fabric with a stated pH window is the direct mechanism ancestor of a urine indicator, alongside thermochromy which is what the programme actually uses. Read next to [[US20070270773A1 Thermochromic Wetness Indicator]] for the disposable-implementation view, and against [[Hygiene Chromic Ink Supplier Landscape（卫生护理变色油墨供应商调研 · 2026-06-12）]] for what is purchasable today rather than academically demonstrated.
- **Breathable barrier design.** Finding 5 gives the textbook justification for why a microporous film can block liquid and pass vapour — the same requirement a diaper backsheet must satisfy. The numbers are order-of-magnitude and directional rather than design-grade, so use them for explanation, not specification.
- **Chromic programme context.** The seven-way chromic taxonomy is a useful framing for deciding which stimulus to design against; see [[Chromic Phenomena Solar Energy and Liquid Crystals]] for the physical chemistry and [[Mechanochromic Elastomer from PDLC 5CB PDMS]] plus [[Interactively Mechanochromic Electronic Textile Sensor]] for the pressure-driven variants.
- **Honest limits.** Everything here is apparel- and military-oriented and cost-blind: the electronics routes (conductive yarns, PEDOT printing, woven displays) assume a washable reusable garment, which is the opposite of a single-use disposable. Nothing addresses converting at hygiene line speeds, adhesive compatibility, or the safety review that any skin-contact chromic chemistry in a hygiene product must clear. Treat this as **background reading for the indicator programme**, not as a development reference.
