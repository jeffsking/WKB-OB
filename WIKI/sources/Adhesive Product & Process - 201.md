# Adhesive Fundamentals – Definitions and Use

Adhesive: A substance which, when applied between the surfaces of two materials, causes the materials to resist separation.

Hot Melt : Is a thermoplastic substance that is melted to fluid state applied then cooled to a solid state.

# Adhesive Fundamentals – Applications

## Why Do We Use a Hot-Melt Adhesive?

$\bullet$ Readily bonds dissimilar materials

$\bullet$ Accommodate sensitive substrates

$\bullet$ Wide range of bond capability

$\bullet$ Can form temporary or permanent bonds

$\bullet$ High peel and shear strength capability

* Low cost for the amounts used

* Hidden bonding

$\bullet$ Variety of methods for product and process design

* No solvents or high toxicity (as in an epoxy)

* No dry time (as with a water or solvent borne adhesive)

# What must an adhesive do to form a good bond?

1. Flow: An adhesive must have the proper viscosity and rheology to be compatible with the application, equipment and substrates. The adhesive must be applied to the substrates in a fluid condition.

2. Wet out: An adhesive must make intimate contact with the substrates. The surface energy of the substrate should be higher than that of the adhesive, since a material will spread only over a surface of equal or higher surface energy. Wet-out is also aided by compression and temperature.

3. Transfer: Usually the adhesive is only applied to one substrate -- it must have sufficient open time to still wet-out and bond to the second substrate.

4. Adhesion: Have strong surface bonding through intermolecular forces.

5. Solidify: An adhesive must solidify (cool, dry, or cure depending on the type of adhesive) to gain strength and become stronger than at least one of the substrates.
