## Scope and Structure 范围与结构

The paper is a standard experimental study: introduction and literature review, experimental part, results and discussion, conclusion, references, and a bilingual summary.

- **Introduction / review of prior art** — general TPE behaviour: TPEs show the mechanical properties of chemically crosslinked elastomers (high tensile strength, impact resistance, resilient elasticity, low-temperature flexibility) while being processed like thermoplastics by extrusion, injection or rotational moulding and calendering. Increasing soft-segment content lowers enthalpy and melting temperature, lowers tensile strength and hardness, gives less crystalline polymers, and raises elongation at break. Typical TPEs listed: styrene-diene triblock copolymers, block copolyurethanes, block copoly(ether-amides), block copoly(ether-esters). Prior work cited: Walch & Gaymans (PBT-b-polyisobutylene, 1994/1993), Kennedy (telechelic dihydroxy polyisobutylene, Mn 1400–10000), Lin & Qutubuddin (PET-b-hydrogenated-butylene in melt, 1994), Schmalz et al. (2001) using PEO-PEB-PEO to introduce soft polyolefin segments by **melt** polycondensation without high-boiling solvents.
- **Experimental Part** — *Synthesis*: two-step catalysed transesterification in solution starting from DMT, BD and HO-PEB-OH; high-boiling 1,2,4-trichlorobenzene solvent; Ti(OBu)₄ catalyst; DPPD thermal stabiliser; reactants chosen for a theoretical hard:soft weight ratio of 60:40. *Characterisation*: inherent viscosity by Ubbelohde viscometer at 30 °C in phenol:1,1,2,2-tetrachloroethylene:toluene (1:1:2 vol.); ¹H NMR in deuterated trifluoroacetic acid on a Varian GEMINI-200 (200 MHz); 24 h Soxhlet extraction in chloroform with residue dried 5 h at 60 °C; complex dynamic melt viscosity on a Rheometrics RMS-605 at 240 °C from 0.1 to 100 rad/s; G′ and G″ by dynamic torsion on bars 63.0 × 12.4 × 1.0 mm at 6.28 rad/s from 30 to 160 °C; DSC on a Perkin Elmer DSC-2 from 50 to 250 °C at 10 °C/min heating and 40 °C/min cooling; TGA on a Perkin Elmer TGS-2 in nitrogen and air.
- **Results and Discussion** — Tables 1–3 plus Figures 1–5.
- **Conclusion**

## Key Findings 关键发现

1. **Design intent: non-polar soft segment.** Soft non-polar olefinic prepolymers are built into the polyester chain specifically to **improve hydrolytic and thermal stability and achieve better phase separation**, which particularly shows up as increased elasticity. Literature coverage of polyolefin soft segments in TPEs is explicitly called very scarce.
2. **Why solution polycondensation.** Phase separation occurs during polycondensation because soft non-polar segments mix poorly with polar DMT and BD. The remedy is performing synthesis in the presence of a difficultly-volatile solvent that is a good solvent for both PBT and the non-polar prepolymer — here 1,2,4-trichlorobenzene at 50 wt-%, used in the first transesterification step at normal pressure to avoid demixing; DPPD is added in the second step and the reaction runs under high vacuum (<1 mm Hg) to remove excess glycol.
3. **Optimised conditions.** **Catalyst Ti(OBu)₄ at 1.0 mmol per mol ester**; **DPPD stabiliser at 1.0 wt-%** of total reaction mass; **5 h polycondensation under vacuum at 240 °C**. A screening set (TPEHB1–3, varying catalyst 0.96/1.44/1.92 mmol/mol) showed inherent viscosity falling from 0.35 to 0.31 dl/g as catalyst rose; across all polymers [η] ranged 0.31–0.38 dl/g and complex dynamic viscosity at 240 °C ranged 21–100 Pa·s.
4. **Incorporation stays partial.** By ¹H NMR the molar soft-segment content was 4.2–6.7 mol-% against a theoretical 4.3 mol-%; hard PBT content 51–62 wt-%; PBT degree of polymerisation 14–23 per mol of soft segment. Chloroform Soxhlet extraction after 24 h confirmed the limitation directly: the **insoluble fraction was rich in PBT segments** while the **chloroform-soluble fraction consisted predominantly of soft-segment oligomers**. Soluble fractions ran 28–52 wt-% with insoluble 46–72 wt-% (rows do not sum to 100 in the published table — noted without correction).
5. **Melting and crystallinity.** Tm 218–222 °C with first and second runs differing little; ΔHm 26.0–33.8 J/g first run and 25.3–33.3 J/g second run, both rising with hard-segment (PBT) content and segment length. Degree of crystallinity **18.0–23.3 %**, consistent with literature for other TPE types. Fraction of hard segments incorporated into crystallites w_c,PBT was 0.29–0.44, indicating short PBT segments remain in the amorphous phase.
6. **Two distinct families appear.** Criterion is crystallisation behaviour. **TPEHB1, TPEHB5, TPEHB6** show a single crystallisation peak and ΔTh = Tm − Tc of 31–32 °C — close to PBT's own ~36 °C, so they crystallise very fast. **TPEHB2, TPEHB3, TPEHB4** show two crystallisation peaks and ΔTh of 57–63 °C, so they crystallise slowly. The high-temperature peak corresponds to crystallisation in a hard-segment-rich phase, the low-temperature peak to PBT crystallising in a phase richer in non-polar soft segments — the paper's evidence for the two-phase structure.
7. **Property split follows the family split.** The fast-crystallising group has a rubbery-plateau **storage modulus roughly one decade higher** than the other group, because its higher crystallinity acts as the physical crosslinking that makes the TPE hard. In the melt the **more homogeneous polymers show lower viscosity** than the less homogeneous ones. All samples show pseudoplastic behaviour at 235 and 240 °C.
8. **Crystallised hard phase = physical crosslink.** The paper states plainly that the crystallised PBT hard phase IS the physical crosslinking in these TPEs, making the polymer material hard — the standard TPE structure-property link.
9. **Thermal stability.** 10 wt-% mass loss onset: **393–406 °C in nitrogen**, **375–397 °C in air**; 50 wt-% loss onset: 427–435 °C in nitrogen, 420–428 °C in air; residue at 500 °C in nitrogen 2.9–4.2 wt-%. Degradation in air begins at lower temperature, as expected. TGA curve shape indicates a complex degradation mechanism.
10. **Stability gain quantified.** Thermal stability and onset of degradation are **about 30 °C higher** than for thermoplastic poly(ester-ether) elastomers containing PTMO stabilised with the same stabiliser.

## Relevance to Hygiene R&D 与卫材研发的关联

**MODERATE 中等关联.** There is no nonwoven, no PP, no SAP and no absorbent core here. The value is as a **structure–property reference for any elastomeric component** in the product — elasticity is the one mechanical function the paper is about.

- **Elastic components.** The waistband, cuff and ear-panel elastication work in this vault needs a working model of what makes a TPE elastic and how it survives converting heat. Finding 8 gives it crisply: crystallised hard phase is the physical crosslink, so anything that disrupts hard-phase crystallisation destroys recovery. Chaining to the practical side, see [[Manufactured Fibre Technology Ch09 Heat-setting of Thermoplastic Fibres]] — heat-setting is exactly the process by which crystallinity is set or lost — and [[Origin of Rubber Elasticity]] for the underlying theory.
- **The 30 °C stability number is the sharpest transferable result.** Substituting a polyolefin soft segment for the usual polyether raised degradation onset ~30 °C. Polyether soft segments are the known weak link for hydrolytic and oxidative attack, so any long-life or high-heat-exposure elastic component is where this matters.
- **Negative result worth having.** Finding 4 is honest and useful: even with solution polycondensation, soft-segment incorporation was only partial and the soluble fraction was largely oligomer. This tells you that olefin-soft-segment TPEs are hard to make cleanly and that extracted oligomer content is a real risk. No such issue is flagged for commodity TPEs.
- **Adhesive angle.** TPE blocks also appear as hot-melt adhesive and elastic attachment components; see [[Adhesive Handbook Part 2 Fundamental Aspects]] for the interfacial side, and [[Spandex Elastomer Brand Comparison & DMAC Safety Assessment（氨纶弹性体品牌对比及 DMAC 安全评估 2026-07-28）]] for the commercial elastane option — noting that this paper presents **laboratory-synthesised copolyesters only**, with no processing, cost or skin-contact data.
