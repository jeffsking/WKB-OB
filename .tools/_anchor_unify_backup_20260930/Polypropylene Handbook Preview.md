## Key Findings 关键发现

Same findings as the twin entry; repeated here only where this document independently carries them:

1. **Grade-to-process map.** Slit film non-CR homopolymer 2–4 g/10 min; continuous filament CR homopolymer ≈35 MFR at ≈250 °C; BCF 12–20 MFR normal MWD; staple homopolymer 10–30 g/10 min; monofilament low MFR 1–4 g/10 min for tenacity; **spun-bonded CR homopolymer ≈35 g/10 min**; **melt-blown ≥500 g/10 min with narrow MWD**.
2. **Lower MFR buys tenacity** at a given draw ratio; higher MFR buys line speed. This trade is stated explicitly for slit film and is the general design lever across all fibre routes.
3. **Nonwovens dominate PP fibre demand**, and the book names the delivered property pair directly: spun-bonded is strong, melt-blown is soft with high bulk — the rationale for combining them.
4. **Melt-blown barrier function.** Fine fibres allow water-vapour passage while blocking liquid water and aqueous solutions.
5. **Hygiene named explicitly.** Diaper liners and feminine hygiene products are given as primary nonwoven applications alongside geotextiles and tarpaulins.
6. **Colour route.** PP is pigmented at extrusion rather than dyed, giving through-section colour that survives fibre breakage.
