# The Role of Surfactants

## One-line Position 一句话定位

Chapter 5 (pp. 166–215) is the formulation chapter: it builds the full causal chain surfactant → interfacial adsorption → free-energy change → wetting/penetration kinetics → macroscopic absorbency, and it separates the two most-confused product roles — wetting agents and rewetting agents — whose optimum molecular structures are actively opposed. Directly actionable for fluff-pulp treatment and core-layer chemistry.（中文：第 5 章建立"表面活性剂→界面吸附→界面自由能→润湿与渗透动力学→宏观吸液性"的完整因果链，并把最易混淆的润湿剂与再润湿剂做了严格区分，直接对应绒毛浆与芯体配方。）

## Summary 摘要

- **Scope.** "The use of surfactants provides one of the most powerful means available to the practitioner for altering absorbency, either to promote it or to suppress it."
- **Definition by function, not by structure.** High interfacial adsorption at low bulk concentration is what makes a molecule a surfactant; not every amphiphile qualifies.
- **CMC is the operating window.** Below CMC, linear monomer activity; near CMC, saturated interfacial adsorption (best free-energy reduction) — saturation adsorption at the liquid–gas interface occurs "at a concentration between one-fifth and one-third of the CMC"; above CMC, micelles ("containing usually between 50 and 300 monomer units") sequester monomer and effective diffusivity drops. Practical dose: "略高于 CMC" so the front concentration stays below CMC without starving the surface.
- **CMC is salt-and-temperature sensitive.** "The CMC of a given ionic surfactant is divided roughly by three for each unit increase in the molality of univalent counterion" — body fluids are high-ion-strength, so effective CMC drops sharply in situ. Nonionics lose solubility with temperature; "at a temperature well below 100 °C ... they precipitate out as giant micelles. This temperature is termed the cloud point."
- **Four adsorption mechanisms.** Amphipathic adsorption (hydrophobe anchored, hydrophile exposed) is the workhorse; ion-pair adsorption "occurs only at very low bulk surfactant concentrations and in the absence of additional electrolyte"; ion exchange swaps surfactant ions for Stern-layer counter-ions "and, by itself, does not effect a change in surface charge at the Stern plane"; cooperative adsorption forms second-layer hemimicelles, and "the surface charge is completely neutralized and then reversed in sign during this step".
- **Two agent classes, opposite optima.** "The properties required for a good rewetting agent are quite different from those for a good wetting agent, and many good wetting agents are poor rewetters, and vice versa." Wetting: small hydrophilic head, high diffusivity — "substances used as wetting agents must diffuse quickly to the interface and therefore branched-chain, medium molecular weight materials are preferred". Rewetting: large hydrophobe + minimally hydrophilic head, nonionic preferred, applied near cloud point, and it must not crystallize on drying — "surfactants which are liquids or amorphous solids when dry make better rewetting agents than those which are crystalline".
- **Branching evidence (Table 3, sodium C14 sulfate series, DIN 53901).** 13/1 = 42 s, 12/2 = 35, 11/3 = 35, 10/4 = 29, 9/5 = 29, 8/6 = 27, 7/7 = 25 (optimum) — symmetric, centrally positioned hydrophilic group wins.
- **Cellulose is a non-customer for wetting agents.** "The advancing contact angle of water against pure cellulose fibers has been measured at 0°"; dry cellulose releases ~10 cal/g on wetting by water. Rewetting agents therefore target residual hydrophobic extractives (resin acids, sizing agents, oils), not the cellulose itself.
- **Self-sizing.** Aged inadequately-defatted pulp deploys residual resin pockets into hydrophobic patches — the classic failure that makes cheap mechanical fluff progressively worse.
- **Competing formulation goals.** Debonding agents block interfibre hydrogen bonding to enable mechanical delamination but reduce hydrophilicity; rewetting agents raise it. The chapter's resolution is cooperative adsorption of ethoxylated quaternary ammonium (alkyl C14–20, EO units n,m = 2–6) with PEO alcohols; phenolic alkyl analogues span C20–38.
- **Compatibility red line.** Mixing anionic and cationic surfactants produces a hydrophobic outer double layer and destroys rewetting — a hard constraint on core/ADL/SAP layer chemistry.
- **Diagnostic relationships.** Wicking-equivalent surface tension σ* = γ₂ₗ·(k_water/k) and dynamic adhesion tension (γSG − γSL)* = γ₂ₗ·(k*/k_water); wetting time log t_w = A' − B'·log C₂.
- **Wicking-consistency thresholds (Hodgson & Berg).** Concentrations at which wicking-equivalent tension converges to equilibrium: SDS ≈ CMC; CTAB ≈ 10×CMC; Triton X-100 > 100×CMC — because TX-100's diffusivity is an order of magnitude below SDS/CTAB.
- **Coverage caveat.** HLB is never defined in this chapter (the text uses the EO/hydrophobe ratio instead), and Table 5's numeric body is OCR-truncated in the extract — both are recorded rather than reconstructed.

## Key Claims 关键论断

- **Dose near CMC, never above it.** Extra surfactant above CMC is spent on micelles that cannot wet — a direct, quantifiable overrun in formulation cost.
- **Design the hydrophile position, not just the chain length.** Central hydrophilic group (C7–C7) beats the straight C14 chain on wetting time; the molecular topology argument is worth more than the carbon count.
- **Don't sell cellulose a wetting agent.** At 0° contact angle the fibre is already saturated; any measured gain is impurity removal. This is the correct basis for judging fluff-pulp pretreatment claims.
- **Rewetting agents are impurity-specific.** Their value is proportional to residual hydrophobic extractives — softwood resin acids vs. hardwood fatty acids/insaponifiables, which are alkali-resistant and therefore persist. This is a defensible price/specification argument in pulp procurement.
- **Self-sizing is a storage-life property, not a batch property.** Pulp that tests well at receipt can degrade with age; a rewrite of acceptance criteria to include aged-plate behaviour is warranted.
- **The debonding/rewetting conflict has a known solution, not a compromise.** Ethoxylated quaternary ammonium plus PEO alcohol, deployed via cooperative adsorption, achieves both — but only if the ion-pair step precedes the hydrophobic one.
- **Never blend anionic with cationic across layers.** The resulting hydrophobic outer bilayer silently kills rewetting; this belongs on the core-layer compatibility checklist.
- **Apply near cloud point; never dry to a crystal.** Both are process-window requirements with direct effect on coating-dryer settings.
- **Verify in situ salt strength.** Because CMC divides roughly by three per unit molality of counterion, the surfactant dose validated in water will not validate in urine-strength brine.
