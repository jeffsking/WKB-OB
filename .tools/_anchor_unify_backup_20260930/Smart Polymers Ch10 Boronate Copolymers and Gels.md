# Smart Polymers Ch10 Boronate Copolymers and Gels

> Source: Smart Polymers — Applications in Biotechnology and Biomedicine, 2nd ed., Ch.10 — *Smart Boronate-Containing Copolymers and Gels at Solid–Liquid Interfaces, Cell Membranes, and Tissues* (Alexander E. Ivanov, Igor Yu. Galaev, Bo Mattiasson; © 2008 Taylor & Francis Group, LLC; body L1–L1634).

## Scope 范围

This chapter introduces a **recognition-driven** smart mechanism that differs sharply from the pure solvent-quality physics of earlier chapters: reversible covalent complex formation between boronate and *cis*-diols (特异性分子识别 + 可逆共价络合). It covers the chemistry of phenylboronic acid (PBA) ionization, the multivalent advantage of polymeric presentation, glucose-selective bidentate chelation, nonenzymatic sugar sensing transduction modes, and biological interfaces (red blood cells, leukocytes, endothelium, mucosa, body-channel plugging).

## Core Arguments 核心论点

**1. Reversible diol complexation is the base chemistry.** "Reversible complex formation of borate and phenylboronate ions with mono- and oligosaccharides in aqueous solutions has been well known for many years" (L91–92).

**2.Polymer presentation creates avidity.** "An inherent property of the reactive polymers is their multipoint (or multivalent) interaction with biological counterparts, which results in much stronger specific binding compared with low-molecular-weight reagents with the same functional groups" (L122–125). Multivalent binding to available diols can become effectively irreversible — e.g. BCC binding to an agarose gel (L1088–1090).

**3. BCCs are acid–base dual-form polyelectrolytes.** "The BCCs are polyelectrolytes, the pendant groups of phenylboronic acid (PBA) being present in both an uncharged trigonal form and a charged boronate form … in a wide range of pH" (L151–153); for NAAPBA copolymers this equilibrium spans pH 6.5–10 (L153–154).

**4. Higher pH ≠ better binding.** "The affinity of ring-substituted PBAs to sugars does not always increase with pH" (L194–198) — 4-bromophenylboronic acid (pKa 8.3) binds glucose best at pH 7.5, above its values at pH 6.5 or 8.5.

**5. Glucose fits bidentate chelation; galactose/mannose do not.** "Allowing for bidentate chelation of glucose by the pendant boronate groups" (L518–520) explains both the high sensitivity of soluble copolymers (measurable phase-transition temperature T_P down to 5.6 µM glucose, L517–520) and glucose-specific *cross-linking* shrinkage of gels, whereas mannose, galactose and fructose cause swelling (L658–659). "Other hexoses, such as D-galactose or D-mannose, do not form bidentate complexes with boronates" (L527–528).

**6. Target: closed-loop insulin delivery.** The goal is "a glucose-responsive polymer gel, containing chemically attached PBA, with the ability to release insulin at high concentrations of glucose" (L297–298) at physiological temperature, pH and ionic strength — stated by the authors as not yet achieved.

**7. Nonenzymatic sensors beat enzymes on stability.** "Glucose sensors utilize the enzyme glucose oxidase; however, these sensors often display poor stability" (L536–537) — whereas "the most effective synthetic ligands for binding glucose in aqueous media are derived from boronic acids" (L582–584).

**8. BCCs act as biomimetic lectins at living surfaces.** They bind glycosylated proteins and lipids, exhibit "lectinlike functions" (L963–964), sterically protect red blood cells, cut leukocyte adhesion by cross-linking the glycocalix (L1343–1345), and serve as "ideal mucoadhesives" (L1417–1418).

## Composition–Property Data 结构–性能数据

**PLL-g-(PEG;PBA) binding to mannan-containing agarose** (Table 10.2, L1052–1068): PEG/lysine ratios of 1:21 / 1:9 / 1:6 / 1:3 / 1:2 with the tabulated degrees of substitution show that **more PEG chains ⇒ more steric repulsion ⇒ less adsorption, even at high PBA content**. Separately, copolymers with PEG/lysine unit ratios of 1:21 and 1:9 reduced adherent IC-21 cells by more than 90% (L1194–1195).

**Pig-urethra plugging, BCC precoat + PVA–borax gel** (Table 10.3, L1488–1491): NAAPBA–DMAA at increasing PBA molar content and NAAPBA–AAm (12 mol%) show a monotonic trend — higher PBA molar content gives more effective complete sealing (L1447–1449).

## Hygiene implication 卫材关联

Genuine transfer value exists in four areas, though every item is biomedical/in-vitro in origin:

1. **Reversibly cross-linked SAP-like networks.** Boronate–diol gelation is instant and reversible; choose a low association constant (PVA–borate ≈ 4 M⁻¹, L1536) so the network is stable in use yet dissolveable by a competing polyol (fructose, 3–5 min at 5 w/v%, L1478–1480). Concept applicable to leak-barrier gels or on-demand-disposable cores. 中文：可逆二醇交联为"用完可解体"芯体提供了化学路径。
2. **Nonenzymatic sugar / wetness sensing in the core.** Replace enzyme strips with boronate gels: linear 1–50 mM (L714–720) or 1.67–16.7 mM electrode readout (L591–593). **Constraint:** response dies above ~10 mM NaCl (L647–651) while urine is roughly 0.1–0.2 M ionic strength — so low-pKa monomers (DDOPBA 7.8) and Na⁺-buffering comonomers are mandatory, not optional (L653–655). This is the single biggest feasibility risk for urine-side sensors.
3. **Sugar-specific mucoadhesives for pads / incontinence devices.** The source names BCCs "ideal mucoadhesives" (L1417–1418) with multivalent glycocalix binding and low cytotoxicity (L1182–1208).
4. **Selectivity-by-sugar as a discriminating indicator.** Opposite gel responses — glucose cross-links (shrinks) while mannose/galactose/fructose swell (L658–659) — plus the ΔT_P vs K_ass mismatch give a library of distinguishable readouts for multianalyte strips.

No claim here translates to product performance numbers; all figures are from the source's own in-vitro models (pig urethra, buffer solutions). 中文：全部为源文体外数据，勿直接外推至产品性能。
