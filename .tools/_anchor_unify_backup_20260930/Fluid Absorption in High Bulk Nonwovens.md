# Fluid Absorption in High Bulk Nonwovens

## One-line Position 一句话定位

Chapter 3 (pp. 93–127) is the most practically actionable chapter in the handbook: it converts "fast and full core" into two measurable numbers (C, Q), proves Capacity is predictable from thickness-per-gram while Rate is not (hence the structural constant K), and supplies the airlaid/needlepunched experimental dataset that directly maps areal density, low-melt ratio, needling intensity and SAP fraction onto product performance.（中文：第 3 章把"吸得快、吸得多"量化为 C 与 Q，证明容量可由每克厚度预测、速率必须引入结构常数 K，并给出可直接指导轻量化与非织造工艺选型的实测数据。）

## Summary 摘要

- **Function-first definition.** The core "exerts the force necessary to pull the liquid in, distribute it within the structure and hold it without releasing under normal external pressure" — pull, distribute, hold. Everything else in the chapter tests one of those three.
- **Two metrics, one solvable.** C (cc/g) is predicted from T/W alone; Q (cc/g·s) is not. "The value of capacity can be monitored and predicted effectively by simply measuring thickness per unit mass and using eq. 1."
- **Rate model and its limits.** Q = (π·γ·r·cosθ·T)/(K·η·W). K is the degree "by which the actual capillary network departs from the ideal"; measured K ≈ 4–10. Stated causes: (1) real webs have pores "of a range of sizes and shapes, which were also not bounded by solid material", (2) capillaries "followed tortuous paths" vs. a straight model capillary, (3) "the fluid diffused in and swelled the fibers" — none of which the model assumes.
- **Areal density is the dominant lever.** 40 → 80 g/m²: C −21%, Q −29%. 40 → 160 g/m²: C −34%, Q −54%. Mechanism: falling T/W and pore radius r under load, plus structural collapse.
- **Thermal bonding validates ES/airlaid as a core platform.** At 80 g/m², 174 °C: the 85/15 blend gives C 23.7 → 20.7 cc/g (36 s → 18 s dwell) at Q 3.12 cc/g·s, while the 70/30 blend gives C 24.7 → 20.0 at Q 1.07 — "the rate in the 85/15 material was more than twice of that in the 70/30 material." Capacity parity with mechanical needling; rate collapses if low-melt share is too high.
- **SAP trade, quantified.** "An increase in the fraction of the superabsorbent (S) caused a great increase in the capacity, as much as 100% with addition of 30% fiber, but a drastic decrease in the rate, from about 6 (cc/g-sec) for web containing 0% superabsorbent to less than 1 (cc/g-sec) for web containing 30%."
- **Saline, not water.** 1% NaCl vs. water on 100 g/m² carded/cross-lapped (NI = 0): cotton C 12.3 → 12.9, Q 0.74 → 0.58; rayon C 10.9 → 11.7, Q 0.88 → 0.73. Higher viscosity and contact angle cut rate; osmotic effect nudges capacity up.
- **Layering needs bonding.** In the pull–push configuration with the polyester side down and no interlayer bond, "no absorbency took place" — the textbook stratification argument fails without an anchoring bond.
- **Needlepunch is a channel-optimising tool.** 1.8 den refined cotton, 1% NaCl, θ 34°: 40 g/m² K ≈ 4.4, 160 g/m² K ≈ 5.4; moving NI 0 → N3 lowers mean K from 5.3 to 4.8, i.e. modest needling improves rate predictability while over-needling chokes channels.
- **Method.** GATS (Gravity Absorption Test System); 1% NaCl standard fluid; EP 12 or 27 gf/cm²; needle depth 10 mm; NI 0–180 needles/cm²; 31.7 cm² sample; areal densities 40–200 g/m².

## Key Claims 关键论断

- **Capacity is measurable before you build; rate is not.** T/W predicts C reliably, so areal density/thickness is the design variable to lock in early. Rate needs empirical K — treat any Q prediction from first principles as upper-bound only.
- **Thinning is the highest-return lever in the chapter.** Every 40 g/m² of core removed pays back in both capacity and rate (40→160 costs 34%/54%); "thin" is not a comfort claim, it is a permeability-and-bulk argument.
- **Low-melt share is a channel-plug dial.** 85/15 vs 70/30 shows capacity surviving but rate dividing by ~3 — the ES/airlaid bonding window sits in the low-teens of low-melt fraction, and pushing strength by more low-melt destroys acquisition speed.
- **SAP belongs in the storage zone, not the acquisition zone.** Doubling capacity at one-sixth the rate makes SAP a reservoir; hydrophilic fibre must do the fast pull and spread, SAP does the hold. Mono-SAP cores are self-defeating on rate.
- **Interlayer bonding is a prerequisite, not an option.** Without it the pull–push stratification yields zero uptake when the hydrophobic layer faces the fluid — laminates need a mechanical or adhesive anchor.
- **Qualify all tests in 1% NaCl.** Water systematically over-states rate; salt content of the fluid is a first-order variable, and osmotic capacity gains can partially offset viscosity losses.
- **Needlepunch has a sweet spot, then a wall.** Modest NI lowers K (faster, more predictable); excessive NI chokes the very channels it creates. Optimise per areal density.
