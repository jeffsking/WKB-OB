# Ch28: Osmotic Dehydration — SAP Physics Bridge

> **Source:** Lewicki, P. & Lenart, J., in Mujumdar (ed.), *Handbook of Industrial Drying*, 4th ed., CRC Press, 2014. Ch.28, pp.663–679.

---

## 1. The SAP Connection SAP关联

This chapter is the **single most direct bridge to superabsorbent-polymer (SAP) science** in the Handbook. The osmotic pressure framework, semipermeable membrane analogy, and electrolyte ion effect are exactly the physics family of SAP swelling/deswelling governed by Flory-Rehner equilibrium and Donnan osmotic pressure.

---

## 2. Key Equations 关键公式

### Chemical Potential of Water 水的化学势

$$\mu_w = \mu^0_w + RT \ln a_w \quad \text{(Eq 28.1)}$$

where $a_w$ = water activity

### Osmotic Pressure 渗透压

$$\Pi V_w = RT \ln a_w \quad \text{(Eq 28.2)}$$

$$\Pi = 4.6063 \times 10^5 \cdot T \cdot \ln a_w \quad \text{(Eq 28.3)}$$

**SAP transfer:** This is the swelling/driving pressure set by the solvent chemical-potential difference. Inside an SAP, this is the swelling pressure of the crosslinked polyelectrolyte network.

### Electrolyte Ion Effect 电解质离子效应

> "Osmotic pressure is related to molar mass of the solute; the smaller the mass, the higher will be the pressure at the same concentration. **Electrolytes show higher osmotic pressure than nonelectrolytes** because each ion affects the chemical potential of a solvent." (p.664)

**SAP transfer:** This is the seed of Donnan equilibrium — mobile ions distributed across a charged, semipermeable matrix raise Π. It is precisely why SAP (a fixed-charge polyelectrolyte) swells in water but **deswells in saline**: the fixed –COO⁻ groups plus counterions create a Donnan osmotic pressure that is suppressed by external salt.

## 3. Cell = SAP Analogy 细胞-SAP类比

| Cell Structure | SAP Analogue |
|---|---|
| Plasmalemma (7.5–10 nm protein-lipid layer, water-permeable, selectively permeable) | Crosslinked gel matrix network |
| Vacuole with osmotic pressure pushing against cell wall | Gel interior with swelling pressure |
| Turgor pressure (difference between cell and surroundings) | Swelling pressure equilibrium |
| **Plasmolysis** (hypertonic solution → water loss → membrane detachment) | **Deswelling** (external salt/load → volume collapse) |
| Countercurrent flux (water-out dominates, then solute-in) | Fast initial uptake then ion exchange/leakage |

> "The plasmalemma is a protein–lipid layer... It is 7.5–10 nm thick, permeable to water, and selectively permeable to other substances." (p.665)

> "A cell placed in a hypertonic solution (osmotic pressure higher than that of the cell) will lose water... This process is called plasmolysis." (p.666)

---

## 4. Microbiology Threshold 微生物阈值

> "Most bacteria, yeasts, and moulds do not proliferate at P > 12.7 MPa, P > 17.3 MPa, and P > 30.1 MPa, respectively." (p.664)

**Cross-link:** The same osmotic-pressure number that drives SAP uptake also sets the antimicrobial window of a hypertonic brine — useful for preservative-free stabilization.

---

## 5. Energy Advantage 节能优势

> "Osmotic dehydration is distinctive in that water is removed from the product **without undergoing the phase change**. It offers a considerable potential for energy saving in comparison with convection drying... convection drying needs some 5 MJ/kg of evaporated water, which is at least twice as much as is needed in osmotic dehydration." (p.674–675)

**SAP transfer:** The "no phase change" advantage is exactly why SAP is so efficient at absorbing liquid water — it skips the vaporization latent heat entirely.

### Water-Binding Forces 水结合力

> "Osmotic dehydration done for 0.5 h led to a **sixfold decrease of water-binding forces** at the surface of apple... Water removal from the tissue by osmosis shows a much stronger effect on water-binding forces than by the convection drying done to the same final water content." (p.678)

**Hygiene transfer:** The osmotic route reorganizes the water-binding structure differently — relevant to understanding how an SAP-bonded core "holds" water vs a thermally-dried web.
