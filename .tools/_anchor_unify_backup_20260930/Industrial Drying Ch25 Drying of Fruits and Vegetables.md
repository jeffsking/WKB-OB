# Drying of Fruits and Vegetables

> Source: Mujumdar (ed.), Handbook of Industrial Drying, 4th ed., CRC Press, 2014, Ch.25, pp.603–630 (Jayaraman & Das Gupta). End page estimated from chapter block order; page numbers derived from PDF-page − 21 — spot-check against print.

## Chapter Overview 章节概览

Survey of fruit/vegetable dehydration: pretreatments (alkaline dip, sulfiting, blanching), an encyclopedic inventory of drying hardware (solar, hot-air, fluidized-bed, explosion-puffing, foam, microwave, spray, drum, freeze, osmotic, heat-pump, ultrasonic), closing on the chemistry of quality loss. The analytical spine is the water-activity (a_w) framework plus glass-transition (Tg) concept, used to rationalize browning, lipid oxidation, pigment loss, shrinkage, and microbiological stability.

## Key Viewpoints 关键观点

### 1. Hot-air drying causes irreversible textural collapse 热风干燥不可逆结构坍缩

> "The problem of hot air drying, which is still the most economical and widely used method for dehydrating piece-form vegetables and fruits, is the irreversible damage to the texture, leading to shrinkage, slow cooking, and incomplete rehydration." (p.625)

The cheapest technology destroys cell-wall architecture — the canonical shrinkage/case-hardening mechanism and the central quality-vs-rate tension of the chapter.

### 2. Water activity is the master stability variable 水活度主导稳定性

> "It is defined as the ratio of the vapor pressure of water P in the food to the vapor pressure of pure water P0 at the same temperature (a_w = P/P_0)." (p.626)

> "microorganisms cannot grow in the dehydrated food system when the water activity range is less than or equal to 0.6–0.7, but other reactions, enzymic and nonenzymic (e.g., lipid oxidation, NEB, etc.) that cause change in color, flavor, and stability continue during processing and storage." (p.626)

Killing microbes ≠ stabilizing chemistry: the microbial cut-off is hard, but NEB peaks at a_w 0.4–0.6 and lipid oxidation has a minimum at 0.3–0.5 then rises (p.627).

### 3. BET monolayer = the safe storage moisture 单分子层最佳水分

> "It has been suggested that the optimal amount of water for long-term storage corresponds in most dehydrated foods to the Brunauer–Emmett–Teller (BET) monolayer value." (p.629)

Not "drier is always better": a single optimal residual moisture exists, and it is material-specific (some items most stable at zero moisture, others at the monomolecular value, p.629).

### 4. Glass transition governs collapse, stickiness, caking 玻璃化转变

> "Glass transition is a second-order phase transition that occurs over the temperature range at which amorphous solid materials are transformed into viscous, liquid state [89]." (p.627)

Above Tg, amorphous low-moisture solids collapse, stick and cake — identical physics to polymer Tg; critical Tg values set process and storage limits (p.627).

### 5. Rate-quality trade-off 速率-质量权衡

> "In general, rapid drying retained a greater amount of ascorbic acid than slow drying." (p.620)

Faster drying improves heat-labile retention but worsens shrinkage; slower drying reverses both. The recurring engineering decision.

### 6. Pectin as structural determinant 果胶决定复水

> "Levi et al. [84] observed that pectin, one of the major cell wall and intercellular tissue components, played a significant role in the rehydration capacity of dehydrated fruits." (p.626)

Rehydration is a network-integrity problem, not merely a diffusion problem — pectin is the polysaccharide load-bearing matrix, analogous to a crosslinked hydrogel network.

## Research Method 研究方法

Review/synthesis chapter (98 references) integrating tabulated peer-reviewed food-engineering data: ascorbic acid/carotene vs drying treatment, sulfite and storage effects, predicted-vs-actual shelf life as a function of a_w and T (Tables 25.7–25.12), chlorophyll degradation kinetics (Fig 25.6–25.8). No original experiment by the authors.

## Strengths and Weaknesses 优劣评估

- Strengths: unifies disparate quality defects under a_w and Tg — a transferable conceptual operating system; shelf-life tables predicted vs actual within ~10%.
- Weaknesses: substrate-specific constants (monolayer a_w 0.32–0.43 for onion; carotenoid optimum at 0.43 a_w) must not be ported numerically to pulp/SAP; Table 25.9 numeric columns partially jumbled in the OCR source; sulfite dependency ("no practical substitute for SO2", p.623) has no analogue in non-food drying.

## Hygiene Implication 卫材关联

Relevance: ★★☆☆☆ as substrate (living, enzyme-active plant tissue vs industrial polymer pulp/SAP), ★★★★☆ as physics — the a_w / sorption-isotherm / monolayer / Tg toolkit transfers; the biochemical pathways (NEB, enzymatic browning, carotenoid oxidation) and all numeric thresholds do not. No "diaper application" is invented for carrots.

- Residual-moisture microbial spec: adopt the a_w ≤ 0.6 microbial-stability principle (p.626) — measure pulp/SAP EMC–a_w isotherm (Ch24 method) and set received-moisture so equilibrium a_w stays below the microbial cut-off.
- BET-monolayer target instead of "driest possible" (p.629): determine the substrate's monolayer and target that moisture, avoiding brittle/case-hardened web and re-moistening shock.
- Avoid Tg-collapse in dried hygroscopic coatings and SAP-containing layers (p.627): keep process/storage temperature below the material's Tg; plasticizing additives lower Tg and must be accounted for.
