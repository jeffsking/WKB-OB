# Fibers and Fibrous Materials

## One-line Position 一句话定位

Chapter 6 (pp. 216–249; Ludwig Rebenfeld, TRI) is the materials catalog plus the one design law that governs all of it: because 'the fiber surfaces constitute the pore walls' in capillary sorption, absorbency is a surface-and-geometry problem — and the chapter proves that special-purpose fibres (alloy rayon, modified cellulose, deep-groove polyester, hollow chicken-feather) win by engineering the pore wall and pore geometry rather than by changing the polymer.（中文：第 6 章确立"毛细吸收中纤维表面即孔壁"这一设计律，并用合金粘胶、改性纤维素、深沟槽涤纶、中空鸡毛等特种纤维证明——赢在表面与几何，而非换树脂。）

## Summary 摘要

- **Fibrous material, defined.** A substrate of fibres in which 'the spaces between the component fibers, referred to as pores, play a key role in their physical properties', with porosity typically 0.6–0.9 or higher.
- **Two sorption modes.** Bulk adsorption/absorption into the fibre, and capillary sorption between fibres; the latter dominates nonwovens and is set by wetting and surface energy, since 'the fiber surfaces constitute the pore walls'.
- **The dual-dependence principle.** Performance = fibre properties × fibre geometry. Fibre property taxonomy: geometric (length, cross-section, crimp), physical (density, thermal, optical, electrical, surface roughness/friction), mechanical (tension, compression, torsion, bending, shear), chemical (acids, alkalies, oxidation/reduction, heat, sorption anisotropy).
- **Cellulose family.** Cotton fibrils sit at a defined ~23° helix angle in concentric sheets; high crystallinity makes cotton effectively cross-linked, so wet strength can exceed dry. Viscose rayon (cellulose xanthate → acid bath) has far lower crystallinity and weakens sharply when wet. Lyocell uses aqueous NMMO and is the environmentally preferable route. Wood pulp fibre (2–3 µm long) is itself a composite — 'the cellulosic fibrils are effectively dispersed in a lignin matrix'.
- **Synthetic geometries as substitutes for chemistry.** Hydrophobic polyester absorbs well anyway because 'it is possible to produce small diameter (fine) fibers and also to produce fibers with irregular or noncircular cross-sectional shapes. Both these fiber characteristics contribute to the formation of effective interfiber capillaries'.
- **Specialised absorbent fibres.** Alloy rayon (≈2× capacity of standard rayon; PVP 35–40 wt% → 4.68–4.69 cm³/g vs 3.06); modified cellulose (CMC #1 16.0, CMC #2 21.0 vs plain cellulose 1.0 — in a centrifugal retention test); deep-groove polyester (capillary distribution + storage in grooves); superabsorbent fibres (gel retention under external pressure); cross-linked CMC spun as CLD/Aqualon; PLA corn fibre (Tm 175 °C, biodegradable); chicken-feather fibre (~6 µm × 8 mm, hollow) with distinctive wet-collapse resistance.
- **The fibre-form argument.** Superabsorbent polymers are better as fibres than as powder/granules because they can be directly built into woven/nonwoven sheets; powder 'would need a support system for an even distribution in a sheet'.
- **Wetting thresholds (perceptual).** Wool ~19%, cotton 11%, nylon 8%, standard acrylic 5%, polyester 2% moisture regain before dampness is perceived — the physics basis of 'dry feeling'.
- **Data coverage caveat.** Tables 2–3 of the chapter and several chemical equations were lost in OCR; only values recoverable from the extract are carried over.

## Key Claims 关键论断

- **Buy the surface, not the resin.** Because fibre walls *are* the pore walls, surface energy and roughness dominate absorption; fibre chemistry matters mostly through wettability.
- **Density and geometry substitute for hydrophilicity.** Polyolefin and PET absorb well through fine denier and non-circular cross-section — the pore network, not the polymer, is the absorber.
- **Wet strength inverts with crystallinity.** Cotton stiffens when wet; viscose collapses. For wet-wipes and core layers, wet strength governs processability, not dry strength.
- **Cross-link density must be judged in the load regime it will be used.** Free-swell test results overstate capacity for a compressed product; procurement should require under-load data (ALP), exactly as the SAP chapter demands.
- **Fibre-form superabsorbents are the sheet-friendly SAP.** Fibres self-distribute in a web; powder SAP needs a carrier system. Fibre-form SAP removes a formulation dependency.
- **Alloy rayon is the quantified alternative to pulp+SAP.** Doubling capacity over standard rayon, and reaching 4.69 cm³/g at 35–40% PVP, makes it a credible partial SAP substitute in the core — worth a supplier conversation.
- **Modified CMC wins precisely where SAP loses.** 16–21% retained under centrifugal load vs 1.0% for cellulose — retention under compression, not free swell, is the relevant metric.
- **Burst/expanded fibres solve gel blocking.** Surface area is multiplied and burst fibres 'did not exhibit the gel blocking effect' — a structural answer to a chemical failure mode.
- **Chicken-feather fibre is a bulk-preservation case study.** Hollow keratin retains bulk when saturated; the design lesson is that collapse resistance — not capacity — is what keeps a core working under load.
- **Denier is the bulk dial.** Lower linear density → higher specific air volume and stronger capillarity, at the cost of processing difficulty and price; a three-axis trade for core spec.
