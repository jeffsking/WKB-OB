--- [[BM Urine Indicator Concept（尿+便双指示概念）]]  (7 句)
   • Signal selection compares urine and infant BM across seven properties. pH is a WEAK discriminator (ranges overlap); titratable acidity is GOOD (buffer capacity, not pH); bile pigment is BEST (binary presence); active enzyme is BEST but needs an aqueous-coated substrate; rheology (urine ≈1 mPa·s vs BM 3–60 Pa·s) is GOOD and drives sampling geometry; temperature is NONE; head-space volatiles are BLOCKED by the granular.
   • Mechanism options are ranked by cost per unit of certainty: **A titratable-acidity lane** (buffered halochromic hot melt, ~US$0.4/1,000, Phase 1, sensitivity ~65–80 %, misses low-VFA formula stool), **B bile-pigment mordant lane** (quaternised cationic polymer as an anion-exchange lane, ~US$0.3/1,000, binary marker independent of feeding mode, but meconium and firm stool release little liquor) and **C protease-cleavage film** (crosslinked gelatin 12–20 µm over a dark printed field, ~US$2.5/1,000
   • The architecture is sample → isolate → react → read: a 10 mm hydrophilic wick at topsheet level in the rear zone; an 18 gsm PE barrier under the wick to stop SAP stealing the sample water and to keep the granular ≥20 mm away in MD; a 90 mm MD travel to the two hot-melt lanes; and read-out under an unprinted clear channel in the backsheet artwork at the rear waist. Two jobs are kept separate — the pH ladder is a load gauge ("change at the right time"), the BM lanes override it ("change now").
   • Cost model per 1,000 diapers totals US$0.54 (ladder 0.07, lanes A+B 0.10, ADL tail 0.23, barrier film 0.14, clear channel 0.00), i.e. ≈US$0.00054/diaper or 0.6–0.9 % of a typical US$0.06–0.09 ex-works diaper. Phase-1 capex is US$38–60 k; annualised running cost at 300 M diapers/year is ≈US$108 k. The deck is explicit that this is a claim and complaint-avoidance project, not a cost-out project.
