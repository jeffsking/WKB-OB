### The trap that decides the whole design

The odour sorbent and the indicator want the same molecules.

Intermediate zeolites are chosen precisely because they have high capacity for amine-type odours over a 700–800 m$^{2}$/g surface, and cyclodextrin complexes the same small organics. Any indicator that reads BM from the head-space — an ammonia-sensitive dye, a VFA-sensitive dye reading vapour — is being starved by the very additive that created the problem.

Design rule: the BM indicator must read the liquid phase, by direct contact, and must be physically decoupled from both the odour granular and the SAP.

### A pH-only BM stripe is a coin toss

Roughly half of formula-fed stool and most late-day urine land in the same pH window. Setting the trip point tight enough to reject urine loses breastfed stool; loosening it turns every wet diaper into a false BM alarm.

## So pH does one job only

Keep the classic pH hot-melt stripe as the wetness / load gauge — it is already the cheapest reliable element in the diaper. Give the BM job to a marker urine simply does not contain.

MECHANISM OPTIONS

# Three ways to read a BM — ranked by cost per unit of certainty

![A\n](2/Oy0YFe4o874ND9Udefofxe/ppt_3867e2b60070d7dc3f413086d0b0036e_a78cf93e26e9e9eaa6d24205f67419bd001a693ee6c0644e/tmp_zip_img/430f0485-c68a-4098-b646-435716bfcecb.png)
### A Titratable-acidity lane

Buffered halochromic hot melt. A weak-base buffer holds the dye at pH 6.5. Only a sample with real buffer capacity — stool liquor at 100+ mM lactate and VFA — can titrate through it.

+ Zero new process. Runs on a second hot-melt applicator.

- Misses low-VFA formula stool. Sensitivity ~65~80%.

≈ US$0.4 / 1,000

Phase 1

## B Bile-pigment mordant lane

A quaternised cationic polymer (poly-DADMAC or chitosan salt) laid down as an anion-exchange lane. Bile pigments and bile salts are anionic: they are captured and concentrated into an intense amber-olive stain. Urine carries none.

+ Binary marker, independent of feeding mode.
Chromatographic concentration makes a faint sample visible.

- Meconium and very firm stool release little liquor.

≈ US$0.3 / 1,000

* ≈ US$2.5 / 1,000

## C

### Protease-cleavage film

Crosslinked gelatin (genipin or transglutaminase, 12–20 µm) laid over a dark printed field. Faecal trypsin and chymotrypsin digest the film and reveal the field. Urine has no active protease, so it cannot open it.

+ Highest specificity available at low material cost.

- Aqueous coating $\rightarrow$ off-line patch + cut-and-place.

Kinetics 10-30 min.

≈ US$2.5 / 1,000

Phase 3 / premium

Recommendation: run A and B together as two adjacent lanes on the same hot-melt station. Two independent chemistries, one applicator, no registration. Two lanes dark = change now; one lane dark = suspect. Hold C in reserve for a premium SKU.

SYSTEM CONCEPT

# The architecture: sample, isolate, react, read

## 1

### SAMPLE

A 10 mm hydrophilic wick sits at topsheet level in the rear zone, behind the urine target. BM pressed against it by the buttock delivers liquor by contact, not by wicking.

## 2

### ISOLATE

An 18 gsm PE barrier under the wick stops the SAP from stealing the sample water and keeps the odour granular ≥ 20 mm away in MD.

## 3

### REACT

The liquor travels 90 mm MD along the wick to two hot-melt lanes: acidity lane (A) and mordant lane (B).

## 4

### READ

Both lanes sit under an unprinted clear channel in the backsheet artwork, at the rear waist where Mom looks first.

## Two jobs, two readouts — do not merge them

Job 1 — Change at the right time

A three-segment pH ladder along MD. Segment 1 trips at insult 1, segment 3 only as the core approaches its design load. Mom reads a fuel gauge, not a binary.

Job 2 — Change now

The two BM lanes at the rear waist. These override the ladder: a BM event demands a change regardless of how much urine capacity is left.

### Clearance rules against the odour granular and the SAP

Keep the odour granular ≥ 20 mm MD clear of the sampling head, and never in the same z-layer as the wick — a 700–800 m$^{2}$/g sorbent sitting against the wick will strip the very VFA and pigment the lanes read.

The barrier under the wick is there to stop the SAP taking the sample water. It does not stop urine reaching the head — rejecting urine is the chemistry's job, not the geometry's.

Do not print graphics over the reading channel. A standard TiO₂ / CaCO₃-loaded backsheet at 15–18 gsm will swallow the signal; leave the lane unprinted.

FORMULATION

# Material composition 1 — the wetness ladder

Halochromic hot melt, applied at ≈30 g/m$^{2}$ as a 2 mm fine line. This is proven, commodity chemistry — the point is to spend nothing here so the budget goes to the BM lanes.

|Component|wt %|Function|Selection note|
|---|---|---|---|
|Ethylene / alkyl-acrylate / acrylic-acid terpolymer — or sulfonated polyester|25 – 35|Water-sensitive film former|Terpolymer for tank robustness; sulfonated polyester for faster wet-out|
|Hydrogenated C5 or C5/C9 tackifier|25 – 35|Anchorage to the backsheet film|Low-colour grade only — yellowing masks the dry state|
|PEG 4000–8000 / polyoxyethylene wax|15 – 25|Hydrophilic channel; sets response time|Raise to shorten time-to-colour; too high and it pre-trips in humidity|
|Nonionic surfactant, HLB < 20|2 – 5|Insult wet-out|Target visible change in under 30 s|
|pH-adjusting acid — isostearic, azelaic or 12-hydroxystearic|3 – 8|Holds the dye in its acid (yellow) form when dry|This, not the dye, is what sets the trip point|
|Bromophenol blue, or BPB / bromocresol green blend|0.05 – 0.3|Yellow → blue across pH 3.0 – 4.6|Patent-precedented at 0.01 – 0.5 wt%; stay at the low end|

Ladder trick, at no extra cost: print three short dashes instead of one long stripe. Same glue mass, same applicator, same registration-free continuous pass — but Mom now reads a load gauge rather than a yes/no, which is what "change at the right time" actually requires.

### Set the segment positions from simulation, not from the drawing

Each segment should sit just ahead of the MD saturation front at insults 1, 2 and 3 for the target wearer. You already have that curve from the three-insult study on the 385 × 110 core — take the MD position where local saturation crosses ≈60 % and place the segment 8–12 mm in front of it, so the dash trips as the front arrives rather than after it has passed. Because the ladder reads distribution, it also becomes a free field diagnostic: if segment 3 never trips across a returns sample, your MD distribution is short of the design intent and the front-zone SAP blend is over-absorbing at the target.

VALIDATION PROTOCOL

# Proving it before you cut steel

## Bench simulants

Breastfed BM simulant — 78 % water, CMC / Carbopol to Bristol 6 (3-8 Pa·s at 10 s⁻$^{1}$), lactate 60 mM + acetate 60 mM, pH 5.0, bilirubin 1.5 mg/g, porcine pancreatin 1.5 mg/g, mucin 1 %.

Formula-fed BM simulant — 72% water, Bristol 4-5 (20-60 Pa·s), total VFA 45 mM, pH 6.8, bilirubin 0.8 mg/g, pancreatin 1.0 mg/g, calcium-palmitate soaps 3 %.

Urine challenge — synthetic urine at pH 5.5 / 6.2 / 7.5, 60 mL insults, plus urease-aged urine at 4 h (pH = 8.3).

### Then real stool, then real babies

Panel A — 40 fresh anonymised infant stool samples, 20 breastfed / 20 formula-fed, applied at 5 g to the sampling head under 2 kPa load at 37 °C. Record time-to-ΔE-15 and final ΔE per lane. Panel B — 60-infant in-home use test, 30 / 30, control arm = current odour-controlled product with no BM lane. Primary endpoint: median BM-to-change latency. Secondary: erythema score at day 14 and caregiver-reported confidence in the readout.

## Acceptance criteria

$\Delta$E*ab on BM insult

≥ 15 within 90 s

$\Delta$E*ab on any urine challenge

≤ 5 at 4 h

False positive rate

< 2 %

False negative, breastfed

< 10 %

False negative, formula-fed

< 20% — state it, don't hide it

Accelerated ageing

40 °C / 75% RH, 12 weeks, $\Delta$E ≤ 3

Humid pre-trip

38 °C / 90% RH, 8 h, no change

Colour transfer to skin

none, wet-rub test

## The test everyone forgets

Run the whole matrix on a diaper that actually contains the odour granular, at full dose, aged.

A lane validated on a granular-free construction proves nothing about this product. The sorbent is the competing reagent: it will pull VFA and bile salts out of the sample if the clearance is wrong, and its capacity changes over shelf life because zeolite and activated carbon keep adsorbing airborne organics in storage unless hermetically packed.

So test three ages — fresh, 6 months, 12 months — and two granular doses. If the lane response drifts with granular age, your clearance geometry is the problem, not the chemistry.
