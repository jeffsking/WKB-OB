# Cross-linked Cellulose and Its Derivatives

## One-line Position 一句话定位

Chapter 7 (pp. 250–299) is the cellulose superabsorbent playbook: it treats DS and cross-link density as the two control variables, walks through cross-linked CMC, HEC, phosphonoalkylcellulose, sulfonated/oxidised cellulose, microfibrillated cellulose and lignin-bearing CTMP, and ends by unifying them under Donnan swelling theory — with commercial anchors at Hercules (Aqualon/CLD) and Kimberly-Clark (Bernardin phosphate fibre).（中文：第 7 章把取代度 DS 与交联密度作为两大控制变量，系统给出交联纤维素类超吸水材料的工艺-性能-理论闭环，并附 Hercules 与 Kimberly-Clark 的产业化案例。）

## Summary 摘要

- **The framework.** DS fixes the solubility ceiling, cross-link density fixes the swelling ceiling; together they define whether a cellulose derivative is a superabsorbent at all. Both must be tuned jointly.
- **Cross-linked CMC — the workhorse.** Etherification with monochloroacetic acid (up to 8 mol/mol cellulose) plus neutralisation with '0.5 mol caustic/mol cellulose to insure attainment of a DS (carboxyalkyl substituents) of about 0.4 or above'. Cross-linkers: epichlorohydrin (~0.5 mol/mol), dichloro-2-propanol (one-step dry), formaldehyde or glutaraldehyde (two-step wet). Equivalent product at 60 °C × 10 h or 80 °C × 3 h; reflectance brightness 75–80.
- **Low-DS burst fibres.** 'The swollen fibers are fixed by either forming into a paste and heating on a roll dryer ... or freeze drying. With the fibers mechanically fixed in this state, the resulting drying forces cause the fibers to burst' — multiplying wettable area and, critically, removing gel blocking.
- **Tampon/composite use.** Karmazyk: cross-linked CMC + conventional fibre blends, DS 0.4–2.0 with 5–40% of carboxyls as free acid; optimal absorption ~25 min by the 5 cm³ syringe method in 0.9% NaCl. Layered structure is the standard way to split fast uptake from high capacity.
- **HEC.** Hydroxyethyl cellulose etherified with ethylene oxide or chloroethanol; normally water-soluble, made superabsorbent by cross-linking or by grafting polyacrylamide, PMMA, PDA or sodium polyacrylate.
- **Phosphonoalkylcellulose.** Urea-phosphate method — fibres saturated with urea + orthophosphoric acid and reacted 'at near 160 °C for about 20 minutes'; ~2 wt% P preferred; 0.015–0.110 mol cross-link units/mol cellulose; acid→salt form ~5× absorbency, refining ~3×. Then the pivot: 'if the refined fibers are reconverted to the acid form, they retain much of the increased absorbency ... This discovery means that the product can have both absorbency and ion-exchange properties which complement one another.' Refining pH 5.5–8 for personal-use items.
- **Sulfonated / oxidised cellulose.** Arai–Goda sulfated ester cross-linked at DS 0.2 still absorbs well in deionised water; oxidised-sulfonated cellulose reaches 8 g/g at DS 0.2–0.5 (hydroxy-sulfonic).
- **Microfibrillated cellulose (MFC).** 'At least the outermost secondary walls are essentially completed disintegrated to microfibrillar form. This involves beating to a Canadian Standard Freeness (CSF) of less than 50' at 1.5–6.0% slurry solids, then freeze-drying and cross-linking (glutaraldehyde, ZnCl₂). Porous plate: 10 g/g absorbency (35.5 cm head, 1% NaCl); XRMFC 16 g/cc absorbency / 8 g/g retention at 0.4 g/cc density; XFMFC 10 g/cc / 3 g/g; FMFC 6 g/cc / 5 g/g; pore-formed MFC 15 g/g / 6–7 g/g retention.
- **Lignin-bearing CTMP.** WRV 131, drip capacity 5.9 g/g, wet compressibility 7.0 cc/g; absorption rate 3–4 ml/s after modification (Eriksson et al.), with AlCl₃ + sodium metasilicate depositing a porous surface layer.
- **Grafting — the performance leap.** 'A partially hydrolyzed polyacrylamide (p-PAM) grafted HEC gave the best absorbency properties of the above modified materials. The water absorbency was 3000 g/g and for a saline solution (0.85% NaCl) the absorbency was 270 g/g.' Cyanethylated pulp peaks at DS 0.35, ≈2× unmodified pulp.
- **Structure–mechanism.** Marchessault's 'constrained paracrystalline gels' describe grafted superabsorbent fibres, held together by weak mechanical forces.
- **Theory.** Donnan osmotic pressure, opposing polymer-network elastic resistance, governs swelling ('almost completely governed by this mechanism'); Lucas–Washburn bridges molecular swelling to macroscopic rate.
- **Benchmark gap.** Cellulose route 10–25 g/g (5–10 g/g in brine) vs. synthetic SAP 300–600 g/g (20–50 g/g in brine) — the reason commercial cores are pulp + SAP composites, typically 80/20 to 90/10.
- **Process/cost.** Bleached pulp is a low-value by-product base, but etherification/cross-linking/phosphonation add process cost and capital; cross-linked CMC can partly integrate with existing paper lines, whereas phosphonation and burst-fibre routes need dedicated equipment.
- **Regulatory watch.** Formaldehyde cross-linking is tightly restricted in infant products in many markets (prefer epichlorohydrin or glutaraldehyde); phosphonation uses ZnCl₂ catalysis and raises heavy-metal residue limits; free-acid CMC needs long-term skin-contact safety assessment.
- **Data coverage caveat.** The chapter's chemical equations (Eq. 8, 9, 12) and Fig. 12 were captured only as equation numbers in the OCR — structures are described from the text, not reconstructed.

## Key Claims 关键论断

- **DS is the binary switch.** Below ~0.4 the material dissolves and is useless as a sheet; at ~0.4 it is insoluble and highly absorbent. DS is the single most consequential specification to demand from a supplier.
- **Cross-link density has to be negotiated against load.** It buys strength and insolubility at the price of capacity; because a real core is compressed, the optimum shifts higher than free-swell testing implies.
- **Burst-fibre is a structural fix for gel blocking.** No chemical change is needed — fixing the swollen state mechanically multiplies wettable area and removes the blocking failure mode.
- **pH control and absorbency can live in one fibre.** The free-acid phosphate form gives both ion exchange and high absorbency — a genuine dual-function option for feminine-care and medical dressings, not two competing additives.
- **MFC is the low-chemistry answer.** High beating plus freeze-drying plus light cross-linking reaches 10–16 g/cc with minimal chemical load — the strongest fit for wet-wipes where residue compliance matters.
- **Grafting is the bridge to synthetic SAP performance.** 3000 g/g in water on a cellulose backbone is the highest figure in the chapter; it is the natural place to look before committing fully to polyacrylate SAP.
- **The cellulose route loses on number, wins on cost and traceability.** 10–25 g/g vs 300–600 g/g justifies the 80/20–90/10 composite; but cellulose inputs sit on a paper-mill value chain, which is strategically attractive where pulp procurement already exists.
- **Formaldehyde is a compliance risk, not a process choice.** Given infant-product restrictions, epichlorohydrin or glutaraldehyde should be the default cross-linker in any formulation that touches this space.
- **Raw-material grade propagates into performance.** Softwood/hardwood, bleached/unbleached, CTMP/Kraft all shift the achievable DS and cross-link density — this belongs in the pulp acceptance criteria, not in the formulation spec.
- **The Donnan lens is the unifying model.** All these routes swell by the same osmotic mechanism; when comparing suppliers, compare salt-strength behaviour (it scales the Donnan driving force) rather than free-water capacity.
