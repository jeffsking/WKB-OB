# Chapter 14 Speciality Polyamide and Polyester Yarns 特种聚酰胺与聚酯纱线

> Source: Manufactured Fibre Technology, Ch.14, pp.360–398.
> Author of chapter: T. Manabe. 本章作者：T. Manabe。

## Summary 摘要

**Speciality yarns 特种纱线** are obtained either from chemically modified polymers by melt spinning, or by physical modification and dedicated equipment (p.360). The family covered is deep-dyeable and cationic-dyeable, **antistatic 抗静电**, **flame-retardant 阻燃**, **hollow 中空**, **microporous 微孔**, **shaped cross-section 异形截面** and **microfibre 超细纤维**. The chapter is explicit that successful commercialization of a new variety depends on **technical capability and favourable market conditions alike** (pp.360–361), then works along three lines: raw-material modification, polymerization/spinning processes, and **rheology 流变学**.

## Differential Dyeability 差别化染色 14.2

1. **End-group chemistry drives dyeing** (p.362): "The amino end groups in nylon 6 have a predominant effect on dye pick-up when dyed with acid dyes". Table 14.1 maps amino end-group content of **10–130 eq/t** to shades from light through ultra-deep.
2. **Process windows are shared** (p.369): "except for the type and amount of stabilizer, light, ultra-deep and cationic dyeable nylon polymers can be produced under very similar polymerization temperature and total vapour pressure". 改性成本低、产业化友好。
3. Recipe calculation example (p.364): stabilizer dosing from end-group balance — "the sebacic acid concentration is thus 47.39 × 202.25 × 10⁻⁶ × 100 × 0.90, i.e. 0.8626 wt % of caprolactam".
4. **Differential dyeing 差别化染色** (p.370): "Differential dyeing, using the modified nylons… is possible using the following routes" — mixing yarns of different amino end-group levels, or acid- and cationic-dyeable yarns, gives multi-colour effects in one bath.
5. The source concedes that **acid-dyeable PET** was not yet commercialized and its treatment stays at literature level (p.373).

## Antistatic Yarns 抗静电纱线 14.3

Statement of the problem (p.374): "synthetic fibres are poor conductors… create problems related to electrostatic charge". Three routes (p.374): "Chemical modification… Physical blending… Electrical conducting materials such as carbon, metal or metal oxide powders are added". Chemical bonding gives the best wash durability, physical blending the worst, and conductive particles need **conjugated bicomponent filament spinning** to retain strength (p.377). For PET the cited routes are block poly(ether-ester) modification and sheath-core structures.

## Flame Retardant Yarns 阻燃纱线 14.4

Route definition (p.380): "by introduction of phosphorus-containing compounds… halogen-containing compounds… using aromatic raw materials for producing wholly aromatic polyester or polyamide". Table 14.5 (p.381) reports **LOI 0.28** for a wholly aromatic type (trademark cited in source as Xfire™) against **0.21** for ordinary polyester — 本质阻燃 vs 添加型阻燃的分野。

## Microgrooves, Microvoids, Microcraters 微沟槽・微空隙・微火山口 14.5

**Microgroove 微沟槽** (p.383) is formed by incorporating a soluble additive followed by **alkali weight reduction 碱减量**, leaving elongated surface channels. The source notes these examples are drawn mainly from Japanese producers, with no general process window or cost data given (p.383).

## Conjugated Microfibre and Shaped Cross-Sections 共轭超细与异形截面 14.6–14.7

- **Conjugated bicomponent 共轭双组分** configurations — **island-in-sea 岛-in-海** and **radial/split-pie 径向瓣** — are the two routes to conjugated microfibre, made spinnable and separable by bicomponent design (14.6, p.377).
- **Shaped cross-sections** (p.387): "the non-circular cross-sections result in greater lustre and covering power and improved handle"; geometry is set by spinneret hole shape (trilobal, rectangular, hexalobal, hollow) and melt rheology. Table 14.6 (pp.393–394) ranks **trilobal brighter than round**. Quantitative lustre relation, Eq. 14.16 (p.394): **Imax(trilobal)/Imax(round) = 1.158 − 0.128·MR + 0.950·TR**.
- **Hollow fibre 中空纤维** (p.391) uses annular spinneret holes joined by "bridges"; the chapter derives the mechanical strength of those bridges (Eqs. 14.10–14.15, pp.391–392) and defines the **orifice hollow ratio**.

## Melt Rheology for Speciality Spinning 特种纱线的熔体流变学 14.8

Empirical relations are quoted for nylon 6 zero-shear viscosity η₀ versus molecular weight and temperature (Eqs. 14.17–14.19a), the Gregory relation for PET with a DEG-corrected variant (14.22–14.26), and RV–IV–Mn conversions (14.20–14.21) (pp.396–398). In fine/hollow spinning, pressure, extrusion speed and **die swell 模口膨胀** must be balanced; a spinneret pressure range of **40–80 kgf/cm²** is cited (pp.394–395).

## Hygiene implication 卫生材料启示

**This is the most directly applicable chapter in the whole batch for the owner's scope** — specifically sections 14.6 (conjugated microfibre) and 14.7 (shaped cross-sections), which are the industrial origin of today's topsheet and fine-denier nonwoven fibre design.

1. **Conjugated bicomponent spinning is the direct ancestor of splittable nonwovens**: the island-in-sea and radial-split configurations described at 14.6 are the same geometry used for splittable bicomponent spunbond and for海岛 staple used in high-coverage coverstock and wipes. See [[PTT-PET S-S Fiber Technology Routes Compared（PTT-PET 并列纤维三条技术路线对比）]] for the modern side-by-side variant.
2. **Non-circular cross-section → lustre, cover and handle** (p.387) is exactly the trade space behind topsheet shaped-fibre selection: trilobal is brighter than round, and the shaped cross-section also raises specific surface area, which interacts with wettability and rewet at the topsheet. See [[三叶形截面纤维（Trilobal Fibre）]] and [[Pentagram PET vs Rounded Trilobal PET (Topsheet Shaped Fibre Selection)]].
3. **Microgroove formation by soluble additive + alkali weight reduction** (p.383) is cited here for PET filament; the analogous idea of creating surface channels to move surface liquid is directly relevant to topsheet run-off and acquisition behaviour, though this chapter gives no data for webs — [待验证] whether alkali reduction is practical on polyolefin webs at all.
4. **Hollow fibre with bridge spinnerets** (p.391) is the standard route to hollow polyester staple used for loft and bulk; the same concept of buying loft with void volume rather than fibre mass applies to bulk in air-through bonded layers.
5. Chapter 14 reflects the late-1990s state of the art: no references to meltblown, spunbond meshes, or modern fine-denier (<1 dtex) polyolefin routes; commercialization is framed as depending on favourable market conditions (p.360), which is worth remembering when evaluating any speciality-fibre business case. Compare against today's functional-fibre landscape in [[功能性差别化纤维（PTT_超仿棉_纳米凉感）]].
6. Background chapter for base PET behaviour: [[Manufactured Fibre Technology Ch12 Polyethylene Terephthalate Fibres]].

Chapter 14 has no content on absorbency, SAP or absorbent-core design.
