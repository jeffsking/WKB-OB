# Surface Tension and Surface Energy

## One-line Position 一句话定位

Chapter 2 (pp. 74–109) supplies the missing micro-level to Chapter 1's macro hydraulics: it fixes surface tension as a thermodynamic quantity, establishes Young's equation as an energy identity, and — most usefully for hygiene R&D — replaces the textbook flat-plate sessile drop with a rod/tube model that gives the capillary pull on an individual fibre, F = γLV·cos θeq·C.（中文：第 2 章为第 1 章的宏观流体力学补上微观热力学，并以"杆/管"模型取代平面液滴，给出单根纤维的毛细拉力。）

## Summary 摘要

- **Thermodynamic definition.** Surface tension is "Gibbs free energy per unit area", and "the specific free energies of the interfaces are constants characteristic of the system" — a state function, not a force.
- **Young's equation, corrected.** γSV = γSL + γLV cos θeq. The book explicitly overturns the common misreading: "Contrary to statements or implications in some of the literature Young's equation is not a force balance. It is a thermodynamic equation showing a valid and easily proved relationship among energies, i.e. scalar quantities." Valid for acute, obtuse, 90°, and θeq = 0 (cos θeq ≈ 1, indefinite spreading).
- **Scope caveat.** "Equation 18 shows an energy equilibrium but tells nothing about the forces or molecular mechanisms that cause the system to approach this equilibrium" — thermodynamics gives the destination, not the path.
- **Non-ideal surfaces.** Wenzel (roughness): cos θW = r·cos θY, r > 1 — roughness pushes hydrophilic surfaces more hydrophilic and hydrophobic surfaces more hydrophobic. Cassie–Baxter (composite/gas-trapping): cos θCB = f1·cos θY + f2·cos 180° = f1(cos θY + 1) − 1.
- **Geometry correction — the business-critical point.** "The sessile drop on a flat plate is not at all typical of the capillary systems of absorbency. The solid surfaces of absorbency are better modeled either by the exterior of a rod or the interior of a tube." For the rod: pull = γLV·cos θeq·C (C = circumference); for the tube: the driving force is (γSL − γSV), independent of tube cross-section and proportional only to three-phase-line length.
- **Zero-angle regime.** "SLV systems for which θeq = 0 are of special importance in absorbency ... these are the systems most commonly encountered in practice." At θeq = 0, WA = WC = 2γLV → liquid fully wets and never beads.
- **Three-phase line (TPL).** "A rope of liquid, a very few molecular diameters thick, that can roll freely along the solid surface" — the hub where all three interface energies act.
- **Work functions.** WA = γLV + γSV − γSL = γLV(1 + cos θeq) (Dupré); WC = 2γLV.
- **Urine is not water.** "If the liquid phase of a zero contact angle system consists of two or more components selective adsorption, adsorption rates, and Marangoni effects can seriously complicate the capillary behavior while equilibrium is being reached."
- **Reference chain.** 32 sources from J. Willard Gibbs (1906/1961) through Harkins & Brown (1919), Schwartz, Defay/Prigogine, Adamson and Wu.

## Key Claims 关键论断

- **Stop measuring absorbency on flat plates.** The textbook sessile-drop protocol misrepresents the geometry of a fibrous web; fibre-level capillary pull must be evaluated with the rod model. This single correction changes what a "contact angle test" means for a core.
- **Hydrophilisation is compounding, not additive.** Grafting −OH/−COOH lowers θeq, and Wenzel then multiplies the gain through roughness (cos θW = r·cos θY) — engineering both surface chemistry and micro-roughness is strictly better than either alone.
- **A structured backsheet beats a chemically hydrophobic one.** Cassie–Baxter lets a modestly hydrophilic polymer behave hydrophobically through geometry — cheaper and more durable than chemistry, and it is the design principle behind anti-rewet backsheet structures.
- **The ideal SAP particle is a Cassie–Baxter doublet.** A thin hydrophobic shell (wax/siloxane) guarantees dry-flowability while the hydrophilic core keeps θeq → 0 for maximum adhesion — flowability and absorption solved on one particle.
- **Adhesive selection is a surface-energy match.** WA = γLV(1 + cos θeq) means glue strength rises monotonically as contact angle falls; adhesive and substrate surface energies must be paired, not chosen independently.
- **Urine never reaches equilibrium.** Because body fluids carry salts, proteins and endogenous surfactants, Marangoni-driven non-equilibrium capillary behaviour is the real operating condition — a reason single-component water tests systematically flatter product performance.
