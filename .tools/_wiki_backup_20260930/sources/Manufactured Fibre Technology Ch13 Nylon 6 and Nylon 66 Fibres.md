# Chapter 13 Nylon 6 and Nylon 66 Fibres 尼龙 6 与尼龙 66 纤维

> Source: Manufactured Fibre Technology, Ch.13, pp.318–352.

## Summary 摘要

Definition (p.318): "A polyamide manufactured from aliphatic monomer(s) is commonly designated as nylon." 尼龙即由脂肪族单体制得的聚酰胺（amide link –NH–CO–）。 The chapter follows the two protagonists along a complete chain — **polymerization → degradation → spinning → structure → application**:

- **Nylon 6** via ring-opening polymerization of **caprolactam (CL 己内酰胺)**, an AB-type monomer.
- **Nylon 66** via polycondensation of **hexamethylene diamine (HMDA 己二胺)** with **adipic acid 己二酸** through the intermediate **AH salt 尼龙66盐**.

## Polymerization 聚合 13.2–13.3

1. **Ring-opening needs a starter** (p.320): "Caprolactam (CL) does not polymerize in a completely dry condition. It requires a catalyst which converts a small amount of CL to aminocaproic acid (ACA)." CL + H₂O → ACA. Water catalysis is industrially dominant because it is controllable; alkali catalysis gives higher rate and higher molecular weight but is hard to control. Typical water-catalysed conditions quoted in the source: temperature **225–280 °C**, water concentration **5–10%**. The continuous **VK tube (Vereinfacht Kontinuierlich, 连续简化管)** reactor is **8–10 m high** (p.325).
2. **Equilibrium limits conversion** (p.324): "In industrial practice, under optimum conditions, the reaction forms an equilibrium with 90% in favour of polycaprolactam (PCL)." Residual monomer must be reduced before melt spinning, otherwise **post-polymerization 后聚合** occurs on the spinline when the water distribution changes (pp.324–325). The exact residual-monomer specification value is garbled in the source (printed as a bare "%") — [待验证].
3. **Raw-material economy differs by mechanism** (p.333): "during the PC stage, 13.5% of the weight of the monomer used is lost in the form of water... an additional amount of monomer (19% in the case of nylon 66 and 6% for nylon 6)." Note an internal inconsistency in the source: section 五 restates the contrast as "13.5% vs 2% 失重" — [待验证] which figure applies to nylon 6.

## Degradation and Additives 降解与添加剂 13.4–13.5

Four degradation classes: **thermal 热**, **thermo-oxidative 热氧**, **mechano-chemical 机械化学** and **hydrolytic 水解**, tracked by volatile products (**CO₂, NH₃, HCHO**) and gelation. Depolymerization back to monomer is cited as industrial-scale evidence for hydrolytic routes. Additives covered: mass colouration and **copper-salt heat stabilisers**.

## Fibre Manufacture 纤维制造 13.6–13.7

- **Melt spinning 熔体纺丝** with spinline structure evolution; the effect of spinning speed on birefringence, crystallinity and axial shrinkage is the experimental core (Fig. 13.15–13.19), again producing the **LOY / POY / HOY / FOY** classes (pp.347–349).
- Integrated **spin-draw (SD)** and mass-colouration spinning variants.
- After-treatment: drawing, texturing, **heat-setting 热定形**.
- Practical value flagged by the source: Fig. 13.17–13.19 reveal the **time-dependent instability of undrawn yarn structure** (axial shrinkage / elongation), which governs package stability (pp.344–346).
- Engineering constraints above about 6000 m/min (take-up difficulty, air drag) are mentioned but not quantified by the source.

## Glass Transition and Structure 玻璃化转变与结构 13.8

- Faster crystallization than PET (pp.341–342): "nylon 66 and nylon 6 crystallize relatively more rapidly, primarily because of more flexible molecular chains"; "'Y crystals are favoured by an increase in winding speed" (the source quotes the gamma-symbol the Greek γ as 'Y). Nylon 6 shows **polymorphism 多态性** — α, γ and pseudo-hexagonal forms depending on processing (p.352) — and the structure is highly moisture-sensitive, which the source identifies as the difficulty in spinline stability control.
- **Tg–regain coupling** (pp.351–352): "The Tg of nylon 6 is reported to be around 50°C... The moisture regain range between 2.0 and 3.5% is specially suitable during production of fibre, where a minimum change of Tg is observed." 温湿度空调因此是聚酰胺工厂区别于其他纤维的关键。

## Hygiene implication 卫生材料启示

**Chapter 13 is largely outside the owner's scope, and should be read as polymer-science background rather than design input.** Polyamide filament has essentially no role in absorbent cores, and PA staple is not used in through-air bonded coverstock in mainstream hygiene.

Where it does transfer:

1. **The Tg–moisture-regain concept transfers directly**: nylon 6 shows ~50 °C Tg shifted strongly by regain, with a 2.0–3.5% window of minimum Tg sensitivity. Any hydrophilic polymer whose stiffness and set behaviour moves with ambient humidity — including moisture-responsive fibre concepts used in comfort/microclimate work — obeys the same coupling. See [[Moisture-Responsive Fibre（湿敏变形纤维）]].
2. **Polymer selection for sheath-core bonding**: nylon's ready crystallization and high melting point versus polyolefin's low melting point is one reason PA is not used as a thermal-bonding sheath; the comparison logic sits alongside [[PP基皮芯与PET基皮芯对比]], which contrasts polyester and polyolefin sheath-core pairs actually used in hygiene.
3. **Process vocabulary only**: LOY/POY/HOY/FOY and the speed–orientation relation are the same framework already applied to PET in [[Manufactured Fibre Technology Ch12 Polyethylene Terephthalate Fibres]]; no new hygiene-specific content arises here.
4. For process-route context in general terms see [[无纺布五大工艺对比]] — but note that none of the five nonwoven routes discussed in the vault relies on nylon.

Nothing in Chapter 13 applies to fluff pulp, SAP, REL core design or polyolefin nonwoven modification.
