# Cross-linked Cellulose and Derivatives

## One-line Position 一句话定位

Chapter VII covers molecular-level chemical modification of cellulose into superabsorbent derivatives — cross-linked CMC (the most commercialized cellulosic route), HEC, phosphonoalkyl/phosphorylated cellulose, sulfonated/oxidized cellulose, microfibrillated cellulose (MFC) and lignin-retaining CTMP — and unifies them under Donnan gel-swelling theory.

本章在分子层面论述纤维素的化学改性超吸水路线——交联 CMC（最成熟的纤维素基路线）、HEC、磷酸化纤维素、磺化/氧化纤维素、微纤丝化纤维素（MFC）及含木质素 CTMP，并以 Donnan 凝胶溶胀理论统一解释。

## Summary 摘要

- **Cross-linked CMC** is the core technology: etherification (monochloroacetic acid, up to 8 mol/mol cellulose) followed by cross-linking (epichlorohydrin, formaldehyde, glutaraldehyde). **Water-insolubility threshold at low DS ≈ 0.35–0.40**; cross-linked CMC gives 5–25 g/g vs 1–2 g/g for unmodified pulp (p. 278).
- **DS vs cross-link tradeoff**: higher cross-link gives strength and prevents dissolution but limits swell space → lower capacity; must be tuned precisely (p. 278). Thermal/acid treatment (Reid process, 60°C×10h or 80°C×3h) can insolubilize without external cross-linker (brightness 75–80).
- **Phosphorylated cellulose** (Bernardin, urea-phosphate, ~160°C×20min): acid vs salt form differs ~5× in absorbency; reconverting to acid form after refining retains most gain and yields combined absorbency + ion-exchange (p. 280). Preferred P ≈ 2 wt%, cross-link 0.015–0.110 mol/mol cellulose.
- **MFC & CTMP**: microfibrillated cellulose (beating to CSF < 50, freeze-dry, cross-link) gives XRMFC 16 g/cc / 8 g/g retention; porous MFC ~15 g/g. CTMP lignin-retaining pulp: WRV 131, drip capacity 5.9 g/g, absorption rate 3–4 ml/s (p. 285–287).
- **Graft routes preview Ch. IX**: HEC grafted with partially hydrolyzed polyacrylamide reached **3000 g/g (pure water), 270 g/g (0.85% NaCl)** — the highest cellulosic-based figure, approaching synthetic SAP (p. 288).
- **Mechanism**: swelling governed by Donnan ion distribution — osmotic pressure from internal ionized groups drives water in until balanced by network viscoelastic resistance (Grignon & Scallan); Lucas–Washburn describes uptake rate in the capillary/CMC network (p. 291).

## Key Claims 关键论断

- Cellulosic superabsorbents are cheap (wood pulp feedstock) but limited to tens of g/g, so they are paired with synthetic SAP (hundreds g/g) in composites — the cellulose layer provides fast capillary uptake and relieves gel-blocking of SAP.
- Burst-fiber (Karlsson) and MFC structures raise surface area and avoid gel-blocking — a structural solution to the SAP gel-blocking problem relevant to core design (p. 278).
