# Effect of Drying Methods on Bacterial Cellulose Aerogel

## Positioning 一句话定位

This peer-reviewed review consolidates how drying methods govern the thermal and mechanical behavior of bacterial cellulose (BC，细菌纤维素) aerogels and provides a parameter-backed basis for selecting a preservation route.  
（中文：该同行评审综述系统梳理干燥方法如何影响细菌纤维素气凝胶的热学与力学表现，为干燥路线选型提供参数依据。）

## Summary 摘要

Bacterial cellulose offers high purity, crystallinity, hydrophilicity, and a nanoscale fiber network without the lignin-removal burden of plant cellulose. The review compares hot-air, freeze, ambient, supercritical CO₂, microwave, bidirectional-freezing, and oven-drying routes. Drying changes pore morphology, density, hydrogen bonding, crystallinity, water uptake, gas permeability, thermal conductivity, and strength. Freeze-drying is the most frequently reported route because it preserves porous structures, but it can produce weaker films than hot-air drying in some systems and remains sensitive to pre-freezing conditions. Oven and ambient drying are simpler and may improve tensile or hardness-related properties, but capillary collapse commonly reduces porosity and swelling. Supercritical CO₂ can deliver very low thermal conductivity while avoiding liquid–gas interfacial stress, although the review identifies cost and process complexity as practical constraints. Additives, oxidation, crosslinking, bacterial strain, culture mode, and medium composition prevent drying method from being treated as an isolated variable.
