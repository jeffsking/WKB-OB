# Handbook of Polymer Testing – Short-Term Mechanical Tests

## Chapter 11: Tear Properties — Deep-Read Report

Book: Handbook of Polymer Testing – Short-Term Mechanical Tests
Author: Edward N. Brown (Rapra, 2002)
Chapter: 11 — Tear Properties (Book pp. 177–184)

## 1. Core Arguments（核心论点）

CA-1 — Tear test is a "flaw-concentrated" force, not an evenly applied tensile force.
> "In a tensile test, taken to break, the force to produce failure in a nominally flawless test piece is measured. In a tear test, the force is not applied evenly but concentrated on a deliberate flaw or sharp discontinuity and the force to continuously produce new surface is measured." [Line 8-12]

CA-2 — Tear strength is geometry-dependent, not an intrinsic material property.
> "Because tear strength is very geometry dependent it is not surprising that a many different tests have been devised… Tear strength from common tests is not an intrinsic property of the material and it can be difficult to directly correlate the results of laboratory tests with service performance." [Line 15-22]

CA-3 — Tearing energy (fracture-mechanics approach) is the fundamental, geometry-independent property.
> "The tearing energy is in theory a basic material property and independent of test piece geometry so that if the tearing energy and the elastic characteristics of the material are known the force needed to tear a given geometry can be predicted." [Line 24-29]

CA-4 — Initiation force ≠ propagation force; both matter for service failure assessment.
> "A very important distinction can be made between the force to initiate a tear as opposed to that to propagate a tear… the propagation force can be very much smaller." [Line 54-59]

CA-5 — Standardisation is thin (only two internationally), and different standard bodies still diverge in geometry, speed, and reporting convention.
> "Only two methods are standardised internationally." [Line 116-117]; contrast ASTM's four methods [Line 194-210].

CA-6 — Thickness-normalised vs. absolute tear force: each convention hides a different physical reality.
> "Whilst normalising to the thickness invites people to believe that tear strength is proportional to thickness, ignoring thickness makes no compensation at all." [Line 176-180]

CA-7 — Common tests can give contradictory results; fracture-mechanics-style analysis is the emerging direction.
> "Dawson and Bowes [14] point out that falling dart impact and Elmendorf tear often give contradictory results and consider the use of single specimen J-integral analysis and crack tip opening displacement methods." [Line 233-236]

## 2. Key Concepts with Operationalized Definitions

### KC-1 — Tear test

- English definition (quotation): "In a tear test, the force is not applied evenly but concentrated on a deliberate flaw or sharp discontinuity and the force to continuously produce new surface is measured." [Line 9-11]
- Where used (original excerpt): "Tear tests are only applicable to flexible sheet and thin films." [Line 8]
- Operational note: Requires a pre-existing cut or a sharp re-entry angle; apparatus response must track rapid dynamic force changes [Line 35-37].
### KC-2 — Tear initiation vs. tear propagation

- English definition (quotation): "A very important distinction can be made between the force to initiate a tear as opposed to that to propagate a tear." [Line 54-55]
- Where used (original excerpt): "after a tear has started, perhaps because of an accidental cut, the resistance to propagation will determine whether the damage becomes catastrophic." [Line 55-57]
- Operational note: Angle tear without a cut is the only geometry measuring initiation force [Line 91-92]; all others with a pre-cut measure propagation.
### KC-3 — Tearing energy (fracture-mechanics quantity)

- English definition (quotation): "the concept of energy of tearing which is the energy required to form unit area of new surface during tearing." [Line 24-26]
- Where used (original excerpt): "the tearing energy is in theory a basic material property and independent of test piece geometry." [Line 26-28]
- Operational note: Historically obtained from the trouser test piece; now supplemented by finite element analysis and applied to plastic films [Line 29-33].
### KC-4 — Trouser tear geometry

- English definition (quotation): "A test piece 150 mm by 50 mm is cut along the centre of its long axis, from one end to half way down its length. The two 'legs' so formed are gripped in the stationary and moving jaws of a universal testing machine." [Line 117-120]
- Where used (original excerpt): "In the trouser tear the stresses must include shear forces. It allows the course of tear propagation to be followed and is a relatively easy shape to cut." [Line 94-96]
- Operational note: Standardised in ISO 6383-1 / BS 2782-360B / ASTM D1938 [Line 116-117, 185-186, 194-195].
### KC-5 — Elmendorf tear test (pendulum)

- English definition (quotation): "applies a tearing force by means of a pendulum. Energy stored in the pendulum is used to produce tearing and the amount used is indicated by the energy lost compared to the total available." [Line 39-47]
- Where used (original excerpt): "the test piece is held in the jaws of a pendulum, one jaw fixed and one attached to the pendulum arm. The pendulum is released and causes an initial cut in the test piece to propagate across it." [Line 147-149]
- Operational note: Constant-radius specimen preferred over rectangular for reproducibility; energy window 20–80 % of pendulum capacity [Line 153-163].
