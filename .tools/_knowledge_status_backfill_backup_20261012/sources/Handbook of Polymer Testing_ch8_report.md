# Chapter 8 · Shear Properties — 深度拆书报告

书名：Handbook of Polymer Testing – Short-Term Mechanical Tests
作者：Edward N. Brown (Rapra, 2002)
章节：Chapter 8 · Shear Properties
页码范围：pp. 135–142

## 1. Core Arguments（核心论点）

Shear forces are geometrically distinct from tensile/compressive forces, yet critically under-tested.
> "Tensile and compressive forces are normal to the plane on which they act but shear forces are parallel to the plane. Simple shear can be represented by planes sliding parallel to a given plane by an amount proportional to their distance from that plane." [Line 8–10]
> "Shear stresses are involved in many applications of plastics but very little shear testing is carried out except on fibre reinforced materials." [Line 25–26]

Shear modulus and shear strength are conceptually distinct and are rarely measured in the same test.
> "Both shear modulus and the shear strength are of interest, but not necessarily measured in the same test. Shear modulus is usually measured at small strains where the stress-strain relationship is essentially linear." [Line 44–46]

No single loading geometry dominates plastic shear testing; each geometry trades off purity of shear state against practicality.
> "There are a number of loading systems which give rise to shear stresses including lap shear, punch shear, torsion and four point loading." [Line 46–47]
> The lap/sandwich geometry "has not been standardised for quasi-static tests on plastics, probably because of the difficulty of bonding test pieces" [Line 50–51]; punch shear is "relatively popular to measure shear strength of plastics" [Line 66]; torsion is "largely restricted to measuring stiffness of rubbers, flexible plastics and coated fabrics as a means of characterising low temperature performance" [Line 82–84].

Torsional (Clash & Berg) tests are the only ISO general-purpose shear tests for plastics, and their purpose is cold-temperature characterisation rather than room-temperature strength.
> "The only ISO tests for shear of plastics in general are torsional methods. ISO 458-1 … is intended for measuring stiffness in torsion at various temperatures, particularly at temperatures below 0 °C." [Line 116–118]

Standardisation in shear testing is notably loose: tolerances, deflection windows and even specimen dimensions vary between ostensibly equivalent standards.
> For ISO 458-2: "The angle of deflection is in this case limited to between 55° and 65° (so much for standardisation)" [Line 141]
> Punch shear: ASTM D732 "follows the same pattern as Method A, but uses a 50 mm diameter test piece and permits any thickness between 0.125 mm and 12.5 mm." [Line 186–187]

In composites, shear failure is deliberately engineered rather than avoided; reduced span-to-thickness ratio forces interlaminar shear failure as the failure mode.
> "The span is reduced to six times the test piece thickness to encourage shear failure." [Line 96–98]

The Iosipescu notched test is the theoretically cleanest shear configuration – the stress between the notches is pure and uniform shear, applicable beyond its original metallic/fibre-reinforced scope.
> "Perhaps surprisingly, the stress across the test piece between the notches is pure shear and is uniform. The shear stress is simply given by τ = P/A." [Line 233–238]

## 2. Key Concepts with Operationalized Definitions

### 2.1 Simple Shear

- Definition (English original): "Simple shear can be represented by planes sliding parallel to a given plane by an amount proportional to their distance from that plane." [Line 9–10]
- Formalisation: shear stress τ = F / (l × w); shear strain γ = x / h. [Line 13–19]
- Use in context: "Both shear modulus and the shear strength are of interest, but not necessarily measured in the same test." [Line 44–45] — the fundamental equations underlie every subsequent geometry.
### 2.2 Pure Shear

- Definition (English original): "Pure shear … is defined as a homogeneous strain in which one of the principal extensions is zero and the volume is unchanged. If the extension ratio l1 = α while l2 = l then l3 is 1/α." [Line 21–23]
- Use in context: Punch shear "The approximation to pure shear conditions are achieved by a punch bearing on a sheet of material supported by a die." [Line 67–69]; Iosipescu "the stress across the test piece between the notches is pure shear and is uniform." [Line 233–234]
### 2.3 Shear Modulus (G)

- Definition (English original): "Shear modulus is usually measured at small strains where the stress-strain relationship is essentially linear." [Line 45–46]
- Equation: τ/A = Gγ, i.e. F/A = Gγ. [Line 58–64]
- Use in context: "Shear modulus can be measured in torsion, although in practice it is largely restricted to measuring stiffness of rubbers, flexible plastics and coated fabrics as a means of characterising low temperature performance." [Line 82–84]
### 2.4 Punch Shear

- Definition (English original): "Punch shear geometry … The approximation to pure shear conditions are achieved by a punch bearing on a sheet of material supported by a die. The smaller the difference between the internal diameter of the die and the external diameter of the punch the nearer the approximation." [Line 66–69]
- Use in context: "The test is carried out in compression by placing the test piece in a special bolster with a close fitting punch … the load on the punch is increased so as to cause failure … within 15 to 45 seconds from first application of the load." [Line 157–160]
