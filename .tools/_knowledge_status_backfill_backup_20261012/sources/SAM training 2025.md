#   SAM Chemistry and Manufacturing

#   Definitions of superabsorbent material

$\bullet$ Non-technical: Material that can take in and hold an unusually large amount of liquid (usually water).

$\bullet$ Technical: Crosslinked, long-chain, hydrophilic and hydrogen bond-capable ionic polymers (that absorb and retain large quantities of water).

$\bullet$ At KC we talk about "SAM" (superabsorbent material)

* Outside of KC, it is more common to say "SAP" (superabsorbent polymer)

$\bullet$ You may hear about "SAF" (superabsorbent fiber). This is chemically similar, but in fiber form rather than particulate.

#   Superabsorbent Base Chemistry

Monomers have a C=C double bond and ionic group, such as COOH.

SAM is usually petroleum-based, but various bio-based alternatives are being developed.

$\bullet$ Polysaccharide-based

* Carboxymethylcellulose (CMC)

* Modified starch

* Alginate, chitosan

* Poly(amino acid)-based

* Polyglutamic acid

by far the most common commercial SAM chemistry

#   Critical Characteristics of SAM:

$\bullet$ Long-chain polymer network (black) with bound ions (yellow) and mobile counterions (green).

* Counterions contribute to osmotic pressure, the driving force for absorption

* Crosslinked network (red)

* A particle is a single molecule (neglecting extractives).

* Without crosslinks, the particle would simply dissolve.

* Hydrophilic and hydrogen bond-capable

* Polymer chains must be able to interact with water

#   Why does SAM swell? 1. Osmotic pressure

Osmotic pressure: Solute (largely mobile ions) concentration is greater within SAM particle than in external solution. This drives water into the SAM by osmosis.

$\bullet$ Mobile ions cannot flow out of particle, because charge balance must be maintained.

$\bullet$ As water flows into SAM, volume of external solution is decreased. $\rightarrow$ Local solute concentration and charge concentration in external solution is increased. This is the “Donnan Exclusion” effect.

$\bullet$ Osmotic pressure depends on osmolality (moles of solute per kg water), not ionic strength.

$$\Pi_{\text{tonic}}=RT\left(c_{i}-c_{i}^{*}\right)$$

$$ \Pi_{\text{tonic}} = \text{Osmotic pressure} $$

$c_i$ = Conc. of mobile ions inside SAM

$c_i^*$ = Conc. of mobile ions in external solution

$R$ = Ideal gas constant

$T$ = Absolute temperature

#   Influence of salinity in external solution

$\bullet$ Higher solute $\rightarrow$ lower osmotic pressure $\rightarrow$ lower absorbent capacity

$\bullet$ Osmolality of 0.9% saline = Osmolality of blood plasma = 287mOsm [=mmol solute] /kg

$\bullet$ Mean osmolality of urine is about the same as osmolality of blood plasma

* But urine is much more variable due to hydration and other health states

$\bullet$ We often use 0.9% saline as a urine simulant for absorbency tests.

#   Why does SAM swell? 2. Electrostatic repulsion

Once SAM particle is hydrated, negative bound ions in nearby polymer chains repel each other, contributing to the expansive force.

$\bullet$ secondary to osmotic pressure driving force
