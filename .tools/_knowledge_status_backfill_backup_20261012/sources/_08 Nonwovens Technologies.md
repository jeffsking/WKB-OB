#   Spunbond (SB)

$\bullet$ Terminology came from historical reference to textile process of "Spinning" or making fibers from natural materials (cotton, wool etc).

$\bullet$ A nonwoven fabric formed by filaments that have been extruded, drawn (SPUN), then laid on a continuous belt and then thermally point BONDed in a heated calendar.

#   Spunbond Characteristics

![A-12 M1 PE SB\n1.2 kV x30' '' i0' '' j' '\n](1/parseimages/figure9fee5c18a01d4c75a87d93e387cdad89_0_1.png)
$\bullet$ Strong (both machine and cross direction)

$\bullet$ High strength due to crystalline orientation (quench and draw).

$\bullet$ Abrasion resistance - 15-30% bond area

$\bullet$ Soft and conformable

$\bullet$ Fiber Size flexibility (13 to 25 microns)

$\bullet$ High porosity

$\bullet$ Dimensionally stable in wet/humid conditions

$\bullet$ Cost effective – Low Density Polymers

$\bullet$ 40% lighter than Polyester and Nylon

#   Bicomponent Spunbond

![](1/parseimages/figure2c7b6cce8f854ddb8af19d0ead57e65c_0_1.png)
$\bullet$ Combine fibers from two polymer types

$\bullet$ Gain benefits of both polymers in one fabric

$\bullet$ Create unique fabric properties due to dissimilar spinning conditions needed

$\bullet$ Utilize expensive active agents only in the fiber surface polymer

$\bullet$ Assist fiber formation of fragile polymers with robust polymers

#   Meltblown (MB)

$\bullet$ Entangled, self-bonding "micro"-fibers

$\bullet$ 2-5µm

$\bullet$ High fiber number density and small fiber size

$\bullet$ Softness, barrier performance, and absorbency

$\bullet$ Poor abrasion and low tensile strength
