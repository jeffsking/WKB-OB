# Chapter 12 Poly(ethylene terephthalate) Fibres 聚对苯二甲酸乙二醇酯纤维

> Source: Manufactured Fibre Technology, Ch.12, pp.271–317.

## Summary 摘要

PET is presented as "the most outstanding member of the polyester fibre family" (ISO definition, p.271): "a polymer comprising synthetic linear macromolecules having in the chain at least 85% (by mass) of an ester of a diol and terephthalic acid". The chapter covers the Whinfield and Dickson invention and commercialization by **DuPont (1953)** and **ICI (1954)** (pp.271–272), then runs the full chain: **polymer production** via two routes → **melt spinning** into four speed-defined product classes → **drawing, texturing and staple lines** → **structure and mechanical behaviour** → **applications** (apparel/home textiles, biomedical sutures and vascular grafts, industrial tyre cord and geotextiles) and future trends.

## Polymer Production 聚合物生产 12.2

Two routes start from different raw materials but converge on the same intermediate:

- **DMT route DMT路线**: dimethyl terephthalate + MEG → **ester interchange (EI 酯交换)**.
- **PTA route PTA路线**: terephthalic acid + MEG → **direct esterification (DE 直接酯化)**.
- Both yield **diglycol terephthalate (DGT 二甘醇对苯二甲酸酯)**, an intermediate with the acid:glycol mole ratio chemically fixed at **1:2**, guaranteeing equimolar 1:1 polycondensation (p.274).

Process claim (p.290): "The polycondensation process is predominantly diffusion controlled." 因此提高真空度、搅拌速率比单纯延长停留时间更能提升分子量；过长的停留时间反而增加热降解风险。

Route comparison (pp.291–292): "cost of TPA is usually less than DMT"; "the reaction product in the DE step has a higher molecular weight than that obtained in the EI step"; and "PET produced via the PTA route has lower average molecular weight, lower melting point and higher dye uptake" — the differences are attributed mainly to **diethylene glycol (DEG 二甘醇)** content. DEG forms by etherification of two glycol molecules and makes the chain irregular, lowering melting point and raising dye uptake (p.281).

**Catalyst activity order** (p.280): **Zn > Pb > Mn > Co > Mg > Ca > Sn > Na > Sb**. Side reactions (ether links, DEG) and thermal degradation are controlled with phosphorus-based heat stabilisers (pp.280–282). Batch / semi-continuous / continuous processing are compared quantitatively in Table 12.2 (p.278).

**High molecular weight PET**: chain extenders and **solid-state polymerization 固相缩聚** — post-polycondensation chips treated under nitrogen or reduced pressure at **220–260 °C** (p.294). Measured result cited (p.295): **IV raised from 0.68 to 1.1 dl/g**, giving **tenacity 9.0 g/den** and **elongation 8%** — used for tyre cord and bottle-grade PET.

## Fibre Production 纤维生产 12.3

### Filament yarn classes 长丝分类 (p.299)

| Take-up speed 卷绕速度 | Product 产品 |
|---|---|
| 500–1500 m/min | **LOY 低取向丝** — "low spinning speed in the range 500-1500 m min-1: the product is called low-oriented yarn (LOY)" |
| 1500–4000 m/min | **POY 部分取向丝** |
| 4000–6000 m/min | **HOY 高取向丝** |
| > 6000 m/min | **FOY 全取向丝** |

At higher speed the pre-orientation consumes the drawing headroom: "natural draw ratio characteristic of low-oriented yarns disappears as the spinning speed increases" (p.313). LOY after drawing can "develop high birefringence of 0.21 or even more" (p.272). DTA evidence (Fig. 12.12, p.300): at **birefringence 0.038** the POY crystallization temperature drops by about **30 °C**.

Filament drawing uses **draw ratios of 3–6** (p.302). The staple line is described step-wise (Fig. 12.16, p.306): spinning and take-up → conditioning → drawing → **heat-setting 热定形** → crimping → drying → cutting, with a troubleshooting section. Typical process parameters and properties are tabled in Table 12.4 (p.309).

## Structure and Mechanical Behaviour 结构与力学性能 12.4

Structure evolution runs amorphous → oriented → crystallized → stabilized by heat-setting (pp.311–314).

- **Nucleative collapse 成核崩塌** (p.311): "This has been termed 'nucleative collapse' [36] because a large number of small nuclei come together to form a crystallite." In an oriented melt the entropy penalty is small and the critical nucleus size approaches the unit-cell size, so high-speed spun PET forms dense microcrystallites.
- **Catalyst effects** (p.312): "the catalyst system could exhibit a greater influence than molecular weight in controlling the rate of crystallization of PET." Residual catalyst can act as a nucleating agent — 一个常被忽视的结构调控维度。
- Mechanical response is given by the stress–strain curve (Fig. 12.17, p.313) and Table 12.5 (p.314). The source itself notes these are treated "very briefly".

## Hygiene implication 卫生材料启示

PET matters to hygiene mainly as **topsheet / acquisition-layer spunbond** and as the **core of some bicomponent staple**, not as an absorbent.

1. **Speed-defined orientation is directly designable for spunbond topsheet**: the four speed bands (p.299) and the observation that draw headroom disappears above ~1500 m/min explain why higher spinning speed gives higher strength but lower elongation and a stiffer hand in PET spunbond. This is the same mechanism behind the fibre selection discussion in [[Pentagram PET vs Rounded Trilobal PET (Topsheet Shaped Fibre Selection)]].
2. **Catalyst residue control is a live compliance issue**: the activity series placing **Sb last** and the finding that the catalyst system dominates crystallization rate (pp.280, 312) are the background for antimony-free PET work — see [[Antimony-Free PET Fibre（无锑 PET 纤维）]]. Residual catalyst acting as nucleating agent means switching catalyst systems can shift crystallization and hence bonding behaviour, not just compliance.
3. **Melting-point depression by DEG / comonomer is the same lever used to make low-melting copolyester sheaths**: the PTA-route fibres have "lower melting point and higher dye uptake" for exactly this reason (p.292). Compare with [[CoPET（低熔点共聚酯）]], which sits at the other end of the same comonomer-isophthalate/DEG logic.
4. **Drawing ratio 3–6 and the orientation window** connects directly to Chapter 8's mechanics — see [[Manufactured Fibre Technology Ch08 Drawing of Melt-Spun Fibres]].
5. Chapter 12 says nothing about surface wettability or finish chemistry for topsheet hydrophilicity — that absence is itself worth noting: PET spunbond topsheet rewet behaviour cannot be read out of this chapter.

Nothing here relates to fluff pulp, SAP or core absorbency.
