# Drying of Pharmaceutical Products

> Source: Mujumdar (ed.), Handbook of Industrial Drying, 4th ed., CRC Press, 2014, Ch.29, pp.687–710 (Zdzisław Pakowski & Arun S. Mujumdar).

## Chapter Overview 章节概览

Drying as the GMP-governed, contamination-intolerant, thermally-cautious stage of drug manufacture — low tonnage / high value where energy cost is secondary to product integrity. Selection-driven structure: material classification (Sazhin/Rebinder, by minimum pore diameter → moisture–material bond strength), physical/thermal/sorptive properties (particle size, Arrhenius first-order thermal decomposition, sorption isotherms with hysteresis), dryer-family catalogue (batch ovens, fluid bed, band, turbo-tray, pneumatic, cyclone, spouted, vibrated, spray, drum, vacuum, freeze), granulation, aseptic conditions (HEPA, class 100, sterilization), and closed-cycle solvent recovery.

> Note: page numbers derived from PDF-page − 21; freeze-collapse minus signs and Table 29.1 group thresholds are OCR/print-ambiguous — treat as indicative.

## Key Viewpoints 关键观点

### 1. The noncompromise quality clause 不可妥协条款

> "The pharmaceutical industry is one in which quality of the final product cannot be compromised. Any deterioration of the product (e.g., by microbial infection, oxidation, thermal decomposition, contamination by metallic particles or by unremoved organic solvent) must be avoided at any cost." (p.687)

This single sentence pre-justifies every expensive construct in the chapter: polished stainless, inert gas, HEPA, freeze/vacuum.

### 2. Bond-energy ladder: pore size → dryer choice 结合能阶梯

> "Sazhin has developed a classification based in principle on Rebinder's proposal, which classifies all materials into four groups... The quantitative basis of the classification is the minimum pore diameter from which moisture is to be removed during drying." (p.688)

Group I nonporous/capillary (>100 nm, free moisture) → Group IV ultramicroporous (pore ≈ molecule size, chemically bound, moisture down to 0.2–0.1%). And: "The energy of the solid–moisture bond increases from capillary-porous to colloidal bodies. Adequate drying temperatures and residence times must be used for drying such difficult to dry solids." (p.691–692)

### 3. Energy is secondary for high-value products 能耗次要

> "Since most pharmaceuticals are low-tonnage and high-value products, considerations of energy saving are usually of secondary importance in the selection of the drying process." (p.688)

Product quality outranks kWh/kg — but this priority should NOT be imported to high-volume consumer hygiene lines where energy/kg dominates.

### 4. Arrhenius thermal-decomposition accounting 热分解动力学核算

> "Usually thermal decomposition follows a first-order reaction kinetics... k = A e^(−E/RT)... thiamine hydrochloride in aqueous solution has E = 92.18 kJ/mol and A = 9.58×10^10 1/min. Processing such solution at 90°C for 10 min would result in c/c₀ = 0.949 (i.e., about 5% loss)." (p.691)

A worked kinetic calculation: pick E/A, set acceptable loss, back out max temperature/time — directly reusable for any active-ingredient thermal window.

### 5. Desorption isotherms govern drying; hysteresis marks colloids 解吸等温线主导

> "For drying purposes only desorption isotherms are of practical significance... Materials showing sorption isotherms intermediate between these two categories belong to the so-called capillary-colloid-porous bodies... The energy of the solid–moisture bond increases from capillary-porous to colloidal bodies." (p.691–692)

Hysteresis is "intense" for colloidal bodies (starch, gelatin) — the desorption branch, not sorption, sets the drying spec.

### 6. Freeze-dried collapse temperature 坍塌温度

> "After all moisture sublimes, a very fine porous structure remains that can be easily rehydrated... The structure is labile and can collapse if its temperature is raised above a certain temperature of collapse. This temperature can be as low as −40°C (e.g., for glucose and fructose) or −10°C (e.g., for starches and proteins) and increases with the increase of molecular weight of the lattice and its dryness." (p.699–700)

The collapse temperature is the glass-transition analogue for amorphous dried solids — stability exists only below it.

## Research Method 研究方法

Structured equipment-selection review with embedded empirical tables: Sazhin/Rebinder 4-group classification (Table 29.1); particle-size classes (Table 29.2); Arrhenius decomposition data (p.691); sorption/desorption bond-type and heat-of-sorption data (Table 29.3); dryer evaporation-rate/residence-time survey (p.693–702, Tables 29.5–29.6); aseptic/solvent engineering (HEPA/class-100/sterilization p.704–708; closed-cycle N₂ purge math p.708).

## Strengths and Weaknesses 优劣评估

- Strengths: explicit GMP/aseptic discipline with concrete specs; quantitative bond-strength taxonomy; real Arrhenius worked example; freeze-collapse concept; honest that energy is secondary for high-value product.
- Weaknesses: entirely pharmaceutical — no fibre, nonwoven or SAP examples, so every hygiene link is by analogy; freeze-collapse temperatures printed with ambiguous minus signs; Sazhin group thresholds overlap (selection aid, not rigorous); no residual-moisture target for microbial stability is given.

## Hygiene Implication 卫材关联

Relevance: ★★★★☆ — the most genuinely transferable chapter of this food/pharma batch: its GMP residual-moisture control, aseptic/cleanroom discipline, sorption-isotherm reasoning, and closed-cycle/solvent-safety logic map onto medical-grade nonwovens and lotion/active-coated wipes. Transfer is by discipline and method, not by substrate data.

- Noncompromise contamination clause (p.687): mirror "avoided at any cost" for medical-grade lines — bioburden, oxidation, metal-particle and residual-solvent acceptance as zero-tolerance critical limits.
- Desorption isotherm + bond-energy ladder for moisture spec (p.691–692): build the final-water-content spec from the desorption branch so bound (fibre/SAP) moisture, not just free surface water, reaches target — prevents rewet/clumping.
- Arrhenius loss check on every active (p.691): take E/A of the wipe-lotion active, set acceptable loss, back out max web temperature/residence before dryer setup.
- Aseptic/HEPA/class-100 converting discipline (p.704–707): HEPA 99.97% @ >0.3 µm, class-100 laminar flow, 200–250°C sterilization protocol, and elimination of sliding/rotating parts that shed metallic particles.
- Closed-cycle inert-gas drying (p.708) where solvent-borne coatings or flammable/toxic actives are present; watch sticky/defluidization of fine powders (p.690) — fine SAP fines or additive powders that plasticize in residual moisture can defluidize a fluid bed.
- Do NOT import the "energy is secondary" priority (p.688) to bulk hygiene lines — that posture is exclusive to low-tonnage, high-value pharma economics.
