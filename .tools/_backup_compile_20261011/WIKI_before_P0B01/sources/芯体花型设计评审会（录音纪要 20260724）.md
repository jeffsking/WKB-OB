## Summary 摘要

The meeting reviewed one drum of patterns tested on a single trial round. Codes 1–2 were CTRL variants whose narrowest points were aligned to the national-standard injection point (centre-front 35 mm) and to the low-pressure / cradle injection point (centre-front 85 mm); Code 3 was an existing drum design ("海王 / Aquaman"); Code 4 was a **patent-avoidance** variant (the X pattern with both ends joined, drawn in one stroke as a figure-eight). Four patterns (grid / tape / chocolate / leaf-vein / Tesla) were then trialled on one drum, and **leaf-vein scored best**; codes 8–10 stacked leaf-vein with a large fan tail (大扇尾) and with MD zoning. Four conclusions were drawn: (1) a **continuous central channel (中轴) is mandatory** because the channel/hollow slot under the injection point determines high-pressure diffusion; (2) **side branches are helpful but optional** — they speed intake and raise ICP utilisation, yet can be freely adjusted, so the team recommends a leaf-vein-style profile; (3) **ultrasonic bonding** can further raise longitudinal spread and intake time; (4) **bonding width 3 mm > 6 mm** for cradle 3rd intake, with 8 mm the safest for tooling life (a caveat: the 3 mm vs 6 mm difference is not statistically solid, so the point was deliberately weakened). The centre-concentrated SAP density is produced on the forming wire by enlarging the mesh apertures over the centre while keeping the powder feed and line speed constant. On IP, the reading was that P&G patents bar **≥2 longitudinal grooves**, and a leaf-vein drawn at **30° (<45°)** can be argued as transverse. Next steps: take four codes (CTRL, no-slot, leaf-vein + 3 mm bonding, large fan tail) to dummy testing, open the patent-conflict discussion with IP, and shorten the LSN. The meeting also flagged a material-side verification idea: do **not** cut the IEL — use its ES fibres as the hot-melt binder to bond the LSN/PSN, and confirm appearance is acceptable.
