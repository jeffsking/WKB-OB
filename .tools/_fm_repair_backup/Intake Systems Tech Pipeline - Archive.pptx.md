## Newtonian Fluids

1. Follows Newton's Law of Viscosity

Describes the relationship between shear stress and velocity gradient in a fluid.

$$\tau=-\mu\frac{dv}{dy}$$

where

$\tau$ is the shear stress exerted by the fluid ("drag")

$\frac{dv}{dy}$ is the velocity gradient perpendicular to the direction of shear.

2. Shear Stress linearly dependent on the velocity gradient
   * viscosity does not depend upon the shear stress.

## Non-Newtonian Fluids

1. Follows Power-Law fluid

Described by two parameters rheological model of a pseudoplastic fluid where the viscosity decreases as shear rates increases

$$\tau=A\left(\frac{du}{dy}\right)^{n}+\tau_{y}\ldots\text{Power Law Fluids}$$

Where:

$\frac{du}{dy}$: velocity gradient or shear rate
A: Flow consistency index
n: flow behaviour index
n: appert viscosity

2. Shear Stress is not linearly dependent on the velocity gradient
gradient
9. Viscosity depends on the shear stress.

## CAUSES

$\bullet$ Fluids stays on the body, not on the pad

$\bullet$ BSL impedes intake

$\bullet$ Poor layer interfaces for handling complex, variable flow

$\bullet$ Materials do not absorb/desorb fast enough

$\bullet$ Retention prevents fluid distribution

$\bullet$ Pad is too stiff

$\bullet$ Products bunch/twist in use

## DESIRED FUNTION

* Fluid transfers from body onto the pad

$\bullet$ BSL quickly captures and releases fluid into the pad

* Absorbents system pulls fluid into and along the bottom of the pad

$\bullet$ Pad is comfortable and discrete

$\bullet$ Pad does not bunch/twist in use

## TECHNICAL APPROACHES

$\bullet$ BSL with high open area and suitable surface tension

* Absorbent structures with z-directional capillary gradients and seamless interfaces

$\bullet$ Soft and flexible materials

$\bullet$ Stabilized, resilient absorbents with few ineffective members to prevent collapse in use
