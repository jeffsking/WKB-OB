#   Dry Max core by P&G

SAP is deposited from reservoirs in a printing roll onto a substrate on a grid. Thermoplastic adhesive is used to form a fibrous network applied to the SAP to immobilize it both in the dry and in the wet state.

The diaper utilizes a combination of a chemically bonded ADL and curled cellulose fibers as acquisition and distribution system.

#   Honey comb core by Fameccanica

![C1212b128IN4c141612C12c12C1214c14c14cD1820161010a1620181818181820\n](1/parseimages/imgParseSvr_img_f8efe62ab40f4466847e57636245f524_1)
The core is a layered structure comprising two laminar elements connected to one another (e.g. by hot melt glue).

Honeycomb conformation optimize the ratio between the area of the cells vs. the total area.

During absorption SAP swelling opens some labile bonds thus forming channels and creating void space for further swelling and distribution of fluid.

Application in the diaper includes an high grammage Airthrough bonded ADL.

The core comprises a carrier layer, an auxiliary layer and an absorbent material there between. Layers are bonded together ultrasonically creating permanent primary attachments and temporary secondary attachments.

During absorption SAP swelling opens some temporary secondary attachments, creating new void spaces to store additional liquid.

Application in the diaper includes a 140-150 gsm airthrough bonded ADL.
