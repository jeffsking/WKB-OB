# Soft Liners 2.0

Liners with premium softness, but without performance tradeoffs

## Consumer Insight & Benefit

Liner is the material sitting against baby's skin, so is of significant interest when discussing comfort and similar higher-order benefits. Softer liners can provide premiumization and differentiation between tiers.

Key Claims

### Feature / Technical Solution Description

Many vendors make many soft liners, but the levers used to achieve softness are often at direct odds with the levers that drive absorbent characteristics like rewet and intake.

Smaller fibers feel softer, but create capillarity that traps residual fluid that can be expressed as rewet under pressure. Fiber size is a significant part of how many BCW create softness.

There are many hypothesized solutions that have not yet been tested on KCNA diapers:

$\bullet$ Dual layer BCW: top layer is small fiber for softness, while bottom layer is larger denier for performance

$\bullet$ Top layer may also be less wettable to trap less residual fluid and create a one-way valve action

$\bullet$ PET or ES fibers: create loft (good for cushiony softness) while decreasing capillarity

* This also encompasses high loft and similar spunmelts

# Plastic-Free AC4

Textor topical surge made without traditional plastics

### Consumer Insight & Benefit

Bodyside layers are often made of polymer resins like PE, PP, or PET. While some biostock-source versions of these are available, for the most part these resins use petroleum to produce which is less environmentally friendly. Additionally, they take a long time to degrade after use and may release microplastics. Plastic-free materials may allow more environmentally-friendly positioning. Focusing on the intake layer touching the skin may strengthen the potential claims.

Key Claims

### Feature / Technical Solution Description

Textor topical surge that is used today in T5 POME and T6 might be able to be made partially or entirely plastic-free.

Textor materials are made of 2 layers: a spunbond liner on the bottom and a hydroentangled web on the top that provides texture. Either or both layers could be made plastic-free.

PLA is a biodegradable and bio-based plastic derived from renewable resources like corn starch or sugarcane. It's readily available to use in Textor materials. However, PLA may not meet the strictest definitions of plastic-free.

Lyocell is another readily-available material that is bio-based and made from renewable resources like wood pulp or eucalyptus, but is not a polymer resin in the traditional sense.
