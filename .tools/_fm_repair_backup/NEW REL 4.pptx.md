Theory & Mechanism · Capillary Physics of Void Volume in Nonwoven REL

#   Capillary Pressure (Lucas-Washburn)

$$\Delta P=2\gamma\cos\theta/ r$$

γ = surface tension of urine (~72 mN/m)

θ = contact angle (fiber wettability; target <30° with hydrophilic finish)

r = effective capillary radius (controlled by fiber denier & packing)

Darcy's Law — Bulk Flow Rate

$$Q=(k\cdot A\cdot\Delta P)/(\mu\cdot L)$$

k = permeability (darcy) — dominated by void volume & fiber architecture

$\mu$ = fluid viscosity | L = flow path length

Target permeability: 8,000–15,000 darcy for REL

Compression Recovery — Spring-Back Theory

$$\eta=(h\_ recovered/ h\_original)\times 100\%$$

3D spiral crimp in PTT/PET fiber stores elastic energy under load.

On load release, coiled fiber segments rebound — restoring void channels.

PVA scaffold prevents full Z-collapse; hollow fiber lumen holds open.

Target $\eta > 85\%$ after 4 repeated compressions (simulating 4 voids).

MD Distribution · How Fiber Architecture Drives Machine-Direction Liquid Spread

#   Why MD Distribution Matters

In a 100% SAP core with no fluff pulp, there is no natural MD wicking path. Without engineered MD distribution in the REL, all urine concentrates at the intake zone — causing local SAP saturation and leakage before the rest of the core is used.

#   Three-Mechanism MD Drive

#   1 Coarse fiber macropore wicking

Hollow 6D fiber creates the widest capillary channels (~40–60 µm diameter). Darcy flow velocity ∝ r$^{2}$ — large pores carry liquid FAST in MD before SAP absorbs it.

#   2 Bimodal denier pore gradient

Fine ES (2.2D) creates micropores that retain liquid; coarse hollow (6D) creates macropores that distribute. Net effect: liquid moves MD via macropores, then SAP absorbs via micropores.

#   3. Longer fiber MD orientation

51mm PTT/PET and hollow fibers preferentially align in MD during air-lay web formation. MD-oriented fiber segments create continuous liquid pathways running front-to-back across the core.

#   PVA hydrophilicity contribution:

PVA's inherent hydrophilicity (contact angle <20") makes every PVA fiber a wicking bridge — at 8% loading, they create a dispersed network of high-wettability channels running through the hydrophobic ES/PET matrix.

#   Summary & Key Takeaways

#   Void volume is everything

In a 100% SAP core, void volume maintenance across 4 voids is the single metric that determines leak-free performance. REL is the only engineering lever available.

#   Denier encodes function

1.5D PVA = scaffold dispersion · 2.2D ES = dense bond network · 4.0D PTT = elastic struts · 6.0D Hollow = MD wicking channels. Wrong denier = wrong function.

#   ATB oven = the manufacturing critical point

148-155°C, 2.5-3.2 m/s, 8-12% overfeed. These three numbers determine whether the lab design survives to production. Lock them in trial Stage 3.

#   Four fibers, four functions

ES bonds (50%) · PTT/PET springs back (28%) · Hollow wicks MD (14%) · PVA resists Z-collapse (8%) — each is irreplaceable in the system.

#   Bimodal length is deliberate

38mm (ES+PVA) builds the XY bond plane. 51mm (PTT+Hollow) builds Z-arches and MD orientation. Together they create a 3D resilient architecture.

#   Full cost-to-produce is transparent

100% ES: RMB 11,700/ton (RM RMB7,500 + process RMB4,200). Four-fiber REL: RMB 16,246/ton (RM RMB10,246 + process RMB6,000). Delta: +RMB 4,546/ton = +RMB 0.0032 per diaper. Justified by performance.

Recommended blend: 50% ES 2.2D/38mm · 28% PTT/PET 4D/51mm · 14% Hollow 6D/51mm · 8% PVA 1.5D/38mm
